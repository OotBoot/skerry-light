# m13 · The Fall of Wrackhaven (Act IV, Winter 1891): sound notes

The mission: the night the sea came up Fish Street in Wrackhaven and did not go back. Stage one, **Fish Street**: from the quay, knock up the houses round Fish Street and the Square and lead their people (five, and one more for each player, up to eight) to the town hall in a line behind you, wading, while the flood comes up the street after you and the drowned rise out of it. Cutscene: **the doors shut** on the flooded Square. Stage two, **The Moot Hall**: board the hall's six windows; cutscene **the Keelback** coming up Fish Street through the water and stoving in the great door; put it down, then cover everyone up the stair. Cutscenes: **the water came up the stair after them** (card), and **the leads** opened up. Stage three, **The Belfry**: hold the Council Chamber, the Belfry and the town hall's roof until first light, the drowned up the walls, over the parapet and up the stair out of the drowned hall; cutscene **first light**, a Diver climbing onto the leads; ring the old bell three times (each peal drives the drowned off the roof) for the Light's boats. Outro: the boats come in across the Square, the drowned walk the flooded streets, and from out past the quay the Light across the harbour, still lit.

## Character voice notes

- **The Mayor of Wrackhaven**: in his sixties, portly, chain of office over his nightshirt and greatcoat. A town worthy used to speeches, now giving orders in a flood; a warm West Country burr, breathless, his voice cracking with each one lost. Inside the town hall, give him the hall's echo.
- **The chandler** (new): forties, a shopkeeper's quick, anxious politeness. Wrackhaven accent.
- **A fishwife** (new): fifties, sharp-tongued, strong, more cross than afraid; broad local accent.
- **The smokehouse hand** (new): a lad of about twenty, teeth chattering with cold.
- **The cooper** (new): a big gruff man in his forties, embarrassed to be rescued.
- **Old Pascoe**: the old fisherman who drank at the Drowned Man for forty years. Seventies, hoarse and gravelly, a dry dark humour; he has seen the Keelback before and knows it. Broad old coastal dialect.
- **The schoolmaster**: forties, thin and precise, an educated voice (not local); anxious, observant, the one who notices things (the grey sky, the drowned standing in rows).
- **The chaplain** (new): fifties, gentle and wry, a measured pulpit voice with real faith in it.
- **The smith** (new): fifties, a deep slow bass, few words.
- **The Keeper's Log**: narration, as in m09: slow and flat, read back years later by lamplight.
