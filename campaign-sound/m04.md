# m04 · The Night of the Gannet (Act II, 14 December 1887): sound notes

The mission: Holm and Maren in the lantern room and on the gallery of Skerry Light, a full gale and sleet, the brig Gannet coming in under the cliffs. Stage one, **The First Hour**: the lens taken off its clockwork and the beam turned by hand at the turning bar to keep it on the Gannet as she threads the skerries. A second player rings the gallery bell to make her shorten sail, and others wipe the sleet off the weather panes. Cutscene **The Lamp Fails**: it simply goes out. Stage two, **The Second Hour**: oil from the Oil Room, a new wick from the Service Room, the clockwork wound, the lamp relit, while Maren takes the skiff out with her lantern (cutscene **Maren**). Cutscene **Too Late**: the beam finds the Gannet, Maren's lantern beside her, and she strikes. Stage three, **The Harbour Steps** (the next morning): the fog bell rung for her, and the empty skiff drifting in to the steps with her lantern still lit. Outro: the log's words, and the sea freezing to the horizon, the first of the six seas.

## Character voice notes

- **Holm** (existing; the principal keeper, Maren's husband): eight years at the Light, in his forties; steady and dry most of the night, a man of few words. Scottish east-coast. He breaks twice: the lamp going out ("No. No, no, no.") and Maren's name at the end. In stage three he is hollow and very quiet.
- **Maren** (existing; his wife, heard alive here): about thirty-five, bright, quick, fearless, a boatwoman's confidence and a little teasing with Holm. Same accent as Holm, softer. Shouted against the gale at the gallery; on the water, a raw shout that the wind carries away.
- **A voice off the sea** (new speaker): one of the Gannet's crew, a cry across the water as she strikes; a young man, terrified, heavily distant, wind-torn, almost lost under the storm.
- **The Keeper's Log** (existing narrator): read in Holm's voice the next morning, flat and exhausted, as he writes; the last sentence, "We have no mountains.", almost bewildered.
