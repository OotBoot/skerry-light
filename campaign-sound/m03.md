# m03 · Lighting the Light (Act II, 1841): sound notes

The mission: the Light's tower stands finished but for its eye, in scaffolding on its rock. Stage one, **The Landing**: the lens comes ashore in crates off a lugger at the jetty, is carried up the pier between seas and wound up the tower by a crab winch. Cutscene **The Haar**: the fog rolls in off Lantern Rock and the last sea has carried the lens's heart crate onto the north rocks. Stage two, **The Haar**: braziers lit out across the rock to the north rocks, crabs out of the sea, the heart carried back and wound up. Cutscene **Up She Goes**. Stage three, **The Lantern**: the lens's six panels seated in their frame, oil fetched from the Service Room below, the lamp lit. Outro: the beam sweeps out, and far off the fire on Lantern Rock goes out.

## Character voice notes

- **The Lampwright** (new speaker): a craftsman in his fifties, the man who ground every prism of the lens by hand. Precise, gentle, proud; speaks of the lens as "she" and as something alive. Educated working-man's English, faintly Northern (a Tyneside glassworks man). Quick and sharp when the work is at risk, soft and almost hushed when it goes right.
- **The Foreman**: the builders' foreman, forties, a big voice used to carrying over wind and surf. Plain and practical; calls the Lampwright "sir". West Country. Shouted over weather in stage one; loyal and a little in awe at the end.
- **The Last Fire-Keeper**: the last of the family who kept the fire on Lantern Rock for six hundred years (the man the stories later call the Fog-Keeper, still a living man here). Old, slow, bitter and proud; a deep, worn voice, broad old coastal dialect. Heard across water: a little distance and wind on him. His last line, "Then I will keep it cold. Someone must.", is quiet and final, almost to himself.
