# Skerry Light

A first-person night-sailing horror game for the browser. You have the helm of a small brig coming in under the cliffs at Skerry Light. The water is full of rocks, old wrecks and drifting timber, and you only see them when the lighthouse beam sweeps over them. As soon as it moves on they start to fade, and 3 seconds later they're gone.

## The story

In the winter of 1887 the lamp at Skerry Light failed. The keeper's wife, **Maren Holm**, rowed out with a lantern to warn the brig *Gannet*. The *Gannet* broke up on the skerries with all hands, and Maren's skiff came back empty.

She still stands on the water when the lamp turns, a little nearer your bow every time. If she reaches you, she comes aboard.

The only thing she will not go near is **Plimsoll**, the shark who walked up out of the surf that same winter wearing the *Gannet* cabin boy's blue shoes. He knows the deep water through the skerries and walks ahead of ships to the harbour. Each harbour you reach unlocks another page of the keeper's log.

The log goes on through 1888. The sea stops being one sea: some nights it freezes, some nights the hills burn, some nights the water is warm and green, or full of fog and gravestones, or the rocks are glass. Stranger things than Maren start turning up on the water, and a small yellow thing called **Verity** sits on decks offering to help. Now and then a ship sails through a ring of black stone into a sea of fire, or onto the wine-dark sea of an older story, or into a place at dusk where doors stand in the water. And one night an iron ship with no sails comes into the harbour, from a year the keeper will not write down. Those far seas each have their own page.

- **Maren** comes one stage nearer every 6 seconds or so on the first voyage (a little longer each voyage, up to 11): 400 m, 260, 160, 90, then right at the bow, and one more step after that she takes the ship. Between turns she slowly follows your ship, so sailing hard can buy you some distance. You hear her heartbeat and see red at the edges as she closes in. A second and a half before each step (unless Plimsoll or a crewmate is keeping her off) she gives it away: a low swell from her side, your deck lantern gutters and leans towards her, and red pulses at the edges. Once she's close and out of sight you can hear her oars in the rowlocks.
- **Plimsoll**: while you keep up with him, Maren holds where she is (she doesn't fall back). His reach grows with the lead he keeps: about 120 m at a cruise, further the faster you go (20 m less in the Backrooms), so a ship that keeps up is covered at any speed. His dot on the bearing strip has a ring that fills while he's keeping her off you and shows as an outline when you're nearly close enough, and a faint blue shimmer on the water marks his reach while you're inside it. He keeps pace with your ship at any speed, keeping further ahead the faster you go, stops to wait just inside his ring, and runs to get back in front if you overtake him. He waits only about 4 seconds for a ship that falls behind, then walks on alone. He sometimes snaps up a drowned sailor he walks into.
- **The bell** drives Maren back three stages when she is anywhere ahead of you (or anywhere at all once she is close), sinks the drowned within 150 m, and freezes Knock for 20 seconds within 450 m. It swings again after 15 seconds plus one more for each voyage number (16 s on voyage 1).
- **The drowned** crew of the Gannet swim after you at about 5 m/s, faster on later voyages. Several are out at once, more each voyage. Their eyes always catch the light. One that reaches a ship going under 9 knots climbs aboard and costs a plank; faster ships shake them off. Plimsoll sometimes snaps up one he walks into.
- **Knock**, the Gannet's walking figurehead, moves only while you can't see him. Listen for three knocks on wood (you can hear him from about 650 m, quicker and louder as he nears), turn to face him, and he stops: within 150 m facing him is enough even in the dark, and further off any light on him will do. He is a little faster than your ship, so running only buys time. If he reaches the hull his face lunges at you and he strikes for a plank, then he's thrown far back and waits 30 seconds; if that blow sinks her, the voyage ends. The bell freezes him for 20 seconds within 450 m. One Knock at a time at first, up to two on later voyages.
- **The lighthouse** sweeps round about every 6.5 seconds on the first voyage, a little slower each voyage (up to 9 seconds). Maren keeps her own pace, so the faster beam just shows you more.
- **Flares** light everything within 350 m for about seven seconds, for your whole crew, then take 35 seconds to reload.
- **The bearing strip** at the top of the screen shows the compass, the harbour (or the Backrooms' exit), Plimsoll and his ring, floating crates within 600 m and your crewmates. On a phone it's a slimmer strip under the HUD with just N, E, S and W; on a short landscape screen it fits in between the HUD's two columns.
- **Hull and collisions**: your hull is a capsule from bow to stern, so the whole length of the ship can touch things, not just the bow. Anything you touch pushes you clear. Hitting a rock, wreck, log, wall, the shore or a pier head-on (closing faster than 4.5 m/s at more than about 25°) costs a plank; a glancing touch only scrapes, slowing you and turning you along it. Crewmates' ships bump apart without damage. The drowned and Knock also cost planks. When the planks run out she sinks.
- **Tonight's enemies**: each voyage draws three enemies from a pool of 21 and only those appear (Maren is always out there). The log names them as you set out, and introduces each one the first time it turns up. You never get the same enemy two voyages running when sailing alone; a crew all gets the same three (two on a crew's first voyage). Until you first make harbour, you start gently: your first voyage brings just one enemy, from the gentler ones (the Dancer, the Cactus Mammoth, the Banana Dolphin, the Tyre Frog, the Bomber Goose, the Fridge Camel, the Gentleman or the Drowned: never Knock or Verity), a few seconds later than usual and with Maren a few seconds slower to start; the second brings two. A few one-time hints turn up the first time they're needed: keeping up with Plimsoll, the bell, Verity and the floating crates.
- **Call-outs**: when something needs an answer right now, a short line appears in the middle of the screen, with a ‹ or › pointing the way to turn and the button that answers it pulsing: Maren close or at the bow, Verity aboard, the Brute's grip, the Toucan's dive, the Assassin's shing, the Coconut Bull's trumpet, Knock moving within 300 m, the Stowaway's charge, and the Open Sky's petrels and squalls. With the sound off, warnings you'd otherwise only hear (the knocks, the shing, the trumpet, the Stowaway's scream and Maren's swell) are always written out this way.
- **Close shaves**: slip past a rock at more than 15 knots with less than 2.5 m to spare and she creaks, throws up spray and lurches, and it counts as a close shave (the same goes for storm clouds at your height in the Open Sky). They're on your end card and the crew scoreboard.
- **When you go down**, the end card says what got you, gives one tip against it, and shows a row of dots for how near Maren stood after each of her last dozen moves (ringed blue where Plimsoll or a crewmate held her, gold where the bell drove her back). In a crew the same line appears under the scoreboard. They turn up every half-minute or so each, give you a clear warning before they strike (rings, shadows, a blink, a shing, a trumpet), and only slowly grow more frequent on later voyages.

  **The Stowaway** haunts only the Backrooms, on every Backrooms voyage, on top of the three: a peg-legged pirate who appears down the corridor ahead, screams, and charges straight at where you are. You hear his peg leg thumping closer. Dodge to the side once he sets off; he runs straight past.
- **Verity** sometimes appears small in a bottom corner of your deck, facing you: a round yellow smiling orb that says it wants to help. He just stands there smiling, with a ring counting down 20 seconds. The bell does nothing to him: fire a flare before then and he rolls back over the rail into the water (he comes back later). A crewmate's flare landing within 350 m of you sends him off too. He's always beatable: he only climbs aboard when your flare will be loaded within 15 seconds, and while it still needs longer than he has left (with 3 seconds to spare) his countdown waits and the ring turns grey. Leave him and he stands up into something long and thin standing in that corner, and that jumpscare ends the voyage. He first turns up 20 to 40 seconds into a voyage and comes back 30 to 55 seconds after you send him off (about twice as often in the Backrooms).
- **Six seas**: voyage 1 is always Skerry Light. After that the voyages go round six biomes in turn (so a crew always shares one): Skerry Light, the Frozen Fjord (icebergs, snowy peaks, aurora, snow), the Ember Isles (lava-veined basalt, a smoking volcano, ash and embers), the Coral Lagoon (coral reefs, palms, glowing water, fireflies), the Barrow Shore (mossy rocks topped with gravestones, dead trees, a ruined chapel, rolling fog) and the Crystal Shoals (glowing crystal rocks, crystal spires, two moons, violet aurora). Only the look changes: rocks, routes and enemies work the same. About one voyage in twenty after the first (rolled from the crew's seed, separately from the Backrooms) goes to **the Nether** instead: the voyage opens in the sea you'd have come from, with a black stone portal full of swirling purple light standing in the water ahead. It rushes at you, you sail through in a burst of purple, and come out on a sea of lava under a roof of blocky red rock studded with glowstone, with lavafalls pouring from the roof all round, stepped netherrack rocks glowing with ore and lava, and ash and embers in the air. The Nether has its own three enemies, drawn in code, and they are the only enemies there (no pool draw, no Maren, Drowned, Knock or Verity):
  - **The Cinder Kite**: a ragged, smouldering sail-like flyer that circles high and spits fireballs at where you're going. An orange ring marks where each lands: steer out of it. A flare makes it climb away.
  - **The Slag Wader**: a hunched figure of black slag with glowing cracks that rises out of the lava ahead and wades at you. If it reaches the bow it holds on (slowing you to a crawl) and then burns a plank. Ring the bell to cool it and sink it.
  - **The Furnace Moth**: a sail-sized moth with ember wings that flutters in for your lantern. Once it lands the light drops to almost nothing, and after a few seconds the deck catches. Fire a flare and it lets go to chase the flare, and burns up in it.

  Add `?nether` to force it.

- **The Wine-Dark Sea** (the Odyssey): another one voyage in twenty after the first (the same seeded roll as the Nether, so the two never overlap). A purple-black sea under a golden moon and shooting stars, olive hills with white temples lit by torches, cypresses along the cliffs, and pale marble rocks with veins and broken columns. Its three enemies are the only ones there (no pool draw, Maren, Drowned, Knock or Verity):
  - **The Sirens**: three winged singers on a rock. Their song drags your helm round towards the rock (which breaks planks). Ring the bell to drown them out for 15 seconds.
  - **Charybdis**: a whirlpool that opens on your course, dragging the ship round and in. The middle breaks a plank and spits you out. At full sail you can pull clear.
  - **The Cyclops**: a one-eyed giant wading in the shallows, hurling rocks at where you're going. A pale ring marks where each lands. A flare in his eye blinds him for 16 seconds.

  Add `?odyssey` to force it.

- **The Places In Between**: one more voyage in twenty after the first (the same seeded roll as the Nether and the Wine-Dark Sea, so the three never overlap). A still, glassy sea stuck at dusk under a low peach sun, no stars, a pale mist on the water; staircases climbing out of the sea into the clouds, doorways standing alone on the shore (some lit), street lamps along the cliffs, and smooth pale rocks with a shut door set into each, light leaking round its edges. Its three enemies are the only ones there (no pool draw, Maren, Drowned, Knock or Verity):
  - **The Doors**: four doors in a row across your course. Sail through a dark one and you're put back 110 m. The lit one carries you 110 m on. The gaps between are open water.
  - **The Echo**: a pale copy of your own ship sailing straight at you, steering a softer mirror of your helm most of a second late, so it follows your dodges. Turn early and hold it, or ring the bell and it shatters.
  - **The Sleeper**: a vast face under the water ahead of you. When the water over it brightens it's waking: ring the bell to settle it. It gives five seconds' warning. If its eyes open you lose a plank and everything slows for three and a half seconds.

  Add `?between` to force it.

- **The Open Sky**: one more voyage in twenty after the first (the same roll again, so it never overlaps the other rare seas). You aren't sailing: whatever boat you chose, you're flying a little propeller plane at night over a sea of moonlit cloud, with snowy peaks standing out of it and the lighthouse on its mountain. You can go anywhere in the air: ← → bank and turn, **↑ climbs and ↓ dives** (the Faster/Slower buttons become Climb/Dive, and the left stick works the same on a controller). Diving gathers speed and climbing costs it; you can't go below the cloud floor or above about 120 m. The rocks become towering storm clouds stacked at different heights, so you can thread between them sideways or go over or under; flying into one costs a plank. Lightning flickers every few seconds and lights every cloud for a moment; the beam, your landing light (weaker the further above or below a cloud you are), flares and the carrier's jet light them too. Floating crates hang from little balloons at all heights. To land, come down below 14 m between the runway lamps where the harbour mouth is. From the cockpit you look over the cowling and the spinning prop, with the canopy frame and an instrument panel (speed, altitude, heading, hull); in third person the chase camera follows the plane, and your crew sees you as a plane in your skin's colours. Its three enemies are the only ones there:
  - **The Thunderhead**: a ring of white light opens around you at your height; get out of it before the bolt comes down.
  - **The Petrels**: a flock of storm birds crossing your course at your altitude. Climb, dive or turn round them, or sound the horn (the bell) to scatter them; flying through them is a birdstrike.
  - **The Squall**: a warning, then a gust from one side shoves you sideways and down for three seconds. Lean into it and climb.

  Add `?sky` to force it. Each voyage opens with the sea's name sweeping across the top of the screen in its own colour, then fading (for the Nether, as you come out of the portal). Add `?biome=fjord` (or `ember`, `lagoon`, `barrows`, `shoals`, `skerry`) to the address to force one.
- **The Backrooms**: one voyage in twenty (rolled from the crew's seed, so a whole crew goes in together), the lights go out and the sea comes back with walls. It's a flooded maze of yellow rooms under buzzing tubes that go dark each time the lamp would have turned. Plimsoll knows the way to the green EXIT sign, and the bearing strip points at it. Maren, the drowned, Knock and Verity all follow you in. Add `?backrooms` to the address to force one (solo only).
- **The sea**: voyages start about 2.4 km out (further each voyage), through a wide stretch of skerries, wrecks and drifting timber.
- **Harbour**: steer between the real red and green pier lamps. Each voyage after that has more rocks, wreckage and drowned, and the beam turns slower.

## Graphics: 3D and Classic

The world is drawn in real 3D with WebGL (three.js):

- **The sea** is a moving surface that catches the beam, the moon and your lantern.
- **The lighthouse** is a solid tower, and its beam is a cone of light with a spotlight in it that lights the rocks and cliffs as it sweeps.
- **The coast and landmarks** are solid: cliffs, headland, piers and cottages, and each sea's own landscape (the Fjord's mountains, the Ember Isles' volcanoes and lava rivers, the Lagoon's palms, the Barrow Shore's chapel and dead trees, the Shoals' spires, the Nether's lavafalls, the Wine-Dark Sea's temples and cypresses, the stairs, doors and lamps of the Places In Between).
- **Skerries, bergs, reefs, wrecks and timber** are solid shapes, and still only show where some light reaches them.
- **The Backrooms** are a 3D maze: papered walls, flooded carpet, a ceiling of humming tubes, all fading into the yellow haze.
- **The Open Sky's** clouds are 3D, made of soft puffs that turn to face you, over the 3D sea and mountains.

The sky is painted on a canvas behind the 3D view, and the creatures, ships, deck and effects on a canvas over it. The 3D camera projects exactly as the painted one does, so they line up, and anything painted behind a solid rock, wreck, thick cloud or Backrooms wall is hidden by it.

Settings → Graphics switches between **3D** and **Classic** (the original painted view, lighter on old devices). If WebGL isn't available the game uses Classic by itself.

## Wreckers' Night: over the shoulder, and jumping

Press **V** (or the camera button at the top of the phone screen, **Menu → View**, or click the right stick on a controller) to switch between first person and an over-the-shoulder camera. The camera sits behind you and to the right, and you see your own wrecker from behind, with the gun arm reaching toward the crosshair. Shots go where the crosshair points: the game finds what the crosshair line lands on, then fires from your eyes at that point. If a rock or the island's edge gets in the way, the camera pulls in and then eases back out. The longarm's telescope still looks through your own eyes, and the choice is remembered. Press **Space** (the arrow beside Fire on a phone, or **A** on a controller) to jump about a metre. Jumps travel with your position to everyone else, and the fair-hit check accounts for jumps, so a well-timed jump can make a shot pass under you. The bell has moved from Space to **B** only.

## Your wrecker, in 3D

Over the shoulder with the 3D renderer, you see your own wrecker as a full 3D figure standing in the scene (`src/fpsbody.js`). Rocks hide it, and the beam and your lantern light it. It's built from your boat skin's colours:

- **Coat and clothes:** a long open coat in your hull colour, with cuffs and lapels in the rail colour, brass buttons, a stand collar, a back vent and a skirt that swings out as you run. Under it are a waistcoat, a shirt and cravat, and a sash in your sail colour.
- **Kit:** a belt and buckle, a bandolier with cartridge pouches across the back, a powder horn and a dirk.
- **Legs:** breeches, stockings and turned-down sea boots.
- **Head:** hair in a queue tied with a ribbon, and a braided tricorn with a cockade.
- **Weapon:** the same 3D model and skin as in first person, held properly. The pepperbox is out in one hand, the long guns are shouldered with your left hand on the barrel, and the cutlass is carried point-up and swung with each cut.
- **Lantern:** carried in your left hand, or hooked to your belt when you need both hands. It glows while lit.
- **Movement:** legs and arms swing as you walk and run, the body leans and follows your aim, and the gun kicks when you fire and tips up to reload. You tuck in a jump and sink a little on landing.
- **Close camera:** the figure turns see-through when the camera is squeezed up close against a rock.

With the Classic renderer the flat figure is used instead.

## Verity on the island

Verity, the small smiling yellow thing from the voyages, comes ashore in Wreckers' Night (`src/fpsverity.js`, using the main game's pictures and voice). Now and then, while you have a flare left, he sits down on the shingle a few steps in front of you, off to one side, smiling, with a ring counting down his twenty seconds. On a phone the Flare button pulses. His usual rules apply: bullets and cutlass cuts pass straight through him, the bell doesn't bother him, and you can't walk away from him, because if you look away he's in front of you again. A fresh flare anywhere on the island sends him rolling into the dark, including another player's flare. If you leave him, he stands up and you get the jumpscare (softened if Scares is set to Softened). That counts as a death, "Verity helped", but nobody's kill. Other players see him sitting in front of you.

## Playing on a phone in landscape

Both games work with the phone turned sideways. On short landscape screens the title page puts Skerry Light and Wreckers' Night side by side, the voyage HUD shrinks (smaller labels, a two-line log, smaller sail and tool buttons), and the menus, settings and crate screens get wider and shorter so they fit without scrolling where possible. In Wreckers' Night the move stick stays under your left thumb and the fire cluster under your right, and the first-round hints move clear of the buttons. Phone notches are respected on both sides.

## The Drowned Reach (a war for the islands)

A third game, on its own card below Wreckers' Night (`src/war.js` is the game, `src/warview.js` draws it). It plays like the territory games: you land on an archipelago of skerries and take it, one against sixteen bot nations (one to fifty, your choice), or together with up to nine other people, each with a temper of its own. Nothing here touches the voyages or Wreckers' Night.

Your people grow with the land you hold, up to a limit the land sets. The slider sets what share of them an order sends. Tap empty land, drowned land or an enemy's land touching yours to march on it; the force makes for the spot you tapped, then spreads out from it in every direction (leaning on past it), filling in round as it goes, until its people are spent or nothing more can be taken. An advance into empty or drowned land is fast and keeps going on its own: it draws fresh people from home as it needs them (keeping about an eighth of your cap back), and waits for your people to grow if none can be spared, so one tap fills the wilds. Attacks on other nations use only what you send. Hills, watchtowers and a ringing bell make tiles dearer to take, and a big nation is overstretched: the more land it holds, the more each tile it takes from others costs it. Tap a far shore and a boat carries the force there (a landing needs 25). Gold comes with land, harbours and whole islands, and buys harbours (gold, shore light, fire-ships), watchtowers (dear tiles, and they fire on boats), beacons (light that keeps the drowned off), flares (light and clear a spot) and fire-ships (sail from a harbour and burn everything round where they land). People are counted in thousands, as in OpenFront (a big nation runs to millions). Cities (from 150 gold, dearer each time) raise your population cap by 250K, speed your growth a little and pay gold. Factories (from 220 gold) lay railroads to every city of yours within 16 tiles and join them to each other; a city on a railroad pays double gold (marked ×2). Cities and factories need a little room between them, and the bots build them too. Take the land under an enemy's building and the building is yours, whole (fire-ships, the drowned and the tide still destroy what they take). Hold a whole great isle for a bonus, as in Risk. Choose how the bots play: Easy (slow to grow, slow to anger), Normal, Hard (quicker, richer, cleverer about whom they hit) or Brutal (they come for you). Pacts last four minutes. Bots offer them rarely and for a reason: to cover one border while they fight on another, to stand with the neighbours of a runaway leader, or to buy peace from someone much stronger. They never ally with the runaway leader, they remember oath-breakers, and they keep no more than two or three pacts. The Pacts button on the map opens the list of nations, with how each feels about you: tap one to ask for a pact (a refusal says why, and you can ask again in 45 seconds), and tap a sworn nation twice to break the pact, which every nation remembers. The first 75 seconds are a landing truce.

The Reach's own rules carry over. The Light stands on its rock in the middle. By day it is dark; at night its wide beam sweeps the map once every 51 seconds. At night a fog of war covers everything you can't see: you see only your own and your allies' land and a few tiles round it, and whatever the beam, beacons, flares and harbour lights show. In the fog other nations show as they stood at dusk, with their strength unknown, and their attacks and boats are hidden; wherever the beam passes, everything is revealed as it really is. whoever holds the rock is the Keeper and gets gold and safe shores. At night the drowned rise on any shore the light doesn't reach and take it into the dark; by day they sink back and leave the land empty. The tide turns every ninety seconds: at low water the shoals dry into land bridges between islands, at high water they drown and anyone on them loses them. Ring the bell to hold for a while. You win with six tenths of the land or when you stand alone; when your last tile goes you can watch the war on. A map word gives the same islands (for the same number of nations) every time. The map grows with the number of nations: about 155×96 tiles for 4, 200×124 for 16, 311×193 for 40, about 363×225 for sixty nations. When the war begins, the camera goes to your landing. The game runs at 1× by default; empty land is taken fast and cheap. Harbours snap to your nearest shore tile within a few tiles of the tap, and in harbour mode your free shore is lit up. Wins and records are kept in the browser.

Keys: drag or arrows pan, wheel or +/- zoom, Q/E the share, H harbour, T tower, G beacon, F flare, X fire-ship, B bell, Space pause, 1/2/3 speed, Enter land now, Tab the leaderboard on a phone, Esc menu. On a phone: drag to pan, pinch to zoom, tap to order.

### Banner crates

`src/rcrates.js`. **Banner crates** on the Drowned Reach card open the Herald's Crate: banners that paint your land on the map, which everyone you play with sees too. Same grades, odds, reel, Open all and duplicate counts as the other crates (gold in the reel is always your prize), and the same spread: 3 Deckhand, 2 Bosun, 2 Mate, 2 Captain, 1 Admiral, plus your nation's own colour, which everyone has.
- **Deckhand:** a colour or a simple pattern (Harbour Slate, Sailcloth Stripes, Kelp Check).
- **Bosun:** a richer pattern with a lit edge along your borders (Tartan of the Isles, Herring Scale).
- **Mate:** the pattern moves (Running Tide, Phosphor Bloom).
- **Captain:** it moves, and your borders glow, pulsing (Ember Coast, Northern Lights).
- **Admiral:** The Keeper's Beam: shifting gold with the Light's beam sweeping across it, sparkles, and the brightest glow.

You earn one for every 400 tiles you take (the count carries over between wars), one for each war you see through (three minutes or more), and three for a win; the first is free. Kept in this browser (`skerry-rcrates`), apart from the boat and weapon crates. Playing together, each player's banner travels with them through the lobby.

### The Drowned Reach, together

`src/warnet.js`. Under the solo setup on the title card is **Play together**: host a war, and anyone else with the page open sees it listed and can join, up to ten players. The host picks how many bot nations (none to fifty) and how they play, and starts whenever they like, alone or with everyone in the lobby. Everyone chooses a landing and taps Ready; all land together when everyone is ready or the clock runs out. Players can offer each other pacts (each offer waits half a minute for an answer) as well as the bots. The war runs at 1× and doesn't pause, and it ends for everyone when one nation holds six tenths of the land or stands alone (or no player is left). A player who leaves for more than 25 seconds hands their nation to a bot; if the host leaves, the war ends.

How it works: the host's browser runs the whole war, the same code as a game alone. Everyone starts from the same seed and settings, so the map, names and the bots' first landings are built identically everywhere and never sent. The host streams what changes through its room presence (4 KiB at most, ten times a second): a header (clock, tide, beam, phase, pact offers, the end), the nations a chunk at a time, the map's owners as run-length-coded rows (changed rows first, then a slow sweep of the rest so anything missed is put right), the buildings a stretch of the map at a time, attacks, boats and flares, and log lines marked for everyone or for one player. The others only draw what they're sent, and send their orders back in their own presence as a short numbered list, so none is applied twice. On the self-hosted server, everyone in one war hears each other at full rate, and everyone else gets only the lobby listing, not the stream.

## The scoreboard

`src/scores.js` (the card on the title screen) and `server/scores.js`. One board for all three games, each player's best:
- **Skerry Light:** most voyages in one run (and voyages made in all).
- **Wreckers' Night:** most kills in one round (and rounds won).
- **The Drowned Reach:** wars won, alone or together (and the most land taken in one war).

Your bests are also kept in your browser, and only ever go up.

- **On claude.ai** the artifact keeps the board in its own shared database: one document per player under `scores/`, readable by everyone who can open the page, written only by its own player (Contributor access or above). Names are shown as each viewer's claude.ai shows them.
- **On your own server** the board is kept in a free [Upstash](https://upstash.com) Redis database, reached over its REST API (no driver to install). To set it up:
  1. Sign up at upstash.com and create a Redis database (the free plan is plenty; pick the region nearest your Render service).
  2. On the database's page, under **REST API**, copy `UPSTASH_REDIS_REST_URL` and `UPSTASH_REDIS_REST_TOKEN`.
  3. In Render, open the service, then **Environment**, and add both as environment variables with exactly those names. Render redeploys by itself.
  4. The server's log then says `Scoreboard: kept in Upstash Redis`.

  Without them the server keeps the board in memory, and it starts over whenever the server restarts. Players on the site choose the name shown beside their scores. Anyone can send a score, so the server only takes numbers the games can plausibly produce, one score every few seconds from each address, and one entry per browser per game; it's a board for honest players, not proof against a determined cheat.

## Wreckers' Night (multiplayer FPS)

A separate game with its own card on the title screen, below Skerry Light (a banner at the top of the title screen points to it). The card has the weapon picker (the pepperbox now, four slots waiting for more weapons), Go ashore, Settings, your name, who's on the island, and how to play. You go ashore on foot on a skerry at night with a six-shot pepperbox pistol and a lantern, and shoot it out with everyone else on the island. It never touches your voyages, crates, cosmetics or crew; your equipped skin only colours your coat and lantern.

- **Light:** you only see what light reaches. The lighthouse beam sweeps the island and shows anyone it crosses (a warning tells you when you're in it). Your lantern (L) lights what's near you, and shows you to the whole island while it burns. Flares (F, two a life) light a wide circle for everyone.
- **The bell** (B or Space): everyone shows on your bearing strip for a few seconds, and you show on theirs.
- **Five weapons**, all carried (1-5, Q for the last one, the mouse wheel, RB or the d-pad on a controller, the weapon button on a phone); the one picked on the title card is in hand when you land:
  - **Pepperbox:** six quick shots, one damage (two to the head). Its barrels turn a place each shot; reloading swaps the whole barrel cluster.
  - **Blunderbuss:** one shot of nine balls in a wide spread: deadly close, harmless past fifty metres. A flintlock: the cock snaps onto the frizzen and the pan flashes before it roars. Reloading: powder from the horn, a handful of shot, two strokes of the ramrod, prime, shut the frizzen, cock.
  - **Longarm:** one heavy shot, three damage, a head shot kills; a little wander from the hip, dead true through its brass telescope (hold right mouse, LT, or Scope on a phone). Reloading: tear and pour a paper cartridge, ram it twice, return the rod, thumb on a fresh cap, cock.
  - **Harpoon gun:** a barbed harpoon on a line that flies slow enough to see (and dodge), drops a little, and kills what it hits; it stays stuck where it lands for a while. Reloading: crank the line back onto the drum, slide a new harpoon down the barrel, cock.
  - **Cutlass:** no powder; swing within arm's reach for two damage a cut, in a forehand, backhand, thrust combination with a pale trail through the air. R turns it to the light and wipes the blade.
  - **Avtomat** (6): an automatic rifle. Twenty-five rounds a magazine at about 700 a minute for as long as you hold the trigger, one damage a hit (two to the head). It climbs hard and jumps sideways with every round, sprays from the hip, and settles back only part of the way when you let go; the reload is long (3.9 s): tip it, drop the empty magazine, rock a full one in and slap it home, rack the handle. Hold right mouse, LT or Aim on a phone to look down its iron sights: the view comes 1.45× closer, the crosshair gives way to the notch and the hooded front post, every round goes where the post sits, and the climb is about a third less.
  Everyone sees what everyone else is carrying, and every shot (balls, harpoons and cuts included) is judged fairly by whoever it was aimed at.
- **Cover:** the skerries and old wrecks stop shots. Head shots do double damage. Four hits and you're down; you're back ashore 3.5 seconds later, somewhere away from everyone else, and patch yourself up after a few quiet seconds.
- **The drowned crew** wade ashore now and then and go for whoever is nearest.
- **Rounds** last five minutes on the clock, so everyone's round ends together; the scores reset and the island moves to the next of the six seas.
- **Wreckers (bots)** fill the island up to four people, and step aside as players come ashore. They and the drowned crew are run by one player (whoever has the lowest id on the island) and shown the same to everyone; if that player leaves, the next one carries them on where they were.
- **Fair hits:** a shot travels as an event (where it was fired from and which way). Whoever it was aimed at checks it against where they really were over the last third of a second, rocks included, so ducking into cover in time always counts. The host judges shots at the wreckers and the drowned the same way.
- **Controls:** mouse and keyboard (click to aim, WASD, Shift to run, click to fire, R reload, Tab scores, Esc menu), a controller (sticks, RT fire, X reload, Y lantern, B flare, LB bell), or touch: put your left thumb down anywhere on the left to walk (a stick appears under it; push to the edge to run), drag on the right to aim and tap there to fire, or hold the big Fire button and drag it to aim and shoot with one thumb. Aiming eases onto anyone you can see near the crosshair. The phone layout keeps the screen clear: menu and scores top left, a small compass and clock, your score and the last two lines of the feed top right, and the lantern, flare, bell and reload buttons in an arc round Fire, which shows your ammo.

It uses the same connection as crews (the claude.ai room, or the crew server), on its own key in presence, so the two never mix; the server relays island players to each other at full rate.

### The islands

Each round the island is one of six kinds, as well as being in one of the six seas. Everything on it is laid out from the round number (`src/fpsisle.js`), so every player builds the same island and nothing extra is sent over the network.

- **The Shingle Skerry:** grey shingle and tide pools, boats drawn up and turned over, lobster pots, fish drying on a rack, driftwood, cairns, a half-fallen sheepfold, and a bonfire still glowing. Gulls overhead.
- **Wreckers' Reef:** dark wet rock and weed, five broken hulks, timbers, spilled crates and barrels, old cannon, an anchor, a fallen mast, a salvage fire and a lantern on a spar.
- **Chapel Islet:** turf, a roofless chapel with a tall gable and candles on the altar, a graveyard inside a low broken wall, headstones and a cross, a well with a lantern, a ring of standing stones, dead trees. Bats round the lights.
- **Smugglers' Cove:** sand, a jetty out into the sea with a boat moored at the end, a boathouse open to the water, stacks of crates and barrels, lantern posts, nets drying.
- **Signal Head:** heather, crags along one shore with a stair cut up them, a ruined round signal tower with its beacon lit, drystone field walls with gaps, standing stones, wind-bent thorns, a sheep fence.
- **The Salt Marsh:** mud, pools and reed beds, boardwalks, a fowler's hut, eel traps, peat stacks, fences and posts, a punt, and pale marsh lights over the water. Thick fog.

Walls, crates, barrels, boats, headstones, stones and the rest are real cover: they block you, stop shots and hide anyone behind them, like the rocks. Low walls stop a shot at the body but not one at the head. Fences stop you but not shots, and a roof stops shots but not you. Lanterns, fires and marsh lights light the ground round them, and anyone standing near one. After laying an island out, the game checks that you can walk to every part of it from the middle, and thins the props out if less than 60% of it is walkable; respawns and the wreckers only go where you can walk. Wreckers turn aside to get round things in their way.

In 3D the ground has gentle rises and hollows (a quarter of a metre at most), patches of colour, worn tracks with footprints, a wet band and foam at the shore, grass, reeds, stones and seaweed, and each kind of island has its own sky tint and fog. The props are merged into a few meshes, so the island costs only a dozen or so draw calls. The Classic renderer draws each prop as a flat-shaded block, with the pools, boardwalks, lights and birds.

### Weapon crates

Wreckers' Night has its own crates, the **Gunsmith's Crate**, opened from **Weapon crates** on its title card. They hold skins for the six weapons (pepperbox, blunderbuss, longarm, harpoon gun, cutlass and avtomat): ten each, 3 Deckhand, 2 Bosun, 2 Mate, 2 Captain and 1 Admiral, plus a plain finish everyone owns. They use the same grades, odds, reel, Open all and duplicate counts as the Skerry Crate, and gold in the reel is always your prize. Captain and Admiral finishes carry a glowing accent. You earn one crate for every 5 kills (the count carries over between rounds and visits) and one for each round you see out on the island after at least a minute ashore; your first one is free. Weapon crates are entirely separate from the boat crates: their own save in the browser (`skerry-wcrates`), their own count, and their own save code (`WCRATES-...`) on the card, so nothing here touches your boats, voyages or crew. `src/wcrates.js` gives the gun renderer `wcEquippedSkin(weaponId)` (colours: metal, dark, wood, brass, accent, glow), and fps.js calls `wcEarn('kill' | 'round')`.

## Boats

Choose your boat with the Brig / Cutter / Barque / Carrier picker on the title, harbour, end and crew screens, or from the shipyard at the top of the **Crates** screen. Your choice is kept on this device, and your crew sees which boat you're sailing.

| Boat | Hull | Acceleration | Steering | |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Brig | 4 | 1.3× | 1.2× | the all-rounder, two masts, a carpenter who patches a plank every 40 seconds, and floating crates reload her flare and bell |
| Cutter | 2 | 1.7× | 1.4× | small and quick, one tall mast |
| Barque | 5 | 0.8× | 0.9× | long and heavy, three masts, and a bow gun |
| Carrier | 6 | 0.6× | 0.7× | a modern supercarrier, 40 m long; 8% faster flat out, floodlights, and a scout jet |

Each boat has its own deck and bow as you see them from the helm, and its own length and rig as your crew sees it.

The Barque's **bow gun** fires by itself when you're about to run onto a rock, blowing it apart before you hit. It only fires at rocks close ahead (about a second and a half of sailing, at most 45 m) and takes 3 seconds to reload after each shot; the *Bow gun* marker under your hull fills up as it reloads. In a crew, a rock it destroys is gone for everyone.

The **Carrier** is a modern nuclear supercarrier in the style of the newest carrier class: a flat flight deck with an angled landing deck to port, the island aft on the starboard side with its mast and turning radar, aircraft parked on the bow, and a row of lit hangar openings. From her deck you look out over the non-skid, the landing-area markings and the catapult tracks to a broad flat bow. She is the toughest boat (six planks) and a touch faster flat out, and her **floodlights** light the water 1.6 times as far as a lantern. Her **scout jet** (J, the *Launch jet* button, or B on a controller) is thrown off the bow by the catapult and flies a loop about 330 m out along your course and back, its searchlight lighting every rock under it; then it is ready again 25 seconds after launch (about 17 seconds after it lands). The trade: she is huge, so there is far more of her to put on a rock, she gathers way slowly and she turns like a carrier. The jet can't fly in the Backrooms.

## Crates, skins and pennants

Every voyage you play earns a **Skerry Crate** if you go down, and if you make harbour, **5** on voyage 1, then the Fibonacci sequence for each voyage in a row (8, 13, 21, 34, 55... up to 4,181 from voyage 15 on); a sinking starts the streak over at voyage 1. About a dozen floating crates (look for their lanterns, pillars of golden light, and the gold diamonds on the bearing strip) are scattered around each voyage: sail into one to haul it aboard. Make harbour and each one you're carrying is worth **two** more crates; sink and they go down with the ship. Any crate can hold any item:

- **Boat skins** for all four boats (44 Brig, 22 Cutter, 22 Barque, 16 Carrier, plus each boat's plain finish). The Carrier's run from Fleet Grey and Arctic White through Dazzle Stripes, Night Ops and Neon Deck to Stealth, Ghost Fleet, and the Admiral of the Fleet and Aurora Flagship. Thirty of them come from the far seas: fjord ice, basalt and ember, lagoon turquoise, barrow moss, crystal, temple marble, the Places In Between, and the Nether and Wine-Dark Sea monsters, from Lava-Forged and Golden Fleece (Admiral) down. Each boat keeps its own equipped skin. A skin changes your deck, rails, bowsprit and lantern as you see them, and your hull and sails as your crewmates see them; crewmates' ships are lit by their own lanterns, so you can make out their skins from about 300 m away.
- **Animated flair**: every Mate, Captain and Admiral skin moves. Mates glow, Captains have paint flowing along the hull and sails, Admirals sparkle with a sweeping gold sheen, and some have their own: Northern Lights cycles colour, the Ghost Ship and Flying Dutchman flicker with green wisps, Lightning Bolt crackles, Spice Route and Sunset Regatta throw off embers, Ice Barque glints with frost, Abyssal Black has a dark aura, Maren's Lantern burns warm, and Witch Light has orbs circling the masts. It shows on your ship in third person, on your deck in first person, on crewmates' ships, and in the crate previews.
- **Flags**: 20 pennants, which fly from a staff at the tip of your bowsprit (and from your masthead for your crew) on any boat.
- **Flare colours**: 12 of them, from Magnesium White and Green Star to Pier Lamps (red and green), Gold Rain (sparks) and Aurora (cycles through colours). Your crew sees your flares in your colour.

Your first crate is free. The Crates screen shows how many you have, with an Open button, and tabs for your boat skins, flags and flares. A reel spins and lands on your prize. With two or more crates, **Open all** opens the lot at once (same odds) and shows the haul: how many of each grade, your best pull, and every new item. Every crate uses the same five grades: **Deckhand** (sky blue) 79.9%, **Bosun** (violet) 16%, **Mate** (pink) 3.2%, **Captain** (red) 0.64% and **Admiral** (gold) 0.26%. Crates saved by older versions become Skerry Crates.

**Keeping your loot.** Your crates and cosmetics are saved in your browser. Links opened inside other apps (Discord, Snapchat, Instagram, Messages), private windows and cleared site data start with an empty save, so there's a one-tap **Back up save** button on the menus (a red dot means you have loot that isn't backed up). Where the browser has a share sheet (most phones, and some desktop browsers) it opens that so you can send the save to yourself; elsewhere it copies a **save link**. Opening that link on any phone or browser loads everything. The Crates screen also has the save as a plain code, and as a file you can download and load back. Loading a code never takes anything away; you keep every item from both. On claude.ai, your save is also kept in your claude.ai account automatically.

Gold (Admiral) items only ever appear in the reel when one is your prize, so if you see gold scroll past, it's yours. The chance of gold is still 0.26%.

Crates and loot are kept in your browser on that device.

## Sailing together

Up to eight people can sail as a crew. Each of you sails your own brig through the same skerries, and you can see each other's ships and lanterns, hulls and bells.

- On the title screen, one person picks **Start a crew** and sends the **invite link** from the crew screen; opening it shows that crew highlighted at the top of the list, ready to tap **Join**. Others can also pick a crew from the list, or use **Find me a crew** to join any crew with a free berth.
- The crew screen has a **chat** for planning between voyages, and messages also appear in the in-game log.
- The host (whoever started the crew) sets sail when everyone is in. Anyone who joins later sails straight in on the current voyage.
- **Ships within 55 m of each other keep Maren where she is**, the same way Plimsoll does.
- **The race to harbour**: the first ship into harbour on a crew voyage wins a bonus of 3 Skerry Crates for every other ship in the crew (+3 with two ships, +21 with a full crew of eight), on top of the usual harbour crates. "First" means the fastest voyage time, timed from your own launch, so a late joiner isn't penalised; a dead heat goes to the ship with fewer hits. The crew screen tells you while you wait whether you got in first, and the scoreboard marks the winner with a ★ when the voyage ends.
- **Sailing in company**: stay within 45 m of a crewmate for 10 seconds and your carpenters patch a plank.
- **A bell rung within 160 m of a crewmate** drives her back two stages on their ship too.
- **Flares** light the water for everyone.
- **Spectating**: once you sink, are taken or make harbour, you can watch a crewmate's voyage from their deck until the voyage ends, and once per voyage you can **ring a bell for them**, driving their Maren back a stage.
- **Hails**: keys 1 to 4 (the hail buttons on a phone, or LB and the D-pad on a controller) call *Here!*, *Help!*, *Follow me* or *Maren close*. Your crewmates see it over your ship and hear your horn.
- **Scoreboard**: at the end of each voyage the crew screen shows each ship's outcome, time, hits, bells, flares, planks patched, drowned fed to Plimsoll and close shaves.
- **Reconnecting**: if a connection drops, the game reconnects by itself and carries on with the same crew.
- A voyage ends when every ship has made harbour or gone down. If anyone made it, the crew goes on to the next voyage. If nobody did, the host can start again from voyage 1.

Crews connect in one of two ways:

- **On your own website**, through the crew server in `server/`. Anyone with the link can join, with no sign-in. Players type a name on the title screen so their crew knows who is who.
- **On claude.ai**, through the artifact `room` capability, for people who are signed in and can see the artifact. Visitors from a public link get the solo game there.

If neither is available (for example, the file opened straight from your computer), the game plays solo.

## Putting it on a website

### Game and crews together (recommended)

`server/server.js` serves the game and runs the crew connection from the same address, so one deployment gives you a link anyone can open and play together. It uses only Node's standard library (Node 18 or newer), so there is nothing to `npm install`.

Try it on your own computer first:

```
npm run build
npm start
# open http://localhost:8080 in two or three browser windows
```

To put it online, use any host that runs a Node web service and allows WebSockets, such as Render, Railway or Fly.io. On Render, for example:

1. Put this folder in a GitHub repository.
2. In Render, create a **Web Service** from that repository.
3. Set the **Build Command** to `npm run build` and the **Start Command** to `npm start`.
4. Deploy, then share the address Render gives you.

The server reads the port from the `PORT` environment variable, which these hosts set for you. `MAX_PLAYERS` (default 300) caps how many people can be connected at once. Nothing is stored on disk; restarting the server just empties the lobby. `/healthz` reports how many players are connected.

### Game on a static host, crews on a separate server

If you'd rather put the game on itch.io, Netlify or GitHub Pages, run the server somewhere as above and point the game at it:

1. In `src/config.js`, set `SERVER` to your server's WebSocket address, for example `'wss://skerry-light.onrender.com/ws'`.
2. Run `npm run build`.
3. Upload `dist/skerry-light.html`, renamed to `index.html`. On itch.io, zip it first and upload it as an HTML game.

You can also point any copy at a server without rebuilding by adding `?server=wss://your-server/ws` to the page address. A page served over `https` must use a `wss://` address.

### Solo only

Upload `dist/skerry-light.html` anywhere that serves static files. Everything it needs is inside that one file; the crew section on the title screen will say it is solo only.

### Working on the source

`index.html` loads the files in `src/` separately, so you can edit them and reload. Serve the folder (for example `python3 -m http.server 8000`) and open it in a browser; crews won't connect there, since that server has no `/ws`. After changing anything in `src/` or `assets/`, run `npm run build` to update `dist/`.
