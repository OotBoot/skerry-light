# Skerry Light

A first-person night-sailing horror game for the browser. You have the helm of a small brig coming in under the cliffs at Skerry Light. The water is full of rocks, old wrecks and drifting timber, and you only see them when the lighthouse beam sweeps over them. As soon as it moves on they start to fade, and 3 seconds later they're gone.

## The story

In the winter of 1887 the lamp at Skerry Light failed. The keeper's wife, **Maren Holm**, rowed out with a lantern to warn the brig *Gannet*. The *Gannet* broke up on the skerries with all hands, and Maren's skiff came back empty.

She still stands on the water when the lamp turns, a little nearer your bow every time. If she reaches you, she comes aboard.

The only thing she will not go near is **Plimsoll**, the shark who walked up out of the surf that same winter wearing the *Gannet* cabin boy's blue shoes. He knows the deep water through the skerries and walks ahead of ships to the harbour. Each harbour you reach unlocks another page of the keeper's log.

The log goes on through 1888. The sea stops being one sea: some nights it freezes, some nights the hills burn, some nights the water is warm and green, or full of fog and gravestones, or the rocks are glass. Stranger things than Maren start turning up on the water, and a small yellow thing called **Verity** sits on decks offering to help. Now and then a ship sails through a ring of black stone into a sea of fire, or onto the wine-dark sea of an older story, or into a place at dusk where doors stand in the water. And one night an iron ship with no sails comes into the harbour, from a year the keeper will not write down. Those far seas each have their own page.

**The Skerry Compendium.** Beside Patch notes on the main menu, **Lore compendium** opens the game's lore book, laid out like a wiki: the whole history of the Light in one place, tying every game into one story. The fourteen tales, in the order they happened: The Early Watches (The Original, the keeper's first years), Skerry Links (the nine holes on the headland, from about 1883), Skerry League (the crews' ball game in the Harbour Pool, from about 1885, with its two sides, Blue and Orange, and the Gale), Skerry Chess (the lamp room's two boards, from about 1886, and the three who will always sit down to a game: Hob, Mate Tamsin and the Old Keeper), the voyages of 1888 (Skerry Light), Wreckers' Night, The Gannet's Wake (14 December 1888), Keeper's Last Light (the winter after), Freebooters' Sea (1889), Fog Run (1889), Maren's End (the Dry Night of 1890), Drowned Reach (the years after) and Skerry Clash (the rival lights on the Race, with its champions, the Threefold Skerry, the triple draft, the towers and crossing in person), and then The Chronicle of the Light, which tells the whole history again from end to end, with pages for its people (Wenna, Black Jago, Sergeant Ybarra, Jory Tregear, the Receiver of Wreck, Black Nell, Ruth Carrow, the Mayor of Wrackhaven and more), places, ships and nights. Around them, 134 articles in nine categories (Chronicles, Characters, Creatures, Places, Factions, Events, Ships, Objects, and Eras, the five ages of the coast that the Chronicle's acts follow), each with an infobox, links to the rest, See also, What links here and its categories at the foot; a timeline of the Skerries from the first fire on Lantern Rock; search as you type (Enter for full results); Random article and All pages; and on the main page a featured article and a page from the keeper's log that change each day. ‹ Back (or Backspace) goes to the last page read; ✕, Esc or B on a controller closes it. Its text is in `src/lore.js` (the file's top comment shows the shape of an article) and `src/loreui.js` draws it. Where the game says nothing the gaps are filled with lore that fits, and the keeper's log (`SK.LOG`) is never contradicted. Keep secrets out of it entirely.

**The main menu on a big screen.** On a desktop or any screen at least 1100 by 620 pixels, the main menu is laid out for a mouse, keyboard and controller: the eight games in a list down the left (each with its number), the chosen game's card in full in the middle, and the scoreboard on the right (under the list when the screen is under 1440 pixels wide), with Patch notes and Lore compendium under the list. Click a game, use the up and down arrows in the list, or press 1 to 8 to choose; LB and RB do it on a controller, and Start still presses the chosen game's main button. The last game picked is remembered on this device. Phones, tablets held upright and phones on their side keep the long scrolling menu exactly as before. `src/menudesk.js` builds the list; the layout is "the desk menu" in `src/style.css`.

**The Original.** At the very top of the main menu (on the desk menu, above the list of games) a gold **Featured** banner opens *The Original*: Skerry Light as it began, a flat 2D game for one. You are the keeper in the bottom-left corner, and ships stand in from the top and the left for the harbour in the bottom right. They can't see the rocks: swing the beam (mouse, ← → or A / D, or drag a finger) to light them. Each ship has a ring that fills while your beam is on her and drains over 6 seconds in the dark; while it holds any light she remembers every rock you show her and plots a course to the harbour round them all, and when it runs dry she forgets them all. Every ship knows the shore and the lighthouse rock without being shown, and comes into the harbour mouth from above. Ships gather speed all night with no top speed. The fog bell (Space, or double tap) slows every ship for 4 seconds and swings again after 18. Every night brings one more ship and more rocks; lose three in a night and you start again from night 1. Your best night is kept on this device. A controller works too (stick to swing, A for the bell, B back). ‹ Menu or Esc goes back to the main menu, and the night waits, paused, until you return. The bell, a chime for each ship home and the crunch of a wreck follow the volume setting. The game is its own page, `src/original.html`; `src/original.js` opens it in a frame over the menu and the build inlines it.

The version number sits at the top of the main menu, so you can tell which build you're playing. Under the game banners, **Patch notes** opens a page listing every recent update, newest first, sorted into New, Balance, Changes, Fixes and Sound. Close it with the ✕, Esc, or B on a controller.

- **Maren** comes one stage nearer every 6 seconds or so on the first voyage (a little longer each voyage, up to 11): 400 m, 260, 160, 90, then right at the bow, and one more step after that she takes the ship. Between turns she slowly follows your ship, so sailing hard can buy you some distance. You hear her heartbeat and see red at the edges as she closes in. A second and a half before each step (unless Plimsoll or a crewmate is keeping her off) she gives it away: a low swell from her side, your deck lantern gutters and leans towards her, and red pulses at the edges. Once she's close and out of sight you can hear her oars in the rowlocks.
- **Plimsoll**: while you keep up with him, Maren holds where she is (she doesn't fall back). His reach grows with the lead he keeps: about 120 m at a cruise, further the faster you go (20 m less in the Backrooms), so a ship that keeps up is covered at any speed. His dot on the bearing strip has a ring that fills while he's keeping her off you and shows as an outline when you're nearly close enough, and a faint blue shimmer on the water marks his reach while you're inside it. He keeps pace with your ship at any speed, keeping further ahead the faster you go, stops to wait just inside his ring, and runs to get back in front if you overtake him. He waits only about 4 seconds for a ship that falls behind, then walks on alone. He sometimes snaps up a drowned sailor he walks into.
- **The bell** drives Maren back three stages when she is anywhere ahead of you (or anywhere at all once she is close), sinks the drowned within 150 m, and freezes Knock for 20 seconds within 450 m. It swings again after 15 seconds plus one more for each voyage number (16 s on voyage 1).
- **The drowned** crew of the Gannet swim after you at about 5 m/s, faster on later voyages. Several are out at once, more each voyage. Their eyes always catch the light. One that reaches a ship going under 9 knots climbs aboard and costs a plank; faster ships shake them off. Plimsoll sometimes snaps up one he walks into.
- **Knock**, the Gannet's walking figurehead, moves only while you can't see him. Listen for three knocks on wood (you can hear him from about 650 m, quicker and louder as he nears), turn to face him, and he stops: within 150 m facing him is enough even in the dark, and further off any light on him will do. He is a little faster than your ship, so running only buys time. If he reaches the hull his face lunges at you and he strikes for a plank, then he's thrown far back and waits 30 seconds; if that blow sinks her, the voyage ends. The bell freezes him for 20 seconds within 450 m. One Knock at a time at first, up to two on later voyages.
- **The lighthouse** sweeps round about every 6.5 seconds on the first voyage, a little slower each voyage (up to 9 seconds). Maren keeps her own pace, so the faster beam just shows you more.
- **Flares** light everything within 350 m for about seven seconds, for your whole crew, then take 35 seconds to reload.
- **The bearing strip** at the top of the screen shows the compass, the harbour (or the Backrooms' exit), Plimsoll and his ring, floating crates within 600 m and your crewmates. On a phone it's a slimmer strip under the HUD with just N, E, S and W; on a short landscape screen it fits in between the HUD's two columns. It only shows out at sea, not on the main menu.
- **Hull and collisions**: your hull is a capsule from bow to stern, so the whole length of the ship can touch things, not just the bow. Anything you touch pushes you clear. Hitting a rock, wreck, log, wall, the shore or a pier head-on (closing faster than 4.5 m/s at more than about 25°) costs a plank; a glancing touch only scrapes, slowing you and turning you along it. Crewmates' ships bump apart without damage. The drowned and Knock also cost planks. When the planks run out she sinks.
- **Tonight's enemies**: each voyage draws three enemies from a pool of 21 and only those appear (Maren is always out there). The log names them as you set out, and introduces each one the first time it turns up. You never get the same enemy two voyages running when sailing alone; a crew all gets the same three (two on a crew's first voyage). Until you first make harbour, you start gently: your first voyage brings just one enemy, from the gentler ones (the Dancer, the Cactus Mammoth, the Banana Dolphin, the Tyre Frog, the Bomber Goose, the Fridge Camel, the Gentleman or the Drowned: never Knock or Verity), a few seconds later than usual and with Maren a few seconds slower to start; the second brings two. A few one-time hints turn up the first time they're needed: keeping up with Plimsoll, the bell, Verity and the floating crates.
- **Call-outs**: when something needs an answer right now, a short line appears in the middle of the screen, with a ‹ or › pointing the way to turn and the button that answers it pulsing: Maren close or at the bow, Verity aboard, the Brute's grip, the Toucan's dive, the Assassin's shing, the Coconut Bull's trumpet, Knock moving within 300 m, the Stowaway's charge, and the Open Sky's petrels and squalls. With the sound off, warnings you'd otherwise only hear (the knocks, the shing, the trumpet, the Stowaway's scream and Maren's swell) are always written out this way.
- **Close shaves**: slip past a rock at more than 15 knots with less than 2.5 m to spare and she creaks, throws up spray and lurches, and it counts as a close shave (the same goes for storm clouds at your height in the Open Sky). They're on your end card and the crew scoreboard.
- **When you go down**, the end card says what got you, gives one tip against it, and shows a row of dots for how near Maren stood after each of her last dozen moves (ringed blue where Plimsoll or a crewmate held her, gold where the bell drove her back). In a crew the same line appears under the scoreboard. They turn up every half-minute or so each, give you a clear warning before they strike (rings, shadows, a blink, a shing, a trumpet), and only slowly grow more frequent on later voyages.

  **The Stowaway** haunts only the Backrooms, on every Backrooms voyage, on top of the three: a peg-legged pirate who appears down the corridor ahead, screams, and charges straight at where you are. You hear his peg leg thumping closer. Dodge to the side once he sets off; he runs straight past.
- **Verity** sometimes appears small in a bottom corner of your deck, facing you: a round yellow smiling orb that says it wants to help. He just stands there smiling, with a ring counting down 20 seconds. The bell does nothing to him: fire a flare before then and he rolls back over the rail into the water (he comes back later). A crewmate's flare landing within 350 m of you sends him off too. He's always beatable: he only climbs aboard when your flare will be loaded within 15 seconds, and while it still needs longer than he has left (with 3 seconds to spare) his countdown waits and the ring turns grey. Leave him and he stands up into something long and thin standing in that corner, and that jumpscare ends the voyage. He first turns up 20 to 40 seconds into a voyage and comes back 30 to 55 seconds after you send him off (about twice as often in the Backrooms).
- **Six seas**: voyage 1 is always Skerry Light. After that the voyages go round six biomes in turn (so a crew always shares one): Skerry Light, the Frozen Fjord (icebergs, snowy peaks, aurora, snow), the Ember Isles (lava-veined basalt, a smoking volcano, ash and embers), the Coral Lagoon (coral reefs, palms, glowing water, fireflies), the Barrow Shore (mossy rocks topped with gravestones, dead trees, a ruined chapel, rolling fog) and the Crystal Shoals (glowing crystal rocks, crystal spires, two moons, violet aurora). Only the look changes: rocks, routes and enemies work the same. About one voyage in twenty after the first (rolled from the crew's seed, separately from the Backrooms) goes to **the Nether** instead: the voyage opens in the sea you'd have come from, with a black stone portal full of swirling purple light standing in the water ahead. It rushes at you, you sail through in a burst of purple, and come out on a sea of lava under a roof of blocky red rock studded with glowstone, with lavafalls pouring from the roof all round, stepped netherrack rocks glowing with ore and lava, and ash and embers in the air. The Nether has its own three enemies, drawn in code, and they are the only enemies there (no pool draw, no Maren, Drowned, Knock or Verity):
  - **The Cinder Kite**: a ragged, smouldering sail-like flyer that circles high and spits fireballs at where you're going. An orange ring marks where each lands: steer out of it. A flare makes it climb away.
  - **The Slag Wader**: a hunched figure of black slag with glowing cracks that rises out of the lava ahead and wades at you. If it reaches the bow it holds on (slowing you to a crawl) and then burns a plank. Ring the bell to cool it and sink it.
  - **The Furnace Moth**: a sail-sized moth with ember wings that flutters in for your lantern. Once it lands the light drops to almost nothing, and after a few seconds the deck catches. Fire a flare and it lets go to chase the flare, and burns up in it.

  Add `?nether` to force it.

- **The Wine-Dark Sea** (the Odyssey): another one voyage in twenty after the first (the same seeded roll as the Nether, so the two never overlap). A purple-black sea under a golden moon and shooting stars, olive hills with white temples lit by torches, cypresses along the cliffs, and pale marble rocks with veins and broken columns. Its three enemies are the only ones there (no pool draw, Maren, Drowned, Knock or Verity):
  - **The Sirens**: three winged singers on a rock. Their song drags your helm round towards the rock (which breaks planks). Ring the bell to drown them out for 15 seconds.
  - **Charybdis**: a whirlpool that opens on your course, dragging the ship round and in. The middle breaks a plank and spits you out. At full sail you can pull clear.
  - **The Cyclops**: a one-eyed giant wading in the shallows, hurling rocks at where you're going. A pale ring marks where each lands. A flare in his eye blinds him for 16 seconds.

  Add `?odyssey` to force it.

- **The Places In Between**: one more voyage in twenty after the first (the same seeded roll as the Nether and the Wine-Dark Sea, so the three never overlap). A still, glassy sea stuck at dusk under a low peach sun, no stars, a pale mist on the water; staircases climbing out of the sea into the clouds, doorways standing alone on the shore (some lit), street lamps along the cliffs, and smooth pale rocks with a shut door set into each, light leaking round its edges. Its three enemies are the only ones there (no pool draw, Maren, Drowned, Knock or Verity):
  - **The Doors**: four doors in a row across your course. Sail through a dark one and you're put back 110 m. The lit one carries you 110 m on. The gaps between are open water.
  - **The Echo**: a pale copy of your own ship sailing straight at you, steering a softer mirror of your helm most of a second late, so it follows your dodges. Turn early and hold it, or ring the bell and it shatters.
  - **The Sleeper**: a vast face under the water ahead of you. When the water over it brightens it's waking: ring the bell to settle it. It gives five seconds' warning. If its eyes open you lose a plank and everything slows for three and a half seconds.

  Add `?between` to force it.

- **The Open Sky**: one more voyage in twenty after the first (the same roll again, so it never overlaps the other rare seas). You aren't sailing: whatever boat you chose, you're flying a little propeller plane at night over a sea of moonlit cloud, with snowy peaks standing out of it and the lighthouse on its mountain. You can go anywhere in the air: ← → bank and turn, **↑ climbs and ↓ dives** (the Faster/Slower buttons become Climb/Dive, and the left stick works the same on a controller). Diving gathers speed and climbing costs it; you can't go below the cloud floor or above about 120 m. The rocks become towering storm clouds stacked at different heights, so you can thread between them sideways or go over or under; flying into one costs a plank. Lightning flickers every few seconds and lights every cloud for a moment; the beam, your landing light (weaker the further above or below a cloud you are), flares and the carrier's jet light them too. Floating crates hang from little balloons at all heights. To land, come down below 14 m between the runway lamps where the harbour mouth is. From the cockpit you look over the cowling and the spinning prop, with the canopy frame and an instrument panel (speed, altitude, heading, hull); in third person the chase camera follows the plane, and your crew sees you as a plane in your skin's colours. Its three enemies are the only ones there:
  - **The Thunderhead**: a ring of white light opens around you at your height; get out of it before the bolt comes down.
  - **The Petrels**: a flock of storm birds crossing your course at your altitude. Climb, dive or turn round them, or sound the horn (the bell) to scatter them; flying through them is a birdstrike.
  - **The Squall**: a warning, then a gust from one side shoves you sideways and down for three seconds. Lean into it and climb.

  Add `?sky` to force it. Each voyage opens with the sea's name sweeping across the top of the screen in its own colour, then fading (for the Nether, as you come out of the portal). Add `?biome=fjord` (or `ember`, `lagoon`, `barrows`, `shoals`, `skerry`) to the address to force one.
- **The Backrooms**: one voyage in twenty (rolled from the crew's seed, so a whole crew goes in together), the lights go out and the sea comes back with walls. It's a flooded maze of yellow rooms under buzzing tubes that go dark each time the lamp would have turned. Plimsoll knows the way to the green EXIT sign, and the bearing strip points at it. Maren, the drowned, Knock and Verity all follow you in. Add `?backrooms` to the address to force one (solo only).
- **The sea**: voyages start about 2.4 km out (further each voyage), through a wide stretch of skerries, wrecks and drifting timber.
- **Harbour**: steer between the real red and green pier lamps. Each voyage after that has more rocks, wreckage and drowned, and the beam turns slower.

## Sound and volume

Sounds come from where they happen, in every game: a gunshot, a moan, a boss's footsteps, a splash or a bell is panned to the side it's on, and anything behind you is muffled, so you can tell ahead from behind and turn to find it. Headphones help most.

**Volume** is a slider, from off to 100%, and it's in every in-game menu as well as on the **Settings** card: Skerry Light's pause card, the first-person menu (Esc or Start) in Wreckers' Night, Keeper's Last Light, the raids, Fog Run and Freebooters' Sea, the Drowned Reach's menu and Skerry Links' menu. It's one setting, so moving any of them moves them all.

## Battery saver

Phones and tablets get hot and drain quickly when a game draws every frame at full resolution. The **Battery saver** on the **Settings** card (every mode's Settings button opens it) has three choices: **Auto** (the default: on for phones and tablets, off on computers), **On** and **Off**. With it on:

- the game draws 30 frames a second instead of 60 (or 120),
- the 3D view renders at about 1.25 pixels per screen pixel instead of 2, and the painted layers (HUD, creatures, effects) at 1.5,
- everything still moves at exactly the same speed: boats, shots, zombies and golf balls cover the same distance each second, only fewer frames are drawn.

Whatever the setting, the menus and the pause screen draw at 30 frames a second, and the game's sound sleeps whenever you switch to another app or tab.

## Look sensitivity

How quickly the view turns in Wreckers' Night and the Lighthouse, with the mouse and the touch screen, as two sliders: **across (X)** for turning left and right, and **up and down (Y)** for looking up and down. Both are on the **Settings** card, and the same two are under **Options** in the first-person menu (Esc, or the menu button on a phone), each from 0.3 to 3 times the usual, with a Default button that puts both back. Kept on this device; if you had set the old single slider, both start from what you had. A controller has its own look sensitivity on the menu's Controller page.

In first person you can look all the way up at the sky and all the way down at your feet (to just short of straight up and down).

## Graphics

The world is drawn in real 3D with WebGL (three.js):

- **The sea** is a moving surface that catches the beam, the moon and your lantern. On foot (Wreckers' Night and the Lighthouse) it's cut away under the island, along the same wobbly shore its ground is built with, it runs calmer in the island's lee, and its crests are kept a little below the ground, so the swell never shows through the ground, a floor or a dock, from the shore or from the top of the tower. The painted shimmer on the water (the moon's glitter, the Lagoon's glow, lava) is never drawn over the island either.
- **The lighthouse** is a solid tower, and its beam is a cone of light with a spotlight in it that lights the rocks and cliffs as it sweeps.
- **The coast and landmarks** are solid: cliffs, headland, piers and cottages, and each sea's own landscape (the Fjord's mountains, the Ember Isles' volcanoes and lava rivers, the Lagoon's palms, the Barrow Shore's chapel and dead trees, the Shoals' spires, the Nether's lavafalls, the Wine-Dark Sea's temples and cypresses, the stairs, doors and lamps of the Places In Between).
- **Skerries, bergs, reefs, wrecks and timber** are solid shapes, and still only show where some light reaches them.
- **The Backrooms** are a 3D maze: papered walls, flooded carpet, a ceiling of humming tubes, all fading into the yellow haze.
- **The Open Sky's** clouds are 3D, made of soft puffs that turn to face you, over the 3D sea and mountains.

The sky is painted on a canvas behind the 3D view, and the creatures, ships, deck and effects on a canvas over it. The 3D camera projects exactly as the painted one does, so they line up, and anything painted behind a solid rock, wreck, thick cloud or Backrooms wall is hidden by it.

**Indoors and out, on foot.** The weather (drizzle, snow, ash, embers, fireflies, rolling fog) is drawn over the view, so on foot it stops under a roof: wherever there's a ceiling, a roof, the floor above or a stair overhead (any solid lifted off the ground over you and the ground round you; a lintel or a beam doesn't count), and in the Lighthouse's lantern room under its dome (the gallery outside it is out in the weather). It fades out as you go in through a door and back as you come out, the haze on the horizon goes with it, a lightning flash comes in dimmer through the windows, and the sea and the wind are muffled (`fpsUnderRoof` in `src/fpsisle.js`, `audioOutdoors` in `src/audio.js`). It goes by what's overhead rather than by room, so anything built with a roof, on any island or in the station, works the same.

The 3D view is always on (the old Classic setting is gone; an old saved choice of it is simply ignored). Only a device with no WebGL at all gets the painted view instead. If frames start coming slowly, the 3D view quietly draws at a lower resolution until they're quick again, and then goes back up (but not to a resolution that's just proved too slow, for a minute, so it doesn't keep stepping down and up). In the on-foot games (Wreckers' Night, Keeper's Last Light, the raids, Fog Run and Freebooters' Sea), the parts of the scene that stay still against one another (a room's planks and fittings, a figure's buttons and buckles) are drawn together rather than one by one, which saves most of the work each frame; anything that moves, appears or disappears is drawn on its own again straight away, so nothing looks any different. If a tablet or phone drops the 3D view under strain (its graphics chip "loses the context"), the game waits for it to come back, and if it hasn't within a couple of seconds builds it afresh, so after a stutter you're straight back in the full 3D world, never the flat one.

## Shooter controls (Wreckers' Night, Keeper's Last Light, Wrackhaven and Maren's End)

Every first-person mode uses the same layout as most modern console and PC shooters, so your hands already know it.

## Wreckers' Night: over the shoulder, and jumping

Press **T** (or the camera button at the top of the phone screen, **Menu → View**, or up on the D-pad on a controller) to switch between first person and an over-the-shoulder camera. The camera sits behind you and to the right, and you see your own wrecker from behind, with the gun arm reaching toward the crosshair. Shots go where the crosshair points: the game finds what the crosshair line lands on, then fires from your eyes at that point. If a rock or the island's edge gets in the way, the camera pulls in and then eases back out. The longarm's telescope still looks through your own eyes, and the choice is remembered. Press **Space** (the arrow beside Fire on a phone, or **A** on a controller) to jump about a metre. Jumps travel with your position to everyone else, and the fair-hit check accounts for jumps, so a well-timed jump can make a shot pass under you. The bell has moved from Space to **B** only.

## Your wrecker, in 3D

Over the shoulder with the 3D renderer, you see your own wrecker as a full 3D figure standing in the scene (`src/fpsbody.js`). Rocks hide it, and the beam and your lantern light it. It's built from your boat skin's colours:

- **Coat and clothes:** a long open coat in your hull colour, with cuffs and lapels in the rail colour, brass buttons, a stand collar, a back vent and a skirt that swings out as you run. Under it are a waistcoat, a shirt and cravat, and a sash in your sail colour.
- **Kit:** a belt and buckle, a bandolier with cartridge pouches across the back, a powder horn and a dirk.
- **Legs:** breeches, stockings and turned-down sea boots.
- **Head:** hair in a queue tied with a ribbon, and a braided tricorn with a cockade.
- **Weapon:** the same 3D model and skin as in first person, held properly. The pepperbox is out in one hand, the long guns are shouldered with your left hand on the barrel, and the cutlass is carried point-up and swung with each cut.
- **Lantern:** carried in your left hand, or hooked to your belt when you need both hands. It glows while lit.
- **Movement:** legs and arms swing as you walk and run, the body leans and follows your aim, and the gun kicks when you fire and tips up to reload. You tuck in a jump and sink a little on landing.
- **Close camera:** the figure turns see-through when the camera is squeezed up close against a rock.

On a device with no WebGL the flat figure is used instead.

## Verity on the island

Verity, the small smiling yellow thing from the voyages, comes ashore in Wreckers' Night (`src/fpsverity.js`, using the main game's pictures and voice). Now and then, while you have a flare left, he sits down on the shingle a few steps in front of you, off to one side, smiling, with a ring counting down his twenty seconds. On a phone the Flare button pulses. His usual rules apply: bullets and cutlass cuts pass straight through him, the bell doesn't bother him, and you can't walk away from him, because if you look away he's in front of you again. A fresh flare anywhere on the island sends him rolling into the dark, including another player's flare. If you leave him, he stands up and you get the jumpscare (softened if Scares is set to Softened). That counts as a death, "Verity helped", but nobody's kill. Other players see him sitting in front of you.

## Playing on a phone in landscape

Both games work with the phone turned sideways. On short landscape screens the title page puts Skerry Light and Wreckers' Night side by side, the voyage HUD shrinks (smaller labels, a two-line log, smaller sail and tool buttons), and the menus, settings and crate screens get wider and shorter so they fit without scrolling where possible. In Wreckers' Night the move stick stays under your left thumb and the fire cluster under your right, and the first-round hints move clear of the buttons. Phone notches are respected on both sides.

In the first-person modes (Wreckers' Night, Keeper's Last Light and Maren's End) the round buttons at the top left of a phone come first, upright or sideways: the part's name, the clock and what's to be done start underneath them, your health, points and calling's meters sit under those words, and the feed and anything at the top middle (a room's name, a boss's strength, the bridge) move down out of the way whenever the buttons reach across to them.


On foot (Wreckers' Night and the Lighthouse), the walking stick eases in: a small push is a slow walk, the edge of the ring a walk at four-fifths of the keyboard's pace (the same pace looks much quicker on a small screen), and you only run when you push out past the ring to the dotted outer circle (it lights up). The ring follows your thumb only beyond that.
## Drowned Reach (a war for the islands)

A third game, on its own card below Wreckers' Night (`src/war.js` is the game, `src/warview.js` draws it). It plays like the territory games: you land on an archipelago of skerries and take it, one against sixteen bot nations (one to fifty, your choice), or together with up to nine other people, each with a temper of its own. Nothing here touches the voyages or Wreckers' Night.

Your people grow with the land you hold, up to a limit the land sets. The slider sets what share of them an order sends. Tap empty land, drowned land or an enemy's land touching yours to march on it; the force makes for the spot you tapped, then spreads out from it in every direction (leaning on past it), filling in round as it goes, until its people are spent or nothing more can be taken. An advance into empty or drowned land is fast and keeps going on its own: it draws fresh people from home as it needs them (keeping about an eighth of your cap back), and waits for your people to grow if none can be spared, so one tap fills the wilds. Attacks on other nations use only what you send. Hills, watchtowers and a ringing bell make tiles dearer to take, and a big nation is overstretched: the more land it holds, the more each tile it takes from others costs it. Tap a far shore and a boat carries the force there (a landing needs 25). Gold comes with land, harbours and whole islands, and buys harbours (gold, shore light, fire-ships), watchtowers (dear tiles, and they fire on boats), beacons (light that keeps the drowned off), flares (light and clear a spot) and fire-ships (sail from a harbour and burn everything round where they land). People are counted in thousands, as in OpenFront (a big nation runs to millions). Cities (from 150 gold, dearer each time) raise your population cap by 250K, speed your growth a little and pay gold. Factories (from 220 gold) lay railroads to every city of yours within 16 tiles and join them to each other; a city on a railroad pays double gold (marked ×2). Cities and factories need a little room between them, and the bots build them too. Take the land under an enemy's building and the building is yours, whole (fire-ships, the drowned and the tide still destroy what they take). Hold a whole great isle for a bonus, as in Risk. Choose how the bots play: Easy (slow to grow, slow to anger), Normal, Hard (quicker, richer, cleverer about whom they hit) or Brutal (they come for you). Pacts last four minutes. Bots offer them rarely and for a reason: to cover one border while they fight on another, to stand with the neighbours of a runaway leader, or to buy peace from someone much stronger. They never ally with the runaway leader, they remember oath-breakers, and they keep no more than two or three pacts. The Pacts button on the map opens the list of nations, with how each feels about you: tap one to ask for a pact (a refusal says why, and you can ask again in 45 seconds), and tap a sworn nation twice to break the pact, which every nation remembers. The first 75 seconds are a landing truce.

The Reach's own rules carry over. The Light stands on its rock in the middle. By day it is dark; at night its wide beam sweeps the map once every 51 seconds. At night a fog of war covers everything you can't see: you see only your own and your allies' land and a few tiles round it, and whatever the beam, beacons, flares and harbour lights show. In the fog other nations show as they stood at dusk, with their strength unknown, and their attacks and boats are hidden; wherever the beam passes, everything is revealed as it really is. whoever holds the rock is the Keeper and gets gold and safe shores. At night the drowned rise on any shore the light doesn't reach and take it into the dark; by day they sink back and leave the land empty. The tide turns every ninety seconds: at low water the shoals dry into land bridges between islands, at high water they drown and anyone on them loses them. Ring the bell to hold for a while. You win with six tenths of the land or when you stand alone; when your last tile goes you can watch the war on. A map word gives the same islands (for the same number of nations) every time. The map grows with the number of nations: about 155×96 tiles for 4, 200×124 for 16, 311×193 for 40, about 363×225 for sixty nations. When the war begins, the camera goes to your landing. The game runs at 1× by default; empty land is taken fast and cheap. Harbours snap to your nearest shore tile within a few tiles of the tap, and in harbour mode your free shore is lit up. Wins and records are kept in the browser.

Keys: drag or arrows pan, wheel or +/- zoom, Q/E the share, H harbour, T tower, G beacon, F flare, X fire-ship, B bell, Space pause, 1/2/3 speed, Enter land now, Tab the leaderboard on a phone, Esc menu. On a phone: drag to pan, pinch to zoom, tap to order.

### Banner crates

`src/rcrates.js`. **Banner crates** on the Drowned Reach card open the Herald's Crate: banners that paint your land on the map, which everyone you play with sees too. Same grades, odds, reel, Open all and duplicate counts as the other crates (gold in the reel is always your prize). Thirty-one banners, plus your nation's own colour, which everyone has; the common grades between them run through the rainbow:
- **Deckhand (10):** a colour or a simple pattern: Harbour Slate, Sailcloth Stripes, Kelp Check, Lobster Red, Marigold Stripes, Gorse Yellow, Sea Glass, Harbour Blue Stripes, Indigo Night, Heather Check.
- **Bosun (7):** a richer pattern with a lit edge along your borders: Tartan of the Isles, Herring Scale, Rose Tartan, Saffron Scale, Lemon Lattice, Fern Scale, Violet Tartan.
- **Mate (6):** the pattern moves: Running Tide, Crimson Tide, Sunrise Tide, Phosphor Bloom, Spring Bloom, Amethyst Bloom.
- **Captain (5):** it moves, and your borders glow, pulsing: Ember Coast, Northern Lights, Kraken Ink, Frost Fire, Rainbow Squall.
- **Admiral (3):** the best, and alive with light: The Keeper's Beam (rippling gold with the Light's beam sweeping across it, white-hot at its heart, a fainter beam crossing it, and motes glinting), The Drowned Crown (caustic light through the deep, rings of water-light rising and spreading, crowns glowing under the water, and bubbles drifting up), Prism of the Light (cut-glass facets flashing through the spectrum, the white beam sweeping through with a rainbow fanned out either side); their brightest light blooms softly over the map, glints of light twinkle over the land, and their borders have the brightest glow, with light running along them. Their crate cards move too.

The grade's odds are the same however many banners it holds: more banners in a grade only means more to choose from within it.

You earn one for every 400 tiles you take (the count carries over between wars), one for each war you see through (three minutes or more), and three for a win; the first is free. Kept in this browser (`skerry-rcrates`), apart from the boat and weapon crates. Playing together, each player's banner travels with them through the lobby. The bot nations fly banners too: a little under half of them, rolled at the crate's own odds, and in every war at least one flies an Admiral's (chosen from the map word's seed, so everyone in a war played together sees the same ones).

### Drowned Reach, together

`src/warnet.js`. Under the solo setup on the title card is **Play together**: host a war, and anyone else with the page open sees it listed and can join, up to ten players. The host picks how many bot nations (none to fifty) and how they play, and starts whenever they like, alone or with everyone in the lobby. Everyone chooses a landing and taps Ready; all land together when everyone is ready or the clock runs out. Players can offer each other pacts (each offer waits half a minute for an answer) as well as the bots. The war runs at 1× and doesn't pause, and it ends for everyone when one nation holds six tenths of the land or stands alone (or no player is left). A player who leaves for more than 25 seconds hands their nation to a bot; if the host leaves, the war ends. A player who drops out can rejoin and take their nation back (see Rejoining a game in progress).

How it works: the host's browser runs the whole war, the same code as a game alone. Everyone starts from the same seed and settings, so the map, names and the bots' first landings are built identically everywhere and never sent. The host streams what changes through its room presence (4 KiB at most, ten times a second): a header (clock, tide, beam, phase, pact offers, the end), the nations a chunk at a time, the map's owners as run-length-coded rows (changed rows first, then a slow sweep of the rest so anything missed is put right), the buildings a stretch of the map at a time, attacks, boats and flares, and log lines marked for everyone or for one player. The others only draw what they're sent, and send their orders back in their own presence as a short numbered list, so none is applied twice. On the self-hosted server, everyone in one war hears each other at full rate, and everyone else gets only the lobby listing, not the stream.

## The scoreboard

`src/scores.js` (the card on the title screen) and `server/scores.js`. The **Minimise** button at the top of the card shrinks it to a small bar with just its name; **Show** opens it again, and the choice is kept on this device. Every game you play goes on the board as an entry of its own, by itself, so you can be on a board many times:
- **Skerry Light:** each run: the voyages made, and its close shaves.
- **Wreckers' Night:** each round: your kills, and whether you won it.
- **Drowned Reach:** each war: won or not, and the land taken.
- **Keeper's Last Light:** each night: the rounds held, and the drowned sent back.
- **Maren's End:** each raid: the time through, the damage done to its bosses, and the drowned sent back.
- **Fog Run:** each run: the furthest island reached, the time through if won, and the islands lit.
- **Freebooters' Sea:** each session: the sovereigns banked, the voyages done, and the sovereigns banked in all.
- **Skerry Links:** each full round of nine: the score (and to par), the holes played and the birdies; the lowest round ranks first.
- **Skerry Clash:** not games but keepers: one row for each username, with its rating (MMR), its wins and the matches rated, the highest rating first (see **Rating (MMR)** under Skerry Clash).

It sits at the top of the title screen: the top ten of every game at once, each with an **All** button (once there are more than ten) to see every entry, and your own marked "(you)" (your best game is shown under a board where you're not in what it shows). A game is logged as it goes, and once more when it ends.

**Incomplete games count too.** Leave a game or lose it before its end (or close the page) and it's still logged, as far as it got, with a yellow **\*** by the name; a line under the board says the asterisk marks an incomplete game. A game's end: the boat lost, or coming ashore from harbour (Skerry Light; leaving from the pause menu at sea is incomplete), the round ending (Wreckers' Night), a war won or lost (Drowned Reach), the Lighthouse falling (Keeper's Last Light), getting through the door (Maren's End; going down before it is incomplete), and leaving (Freebooters' Sea, which has no other end).

The boards from before kept only each player's best; those are still shown, each as one game, so nobody's old scores went. The server keeps the best 4000 games of each mode, and shows at most 25 of one player's. Your bests are still kept in your browser and your save (cloud saves, below).

**Your name** is one username for every game: type it in the board's box or any of the other name boxes (sailing together, Wreckers' Night, the Lighthouse) and the rest fill in. It's kept on the device (and in your save, so a new device that loads your recovery code gets it too), and it's your name on the board; with none chosen, you're "A sailor". The Drowned Reach's house name is kept on the device as well. On the game's own site your games are marked as yours by an id made from your recovery code (never the code itself), so they're "(you)" on every device that has it.

- **On claude.ai** the artifact keeps the board in its own shared database: one document per player under `scores/`, readable by everyone who can open the page, written only by its own player (Contributor access or above). Names are shown as each viewer's claude.ai shows them.
- **On your own server** the board is kept in a free [Upstash](https://upstash.com) Redis database, reached over its REST API (no driver to install). To set it up:
  1. Sign up at upstash.com and create a Redis database (the free plan is plenty; pick the region nearest your Render service).
  2. On the database's page, under **REST API**, copy `UPSTASH_REDIS_REST_URL` and `UPSTASH_REDIS_REST_TOKEN`.
  3. In Render, open the service, then **Environment**, and add both as environment variables with exactly those names. Render redeploys by itself.
  4. The server's log then says `Scoreboard: kept in Upstash Redis`.

  The server sends the page compressed (Brotli, or gzip for browsers without it) and with an ETag, so a browser that already has the current version gets a 304 and downloads nothing; the server sends dist/web.html, which is the page without its sound inlined (about 1.7 MB compressed instead of 15). The recordings come as separate files once the page is up, and the browser keeps them for a year, so a new version only re-downloads the page itself. The game plays its synthesised sounds until each recording arrives. `npm run build` makes the compressed copies (dist/web.html.br and .gz, not kept in git), so a waking server only reads files and is ready in about two seconds. dist/skerry-light.html stays all-in-one, so you can open it straight from disk. On Render's free plan outbound bandwidth (5 GB a month) is the limit that runs out first.

  Without them the server keeps the board in memory, and it starts over whenever the server restarts. Players on the site choose the name shown beside their scores. Anyone can send a score, so the server only takes numbers the games can plausibly produce, one score every few seconds from each address, and one entry per browser per game; it's a board for honest players, not proof against a determined cheat.

## Keeper's Last Light (rounds against the drowned; called the Lighthouse in the code and below)

A fourth game, on its own card on the title screen (**Hold the Light**). First person: the keeper's station at the Light, shut up for the night, and the drowned coming up out of the sea for it; you land on its jetty and fight your way up through the courtyard and into it, round after round, in the manner of the old wave-survival "zombies" games. It uses Wreckers' Night's engine, weapons and weapon skins, and your outfit; its crates are the same weapon and outfit crates (a kill here counts a third of one there, and every fifth round held earns one of each).

- **Rounds.** The drowned haul themselves up the jetty's ladders out of the harbour, wade up the shingle to the gaps boarded up in the courtyard's sea wall, come across the yards and up the beach, and tear the boards off the windows to climb in: 6 in the first round, 8, 13, 18, 24, then more every round, and tougher every round. A drowned man's strength, in pepperbox body shots (before the damage scaling below), is 2 + 0.75 × (round − 1) up to round ten, gentle so the wall weapons last through the early rounds while you save for the Lampwright's Bench, then 8.75 × 1.14^(round − 10), 14% more every round, compounding (each one a tenth either way): round 1, 2 (two pepperbox shots, or one through the head); round 5, 5; round 10, 8.75; round 15, 17; round 20, 32; round 25, 62; round 30, 120; round 40, 444. Past the twenties only a reforged weapon (and reforged again) keeps up.. The Fresnel Lamp's burn and its sparks are fixed fractions of it. Shamblers at first; joggers from round three; runners from round six. Up to 24 at once. Clear a round and there's a fifteen-second breather before the next round's banner (the last of them down, then the quiet). They don't come on in a file: each has its own gait, a weave from side to side (wide in the open, narrow in a doorway or on the stair, none once it's on you), a pace that comes and goes, a stumble or a stop to sway now and then (not close to you), its own lane a little to one side of the shortest way, and, on your floor and in the open, its own side to come at you from (a point out round you, drawn in as it closes, if it can walk straight there). They keep further apart, too (a softer give out to arm's length). They reach for you with their arms out in front, elbows bent, and when one's on you it rears back with one arm up over its head, lunges in and clubs it down across you (the knock lands as the arm comes down), then staggers back; together, everyone sees the same swing. One squeezed off the walking grid (hard against the newel, say) finds its way back onto it, so they come up and down the tower's stair as they should; one that's got no nearer you by the walk for half a minute, more than eight metres off, goes back to sea and comes again.
- **Windows are theirs.** You can't go out through a window, boarded or not, walking or jumping: each opening is closed to you, sill to lintel (`zmWinPen`), on the ground floor and up the tower alike, and so are the boarded gaps in the courtyard's walls and the heads of the jetty's ladders. Only the drowned come in by them. The jetty's ladders have no boards: what comes up them is on the deck at once. Nor can you go into the harbour: the water round the jetty is out of reach (`seaguard`), and its edges hold you, walking or jumping.
- **Unstick a drowned** (the in-game menu, Esc, only here): if one's stuck somewhere out of reach and the round won't end, this takes the one that's gone longest without getting any nearer you by the walk (else the furthest off) back out to sea, and it comes up again outside the window nearest you, to tear its way in. Once every 20 seconds, for everyone; a line in the feed says who did it. Joined to someone's night, it asks the host (an `unstick` request).
- **Points.** 10 a hit, 60 a kill, 100 through the head, 130 with the cutlass, 80 burned by the Fresnel Lamp. You start on the jetty with 500 and a pepperbox (together, all of you along its pier). Nailing a window's boards back (hold Use) is 10 a board, up to 500 a round for each player (after that the boards still go back, for nothing).
- **Wall buys.** Weapons hang on boards on the walls with their names and prices chalked under them: Pepperbox (250) on the jetty's shed, Blunderbuss (500) on the courtyard's sea wall, Longarm and Blunderbuss (500) in the Keeper's Quarters, Pepperbox (250), Cutlass (750), Harpoon gun (1000), Avtomat (1200), another Blunderbuss in the Boathouse, and up the tower an Avtomat (the Chart Room's floor), a Harpoon gun (the Lamp-Wright's Store's) and a Longarm (the Service Room's), each hung and bought on its own floor. Buy one again for half its price to fill its ammunition. You carry two guns and, beside them, one blade (the cutlass or the Boarding Axe): a blade never takes a gun's place, so drawing the cutlass never leaves you without your guns. A third gun replaces the gun in your hand (or, with the blade out, the gun you held last); a second blade replaces the first. Each carries a reserve (reloads draw from it). Damage is scaled for the drowned: the longarm hits four times as hard and goes through three of them, the harpoon ten times (40 a hit: every one of them to round twenty), and an iron that kills goes on through into the next, up to four more; the cutlass two and a half. The harpoon carries 30 in reserve here. The blunderbuss takes a heavier charge here than on Wreckers' Night (`ZM_SHOT`) and throws it in a wide, flat fan from side to side instead of a round cone: twenty-eight balls laid evenly across about 46 degrees (0.4 either way), barely rising or falling, each its own hit at 1.2 times weight, so one blast sweeps across a whole line of them; every ball goes on through into the one behind as well (`ZM_PIERCE`). Whole out to 4 m, a ball's weight falls away in a line to 10% at 16 m. It holds two charges here, not one, loads both in about 2 seconds (three quarters of the time), and carries 40 in reserve (`ZM_LOAD`, `ZM_AMMO`). On average one of them alone in front of you takes 24 at 2 m, 12 at 4 m, 6 at 6 m, 4 at 8 m and under 1 past 14 m: held down, about 15 a second at arm's length, 7 at 4 m and 4 at 6 m, in line with the other guns (the Avtomat about 5 a second, 10 with head shots; the Longarm 4, 6.5).
- **Door buys.** The sea gate from the jetty into the courtyard (750; the Coal Store opens with it, through its arch), the Signal Hut's door (500), the Keeper's Quarters' door from the courtyard (1000), and nine more doors and heaps of wreckage between the rooms of the station (750 to 1250).
- **The wreck chest** (950): a random weapon, never one you're carrying. It stands on one of six plinths: the Fog-Signal House (where it starts), the East Passage, the Store Hall, the Wreck Store, at the top of the tower, in the lantern room, and in the courtyard, at the foot of the station's wall. A pale shaft of light stands over it. After three goes in one place, each go has a one-in-three chance of a gull taking it: your points come back, and the chest turns up on another plinth.
- **The Fresnel Lamp** (only from the chest): a signal lantern with a stepped lighthouse lens in it. Hold the trigger and it throws a cone of the Light's own fire, thirty metres or to the first wall, ten bursts a second; whatever stands in it burns (about a third of a drowned man's strength each burst, whatever the round), and each one it burns throws a spark to the nearest that hasn't, and on. It burns whatever part of one the beam is on, head to foot, so the tall ones (the Diver, Maren) burn wherever you hold it on them, and a broad one is that much easier to keep in it. Sixty bursts to a reservoir; reloading unscrews the empty reservoir, screws in a full one and cranks the wick up. Only in the Lighthouse's chest: at Wrackhaven the Hermit's Claw takes its place.
- **The Hermit's Claw** (only from Wrackhaven's chest, in place of the Fresnel Lamp; `src/zmcrab.js`): a great red crab's claw on a brass gauntlet, with a whelk shell on the cuff that crabs live in. Each pull, the claw snaps shut with a crack and a burst of water off its tip that hurts and knocks back everything right in front of you (3.4 m, a wide cone), and a hermit crab is flung out of the shell. It lands, scuttles sideways to the nearest drowned it can see, and clamps onto its leg: that one is held where it stands and nipped three times a second (each nip about an eighth of a drowned man's strength, whatever the round) for up to 3.6 seconds; then the crab goes on to the next (two in all) and burrows into the floor. Three crabs to the shell, 18 in reserve; reloading tips the claw over, pushes a new crab into the shell's mouth and works the claw twice. Same odds in the chest as the Lamp (9). Together, the thrower's screen runs its crabs; the others see the snap and the crabs scuttling.
- **The wreck chest's own eight** (`src/zmchest.js`; also never on a wall, and never on Wreckers' Night's islands). The chest's odds are weights, one against another, out of 142 (whatever you're carrying is left out): Avtomat 18, Longarm, Blunderbuss and Harpoon gun 14 each, Duck's-Foot 10, Fresnel Lamp 9 (the Hermit's Claw at Wrackhaven), Grapnel Gun, Flare Pistol and Boarding Axe 8 each, Cutlass, Pepperbox, Net Mortar and Chain-Shot 7 each, Bomb-Lance 6, Punt Gun 5. Damage is in pepperbox body shots against the drowned.

What you unlock joins your other outfits, finishes, boat skins and banners (they only show there once unlocked), to wear the usual way (`src/zmegg.js`).

### Keeper's Last Light, together

Up to four in one night (`src/zmnet.js`). The **Play together** part of the Lighthouse's card lists the nights being hosted here; **Host a night** opens a lobby, and the others tap it to join. Nothing starts on its own: the lobby waits, showing who's in, until the host presses **Start the night** (alone, too, if they like). When the night ends everyone goes back to the same lobby, and the host can start another. You stay in that lobby on the menu too; hosting or joining from another game's card (a raid, a fog run, or a night from the raid lobby) takes you out of it for you, and so does playing alone from another card.

- Each of the others has a small health bar under their name over their head: a segment for each knock they can take (six with Stout Heart), green when whole, amber when hurt, red on the last, an empty bar edged in red when they're down; it shrinks with distance and shows only when their name does (each player's presence carries their health, `hp`, and the most they can have, `hm`).
- More of you, more of them: each round brings half as many again for every player after the first. The drowned go for whichever of you is nearest by the walk.
- Points are each player's own (the list down the right shows everyone's). Doors open for all; the chest belongs to whoever opened it until they take what it offers; salvage works for everyone (a Powder Keg fills everyone's ammunition, a Lamp Flash pays everyone 400).
- The bosses come in the same numbers whatever your number, their strength scaled to it; the host runs them, and streams for each its kind, its strength, its blow and how far into it, and where it'll land (the anchor's ring, the lane of a charge, `bo`); each of you judges their blows on yourself (the ring, the false light, the charge passing over you), and the false light drags only the ones it caught.
- Going down: 45 seconds on the floor (your tonics gone), in last stand: your eyes drop to the floor, you can only crawl (0.6 m/s; no running, jumping, doors, buying or the chest), and you hold a pepperbox (a full cylinder and 12 more rounds, whether you still carry one or not) that you can look around and fire as usual, its hits and kills scoring as ever. Once it's empty, with nothing left to load, you can knife from the floor: Fire (or the knife key or button) slashes as usual, and the phone's Fire button turns to KNIFE. The drowned leave you alone for the ones still standing. A red vignette thumps with your heartbeat, deeper and quicker as the time runs out, under a draining ring with the seconds left, "You're down — X s", and "Hold on…". When you go down, everyone else sees "<name> is down!" in red across the middle of their screen for four seconds (with "Hold Use beside them to get them up" under it, and the same in the feed), on either map. One of the others holding Use beside you for three seconds (a second and a half if they have the Grog Ration) gets you up, with everything you carried back in your hands as it was. While they hold Use your clock stops where it is (in the Lighthouse and in Maren's End alike), and it runs on again if they let go before you're up (a Powder Keg taken while you were down fills it). The one helping sees a ring filling round the sights with your name and your time left; you see "<name> is getting you up" and its progress; everyone sees a marker over you, through walls, with your time draining in a ring, how far off you are, and a gold ring as someone gets you up; the moment you're up, the ring, the marker and the helper's bar are gone from every screen (each player's presence carries their own time on the floor and whom they're getting up). Bleed out and you're back at the start of the next round with a pepperbox, beside someone still standing; until then you watch the night through someone else's eyes: your view goes to a teammate still in it (those standing first), first person from where they stand, their floor of the tower and which way they look (and down at the floor with them if they go down), with "Watching <name> — back next round", their health and, if they're down, their time left, in a band low in the middle. A click, Space, RB (or A) or a tap moves on to the next of them (LB back); nothing else you press does anything, and your weapons and ammunition are off the screen. If the one you're watching bleeds out, it moves on to someone else; when the next round brings you back, the view is yours again (facing the way you were watching). Second Wind still gets you up by yourself. Alone, with no Second Wind, going down ends the night. When nobody is left standing, the night is over for everyone. The pause menu doesn't stop a night together.
- How it works: the host's browser runs the night (rounds, the drowned, windows, doors, the chest, salvage) and streams it ten times a second in its presence under the key `zm` (each of the drowned a short string under its id in `z`, with what never changes about it, its kind, window and look, apart in `zs`). Everyone moves and shoots for themselves and sends where they are, their shots (for the others to see and hear), their hits on the drowned (which the host applies, and credits back as points, kills and headshots), and their requests (a door, the chest, taking from it, a board on a window, getting someone up, unsticking one of the drowned). A swing that comes down on you is judged on your own screen. The server relays a night's presences at full rate between its players, and only a short summary (enough for the lobby list) to everyone else. On the game's own site only what changed goes over the wire: after one whole copy, each player sends a patch of just the parts of their presence that differ from last time (the drowned who stood still, the boards, the chest, the feed and everything else that hasn't changed stay out of it), the server passes patches on the same way, and a whole copy goes every five seconds anyway so nothing can drift. With thirty of the drowned and crabs about, that's about 8 KB a second from the host instead of 14, and a joined player sends about a tenth of what they did. Hits and requests stay in what a joined player sends for three seconds and then drop off. On a joined player's screen the drowned and the other players are shown a moment behind (about one gap between messages, longer when they come in fits and starts) and slide between the places they were said to be, so a late message no longer freezes them and then makes them jump.

How it's built (`src/zombies.js`): the station is laid out by the same island builder as Wreckers' Night's islands (`fpsIsleGen`, with an `indoor` kind that skips the shore), with a few new props (floors, ceilings, stair treads, the core, railings, the lens, tonic cabinets, plinths, hanging lanterns). Unlike the islands, some solids are **floors**: stair treads and the core carry a flag, and anything standing (you, the drowned) can step up onto one no more than 0.6 m above its feet, stands on the highest under it, steps down stairs, and falls off edges; the camera eases over each step. Heights carry through shooting (a body's height is its floor plus any jump). The drowned find their way on a half-metre grid laid over the station, with each cell's floor height, and a Dijkstra search out from wherever you stand four times a second; opening a door rebuilds the grid. The doors, window boards, wall weapons (the first-person models, hung on the wall), the chest, salvage, the beam and the drowned themselves (simple jointed figures with a shambling, lurching walk, a swing, a climb, a fall, and a burn) are drawn in their own group in the 3D scene; the station's lamps light the rooms, with the night dimmer than outdoors. Shots resolve straight against the drowned where they stand (there's nobody else's view to be fair to). The jetty is a floor too (`deck`, its top `ZMD.TOP` = 0.9 m over the ground, up two steps from the sea gate); south of the shore line (`ZM_SHORE`) the island lies under the harbour's own water, a swaying grid laid a hair over the ground that dips under it along the shore and slopes down into the open sea past the island's edge, with a ripple in its light (`zm3dWater`). Nobody walks in it (blocks of `seaguard` round the jetty); the drowned come up out of it (`zmSeaDepth`: how far under they are as they wade), up the ladders at the jetty's edges (windows marked `sea`, with no boards: up out of the water and over the edge in 1.7 s) or ashore to the boarded gaps in the sea wall (marked `shore`). The courtyard and the jetty have no ceilings: the sky is overhead (`ZM_ZONES` marks them `sky`; `zmOutdoors(x, y, z)` says whether a place is under the open sky). The walking grid now runs from the Store Hall's north wall down to the end of the jetty.

## Maren's End (a raid, for one to four)

A game of its own, with its own card on the title screen, **Skerry Raids**, which holds both raids, and its own column on the scoreboard. It's built on Keeper's Last Light: the same drowned, the same way of playing together (host a raid, the others join from the card's **Play together** list, and the host starts it). You go in with a pepperbox and a blunderbuss, and your weapon and outfit crates open from the raid's own card.

**Stamina.** In Maren's End sprinting tires you. A stamina bar sits beside your health bar: full, it's ten seconds of sprinting, and it fills back up from empty in fifteen seconds whenever you aren't sprinting. Run it dry and you're out of breath (the bar goes red and says so): you can't sprint again until it's back to a quarter. It works the same for all three callings and under Maren's Weight (the Weight slows your pace; stamina is how long you can sprint at it). The other modes are unchanged.

**Part I · The Swallow.** You start on a quay at the top of a long, wide pier that runs out over the dry bed of a sea that's gone, and on out over the Swallow, a great funnel-shaped hole in the sea bed (wide at its mouth, narrowing to its throat far below), to a round platform hanging over its middle. Once you're halfway along, the drowned start coming up the pier behind you from the quay end, a good deal quicker than at the Lighthouse. Stand on the platform and it becomes a ring to hold: while any of you stands on it, every one of them you send back counts, wherever it falls; thirty and the platform gives way, dropping you (and anyone on it with you) into the Swallow; anyone still on the pier can step off its end after. You drop straight down the throat (the funnel's sides are only scenery: nothing to catch on or land on, and you can't drift out over them); it's about twenty seconds down, falling fast, the wind roaring past, before you land softly at the bottom. Down there it's black but for pale lichen on the rock and the light of **Maren's Lantern**, which every raider carries in their left hand (low in the bottom-left corner of the screen, kept there whatever your field of view). It's only for Part I: it's gone once you're through the door.

- A wide path winds away through the dark, a long way: out and most of the way round the outside of the cave, then in and down to the door in the middle, with 18 braziers along it (their light reaches about 9 m round them). The drowned down here are a little slower than at the Lighthouse. Hold Use at a dark brazier (just over a second) to light it from the lantern.
- Out in the dark the dark drains your health (from full to nothing in about fifteen seconds), so get from one brazier to the next. Inside a lit brazier's light you get back what the dark took, and your ammunition tops up; what the drowned (or a wrecking ball) took comes back by itself, quickly, once nothing has hit you for about two seconds, anywhere (the same quicker wait as in the raid's other parts).
- Glowing drowned climb up out of the ground and come after you as you go, plenty of them, more the further along you are, and always inside the path's walls.
- **Maren's Weight:** while you're out of the light it settles on you at once, taking a tenth off your pace, and slows you more and more from there (down to a quarter of your pace after about thirteen seconds; a bar under the screen's middle shows how much). Step into any lit brazier's light and it lifts at once.
- **Wrecking balls:** spiked iron balls hang on chains from the dark roof over the path, one between each two braziers and two in the longer gaps (16 in all), swinging across it. Time your way past: one that catches you puts you straight down, however much health you have.
- Every minute or so a glowing Diver comes up out of the dark as well, one at a time. Down here there's no bar for his strength, so you can't tell how close he is to falling.
- At the end of the path is a glowing door. Kill twenty of the drowned inside the ring in front of it and it opens; go through to finish Part I. Anyone who bled out comes back at the next brazier lit. Going through the door takes the whole team on together, and everyone is revived: anyone down is back on their feet, and anyone who bled out comes back with their weapons.

The scoreboard keeps your best time through the raid, the most damage you've done to its boss, and the drowned you've killed across all your raids. The later parts of the raid follow on from the door.

How it's built (`src/raid.js`): the raid is a map of the Lighthouse engine (`ZM_MAPS.raid`), with its own rounds switched off and each part a stage in `RAID_STAGES` (taken in the order of `RAID_ORDER`). A stage lays out its own ground, ticks on the host, sends what changes in the host's presence (under `rd` and `rs`), and draws its own 3D. The next part starts with `raidNextStage()`, and the last one calls `raidWin()`.

### Maren's End, Part II: The Black Water

Through the glowing door at the end of the Deep Path you come out on the near bank of a black river under the ground, running fast from one wall of the cavern to the other. Nobody can swim it, and the bank's edge won't let you try. Across it stand the piles of an old bridge and nothing between them (`src/raidbridge.js`). The banks are strewn with what the river has thrown up over the years: the bones of the drowned, upturned canoes, broken oars, crates, barrels, old nets and coils of rope. None of it is in anyone's way except one big upturned boat on the near bank, which you can put your back to.

- **The circles.** Well out either side of the bridge's foot is a wide ring of old stones (about thirteen metres across) on a raised stone platform, with steps up its south and outer sides and a brazier at its corner; the drowned climb the steps after you. As on the Long Pier in the first part, while the circles are held every kill counts, wherever the drowned fall (a kill inside a circle, or from inside one, always does), and the bridge builds itself out over the water, plank by plank, a good metre up over the river on its piles, with stone steps up to the deck at either end: nine planks, about twenty kills with two or more of you (sixteen alone). The planks fill in along the top of the screen. While you stand in a circle (any of the three) its braziers keep your powder dry: your ammunition is made up a little every second. Here, unlike up the Swallow, you mend by yourself if they leave you alone for a few seconds.
- **Holding the bridge.** The bridge only stands while it's held. Either the near circles are held (with three or more of you on your feet, both of them; with two, one; alone, one), or someone stands in the **far ring**, a third ring of stones on its own platform at the far end of the bridge, with steps up from the bridge and from the stands behind it. Held from the far ring, the near circles can be left and everyone can cross. Leave it unheld and, after a moment (alone, or when you're the only one still on your feet: a minute and a half, counted down at the top), the bridge starts to fall back into the river a plank at a time from the far end; anyone on a plank that goes is swept back to the near bank. The three rings show under the planks (the far one in the middle). The drowned keep coming up out of the river and out of the dark at the cavern's sides, thick and fast: up to fourteen at once alone, four more for each of you beyond the first.
- **The Cutlasses of Plimsoll.** Three times over the first eight minutes (at 0:45, 4:30 and 8:00) a Diver comes up out of the river on to the near bank with a great Cutlass of Plimsoll in his fist instead of an anchor: a heavy cutlass with a sea-blue blade, a row of the shark's own teeth set along its edge and a grip of his skin. Anything hurts him; put him down and his Cutlass falls free, glowing faintly blue where it lies. Use takes it up (it goes into your hands as a third weapon, so you keep your own two; one each). Each of you who's going over to fight wants one. If you go down or leave, your Cutlass falls where you were, for someone else to take up. The top of the screen counts the Cutlass Divers and the time to the next. A Cutlass swings quicker and cuts harder than the ordinary cutlass.
- **The helmeted Divers.** When the bridge first reaches the far bank and someone carrying a Cutlass sets foot over there, the Divers come up out of the river by the far bank one after another, three in all, with a few seconds between them. Nothing hurts them but a Cutlass of Plimsoll (shots and every other blade glance off their brass); sixteen cuts each. Each drops his brass helmet where he falls. Pick it up (Use; one at a time), carry it to one of the three iron stands at the back of the far bank, in front of the door, and Use to set it there.
- **Bleeding out here.** Anyone who bled out is back on their feet (beside someone standing) each time a Cutlass Diver goes down, when the bridge first reaches the far bank, and each time a helmeted Diver goes down or a helmet is set. Two of you can do all of it: one holds a ring while the other carries the Cutlass.
- **The door.** With the third helmet on its stand, the iron door in the rock behind the stands sinks into the floor. Get everyone over (one of you holds a ring for the rest), Use the door, and you all go on to the third part.
- Together, the host runs all of it: what the bridge has built, which rings are held, the Cutlass Divers' clock, the Cutlasses and who carries them, the Divers and the helmets go out in the raid's part of the host's word (`rb`, `rbc` for the Cutlasses, `rbz` for which Divers carry a sword and which a helmet, `rbh` for the helmets and `rbe` for what's just happened), and a joined player asks the host to take up a Cutlass, pick up a helmet, set one down or open the door. The river is a row of solids that stop walking but not shots, with one over each plank's span of the bridge, swapped for the plank's deck as it's built and back again when it falls, so the drowned only find their way across once it's there.

### Maren's End, Part III: Maren's End

The last part of the raid. The door at the end of the bridge lets you out in the Sally Port of an old Spanish sea fort on a headland, its south front a sea wall over the open ocean.

- **The fort:** the Plaza de Armas (the great courtyard) in the middle, with the cistern and four braziers; round it the Powder Magazine, the Guardroom, the Chapel of Our Lady of the Sea, the Cookhouse, the Governor's Rooms (the Lampwright's Bench), the Sally Port, the Barracks and the Officers' Mess, with wall buys and a Mystery Box as at the Lighthouse, but **no tonics**: in the fort you go without. Along the south side, up a grand stair, the **Sea Battery**: cannon on top of the sea wall, a stone sentry box at each end. From up there, far off across the water on the shore, you can see the **Lighthouse**, lit, its one beam turning (as it does from Wrackhaven's quay). Here you mend by yourself again, as at the Lighthouse.
- **The Lampwright's Bench in Maren's End** only gives a weapon its reforged look and its own trick (Twelve Bells' ricochets, the Equinox Gale's spin-up and so on): against the drowned it hits as hard as a reforged weapon does at the Lighthouse, but against the raid's bosses (the Divers, the Mooncussers, the Knock, Maren and her soul) it hits no harder than before; its magazine and spare ammunition stay as they were.
- **The three apothecaries:** San Telmo (west), Santa Bárbara (east) and San Lázaro (north), each lit its own colour, cabinets of drawers and bottles round the walls. A **Mooncusser** keeps each one: step inside and he lifts his false light. Put all three down. The top left shows which are still kept.
- **The Knock, Bat of Maren:** when the third keeper falls, three knocks on wood, and the Knock bursts up through the flagstones of the Plaza first: a driftwood plank half again as tall as Maren (about nine metres), with big rolling eyes, a long nose and a grin, thin bare arms and legs, and a bat. It can always be hurt. Its blows, each its own:
  - **the swing:** close in, a low swing of the bat that knocks you back;
  - **three knocks:** it raps the bat on the flagstones three times, and each rap sends an orange ring racing out across the floor (each one that catches you; jump over them);
  - **the cleave:** the bat up over its head while a line glows on the floor towards you, then down, and the floor splits along it (step off the line);
  - **the throw:** it hurls the bat at you end over end and it comes back to its hand (it can catch you going and coming; sidestep).
  - **its song:** at three quarters, half and a quarter of its health it stands and sings, beating the bat into its other hand, the words coming up line by line top left. The song takes **15 seconds**, and while it sings a shot does only a tenth of its damage. A red bar fills under its health bar; if it fills, the song is sung and **the whole squad is killed**. Every hit also fills a thin gold break meter under it: fill that and the song's broken and the Knock reels, senseless, for about 3 seconds. The break takes 48 damage alone and 40 more for each extra player (128 for three), so a full squad shooting well breaks it about 9 seconds in; if any of you go down while it sings, the break needs only what the ones still standing would (two of you with one down: 48).
  When it falls (it topples over backwards like a felled plank), **"Maren is angered"** comes up, and a few seconds later she rises.
- **Maren Holm:** when the Knock falls she comes up out of the sea and over the wall onto the Sea Battery, three times the height of a man, in a long dark dress, one arm raised. While a sea-green light hangs about her she **can't be hurt**. Her soul is out of her: **Verity**, the smiling yellow egg, glowing and turned to face you as he drifts over the water beyond the sea wall, marked on your screen, big and drifting high enough (about 15 m up) to be seen and shot over the sea wall from almost anywhere in the Plaza. It has **30 health** of its own (counted as weak-spot hits: 15 from the Avtomat, 6 from the Longarm), shown as a gold bar under its mark, and it's whole again each time it rises. **Burst it** and she can be hurt for **30 seconds** (she flushes red, and her bar turns red); then her soul rises out of the sea again somewhere else. Shoot it again, and again, until she's dead. If the raid's secret boss was put down earlier in the same raid, **Maren has no soul**: it never rises, and she can be hurt the whole fight.
- **How hard they hit:** every blow of the Knock's and Maren's takes **half your health** (three of your six knocks, six of twelve with Stout Heart), so two hits put you down. Both strike quickly and often: the Knock's wind-ups are about a third shorter than they were and it's ready again in one to two and a half seconds; Maren's sweep comes in 0.6 s and her ring erupts after 1.6 s, and both move faster.
- **Her second phase: the storm.** At a third of her health she stops, rises off the floor with her arms up and calls up the storm (she can't be hurt while she does): the sky closes over in racing black cloud, and **every light in the fort goes out**. As she calls it the sea takes back every one of the drowned and every other boss (her soul stays), and none come while she calls; from then on there are a fifth fewer in the fort at once, coming a fifth slower. From then on she takes **a quarter less** from every hit, moves about a third faster and strikes more often.
  - **The Lantern Goes Out:** it's dark. Each of you carries a small lantern (the others' show as glows), and all you can see of her is her two pale eyes. **Lightning** flashes every second or two, with bolts over the sea, and for a moment it shows the whole fort, and whatever's creeping up on you.
  - **The Bell Toll:** a drowned bell tolls three times, about 3.6 seconds apart, and two or three big **sea-green circles** (about 15 m across) glow on the floor of the Plaza and the Battery (a bright rim, a curtain of light standing up off it, and a marker with the distance on your screen). At the third toll **everyone outside a circle is dragged under: half your health**. Inside a circle the drowned (and the other bosses) wade **40% slower**. While the bell tolls she calls no rings of her own (her arm sweep still lands). Then the circles fade, and a couple of seconds later the bell starts again with new ones somewhere else.
- **Her blows:** close in she sweeps her arm through you. Further off she raises her arms and a sea-green ring about 8 m across glows on the floor round one of you; after a moment the sea erupts through it. Get out of the ring, or jump. She keeps to the Plaza and the Battery: she's too big for the doors.
- **All through the fight** the drowned keep climbing in at the windows and up the ladders out of the sea onto the Battery, and every half minute or so another boss comes too (the Diver, a Mooncusser or the Keelback): one at a time alone or with two of you, two at a time with three or four.
- **Bleeding out in the fort.** Anyone who bled out is back on their feet (beside someone standing) each time a keeper falls, when the Knock falls, each time Maren's soul is shot, and when Maren is laid to rest, when anyone down gets up too, so everyone can take her token.
- **Her pirate token:** she drops one where she falls. Walk over it and it's yours: **one for each of you**. It's a pirate token like the Lighthouse's, spent on the Keeper's Last Light card. Once everyone standing has taken theirs (or after 45 seconds), the raid is won.
- **Something else, somewhere in Maren's End.** Nobody will tell you where, or how. If you stumble on it, you'll know, and he doesn't sit still: it's somewhere different every raid. It's **Verity**, and he fights in two phases: first as the smiling yellow orb you know from the sea, then, at half his strength, standing up as his real self. Put him down and he drops a pirate token, Verity's Piece of Eight (one for each of you, spent on the Keeper's Last Light card; `src/raidverity.js`).
  The first time you take his token he gives you everything else he has too: **Verity's Wardrobe**, twelve cosmetics no crate holds, in the Locker's grade under his own name and yellow (six pips, a yellow-and-white border turning round the card). They join your other outfits, finishes, boat skins and banners, and they're shown in the Gannet's Locker too (`src/verityfx.js`). Every one moves and every one makes a sound, and he sometimes giggles when you equip one:
  - **Verity's Grin** (outfit): his own smiling yellow head on your shoulders, blinking, its grin widening, his yellow alight in the coat's trim, a yellow halo, and three little Veritys circling you. Now and then you giggle when you take a knock, and in any game each second there's a 1 in 150 chance his voice plays.
  - **A finish for every weapon**: Little Helper (pepperbox), Big Grin (blunderbuss, a great grin round the bell), He's Watching (longarm, his eye in the scope), Tongue Out (harpoon gun, his tongue down it), All Teeth (cutlass, a row of teeth along the edge) and Giggle Box (avtomat, its faces rolling round it). His faces are set all over the gun, each blinking in its own time and grinning wider, and every three seconds his giggle runs down it as a ripple of light (`src/wcadmiral.js`). Now and then a shot squeaks like a toy, and now and then a kill gets a giggle.
  - **A skin for every boat**: his yellow breathing in her topsides with a grin along her side, his giggle running bow to stern, little smiling faces bobbing up in her wake to watch her go, and smiling orbs at her mastheads. And each its own: **Verity's Smile** (Brig: his great face on the mainsail, its eyes looking about, and from her deck his face rising over the horizon like a moon), **The Little Helper** (Cutter: a little Verity hopping over the waves beside her), **The Yellow Room** (Barque: damp yellow wallpaper on her sails and strip lights along her rails, buzzing, flickering and going out) and **Many Happy Faces** (Carrier: Verity balloons tethered over her flight deck). At sea, every couple of minutes, he giggles (or the Helper boings, the strip lights buzz, a balloon pops), and sometimes when you haul a crate aboard.
  - **Verity's Smile** (banner, for the Drowned Reach): his damp yellow over your land with his faces in it, blinking in waves and grinning, giggles rippling out round them, and strip lights flickering along your borders.
- **The victory screen:** all of you stand together on the sea wall at sunset, in your own outfits with your calling's weapon in hand. The player with the best night overall (the MVP: kills, headshots, boss damage, Maren's soul shot, friends got up, less every time you went down) stands in the middle on a gold step, and the rest stand out to either side in order. Under each player are the three things they did best compared with the others (a star marks the most of anyone). These can be any of: drowned sent back, through the head, damage to the bosses, hits on Maren's soul, friends got back up, metres sprinted, metres walked, time in the air, time by a brazier's light, shots fired, shots that hit, most hit with one shot, grenades thrown, ultimates called, or times out of breath. Playing together, the host gathers everyone's numbers, so every screen shows the same people in the same order. The buttons: **Spend your pirate token** (opens the Gannet's Locker), **Play again** (together, back to the lobby) and **Main menu** (`src/raidmvp.js`). Nobody falls when the raid is won: everyone standing stays standing, anyone down gets up, until the screen comes up. The sunset music plays on under it.
- Together it's run by the host, as at the Lighthouse; everyone's hits on her soul add up.

### Maren's End: the music

Every part of the raid has its own music (`src/raidmusic.js`), made with Stable Audio 3 in ComfyUI (three takes of each, the one that loops best kept; `tools/sfx/music.py`). Along the Long Pier, over the dry bed of the sea, a sad and ghostly piece plays: a slow cello, a far-off voice, the wind over the salt and the creak of old wrecks. Step off the end and a dark, deep chant rises as you fall, low voices far off at first, swelling with drums and low brass all the way down, played 30% quicker than it was made, to keep up with the fall, and timed so it reaches its climax just as you hit the floor; as you land it hands over to the Deep Path's eerie cave theme (drones, dripping water, a lonely concertina's slow shanty, creaking timbers). Part II, the black river, has an eerie underground piece of its own (rushing water, a hurdy-gurdy drone, ropes creaking, far-off sailors humming). Part III, the fort, plays a dark Spanish pirate theme on flamenco guitar, castanets and brass. The Knock fights to the thump of a heavy wooden bat; when it sings, a dark chant over knocking takes over and gets quicker and quicker through the fifteen seconds of its song, until it's sung or broken. From “Maren is angered” until she's down, her own theme: a ghostly boss fight with a wailing choir, war drums, a shanty fiddle and a foghorn. When she goes into her second phase, it turns to her last stand in the storm: a great iron bell tolling through it, a screaming choir, pounding war drums, driving strings and brass. It rises low as she summons the storm, comes in full as the fight begins, and swells with every bell toll and lightning flash. Her storm has its own sound too: a thunder crack with every flash of lightning, and a soft, swirling wind kept low in the background, gusting a little with each toll and flash, until she's down. And when Maren falls, a bittersweet sunset after the fight: a slow shanty on fiddle and concertina over warm strings, a far-off ship's horn and the waves, played once through and on into the end card. While the raid's on, the Lighthouse's boss themes keep quiet.

Every boss and miniboss in Maren's End (the Divers, the Mooncussers, the Knock and Maren) has 15% more health than it would at the Lighthouse, before the extra for each player; the helmeted Divers in Part II take sixteen cuts instead of fourteen.

### Maren's End: the three callings

When a raid in Maren's End starts, each player picks a calling from a card over the view (your last one is marked; on a controller, left and right, then A). Each goes down with two weapons of its own, a way of jumping, a grenade and an ultimate. **G** (or **Q**) throws the grenade, which comes back after a while; **X** calls the ultimate once its ring is full. The ring fills slowly by itself and faster with every kill. On a controller they're **LB** and **RB** (or up on the D-pad); on a touch screen, the two round buttons side by side left of the fire cluster (the grenade nearer Fire, the ultimate beside it). Both meters sit bottom left.

| | Mercenary | Brute | Apothecary |
|---|---|---|---|
| Plays | quick and sharp-eyed | hard to stop | keeps the others standing |
| Primary | Avtomat | Blunderbuss | Duck's-Foot |
| Secondary | Longarm | Boarding Axe | Flare Pistol |
| Jump | **Second Step**: jump again in mid-air | **Anchor Drop**: a higher jump; jump again in the air to plunge down and shake the ground where you land (knocks back and hurts all round you, every 3 s) | **Drift**: jump again in the air to float down slowly, for up to 3 s (again to stop) |
| Grenade | **Powder Flask** (back in 16 s): a short fuse, and it bursts at once if it strikes one of them (it reaches 7.2 m) | **Scrap Keg** (22 s): a big, slow blast reaching 10.4 m that throws them back | **Brimstone Vial** (20 s): breaks into a cloud of green fire reaching 6.8 m for 7 s that burns and slows the drowned in it, and mends you and your friends standing in it |
| Ultimate | **Dead Reckoning**: a heavy pistol for 12 s, six shots, each through every one of them in its line | **Bulwark**: 14 s when no knock lands on you, and every pull of the trigger is a quake that hurls back all in front of you | **Wellspring**: a ring of green light reaching 9 m out at your feet for 15 s: inside it everyone mends a knock every 0.6 s, knocks are halved, the drowned are slowed, and anyone down in it gets back up |

In Maren's End you can take six knocks before you go down (twelve with Stout Heart), not three; where you mend by yourself (Parts II and III) it starts after 2.2 seconds left alone instead of 3.2, and a lit brazier in Part I mends you a third faster (1.2 a second). The guns that load one round at a time hold more there: the Blunderbuss 3 shots to a load (about 3 seconds to load all three), the Longarm 2 (its load takes 15% longer), and the Harpoon gun, Flare Pistol, Grapnel Gun, Bomb-Lance, Net Mortar and Chain-Shot 2 each (15 to 20% longer to load). The Punt Gun stays one enormous load. The Duck's-Foot holds 6 volleys to a load there instead of 4, loads in 1.6 s instead of 2.4, and carries 72 in reserve instead of 40. Every gun's reserve is bottomless there: you still reload each magazine, but what you carry never runs out (the count shows ∞). The only salvage the drowned leave in the raid is Double Salvage (double points for thirty seconds): no Powder Kegs, Black Spots or Lamp Flashes, from the drowned, the bosses or the last crab of a swarm. The grenades hit twice as hard as they first did, and their blasts and clouds (and the Wellspring) are twice as wide. The weapons are the ordinary ones, so the skins from your weapon crates and the outfit from your outfit crates go down with you, and kills in the raid count towards both kinds of crate. Together, everyone sees the others' grenades, Wellsprings and quakes, and a mark in each player's calling colour over their head (ringed while their ultimate is up).

## The Gannet's Wake (a second raid, for one to four)

A second raid, picked at the top of the Skerry Raids card (**Raid: Maren's End / The Gannet's Wake**; the pick is kept on this device, and together the host's pick goes out with the lobby, so the lobby list shows which raid each one is). It runs on the same engine as Maren's End: the three callings, going down and getting each other up, stamina, bottomless reserves, crates from kills, and its own column on the scoreboard (best time through, most boss damage in one raid, the drowned sent back in all your raids).

A year to the night after the *Gannet* broke up on the skerries, her bell is heard again off Skerry Light. The keeper's cutter goes out to find what's ringing it. Part I brings the sailing game itself into the raid: you sail the cutter out together. Part II takes you below decks in the risen *Gannet*, and Part III up on to her quarterdeck against her captain. When he falls he leaves a pirate token for each of you, and the raid ends on the same victory screen as Maren's End (MVP in the middle, three best stats each, with flares fired and the bell rung counted too), to its own dawn music.

How it's built: the raid is its own map (`ZM_MAPS.gannet`, `RAIDS.gannet` in `src/raid.js`), with its parts added by `raidAdd`; what the parts share (the Light's beam, on one clock for everyone) is in `src/gannet.js`.

### The Gannet's Wake, Part I: The Skerries

You start on the deck of the keeper's cutter, at the foot of the Light with the open sea to the north (`src/gwsail.js`). Out there, about a kilometre and a half through the skerries, is the Gannet's mark. The deck is yours to walk: from the wheel and the flare locker at the stern, past the hatch and the mast with its bell, up to the bow. The deck stays put under your feet and the sea, the rocks and the Light go by around it, so you can fight, patch and ring the bell while she's under way. It's night and raining, and the only light out on the water is the Light's beam going round.

- **The helm.** Use at the wheel to take it. While you hold the helm your move keys (or the stick) are hers instead of yours: A and D steer, W makes sail (hold it and she makes it quicker), S shortens it. You stand at the wheel instead of walking, and gauges under the screen's middle show her rudder and her sail. Use again lets go, and so does going down; she holds her course and speed. She handles like the Brig in Skerry Light, sail for sail, and nothing here ever slows her down, holes included. One of you holds the helm at a time, and the panel says who.
- **The rocks** only show while the beam is on them, and they fade over three seconds once it has gone by. Your own lantern shows any rock right beside her, faintly. Brush one and she scrapes along it. Hit one head-on and she loses a plank: a hole opens in the deck near where she struck, the sea comes in at it, and she sheers off. Stand at a hole and hold Use (about two seconds) to nail planks over it; anyone can, and you can do it while she sails.
- **The flood.** The bar beside her planks fills from every open hole. With every hole patched the pumps bring it back down. If it fills she goes down, and that's the end of the raid.
- **Maren** stands on the sea behind you and comes one stage nearer every turn of the beam: 400, 260, 160, 90, then 35 m. You hear her before you see her: a swell under the stern, a woman humming, oars where there's no boat, and once in a while her shape out on the bowsprit in the beam. One turn after 35 m she's aboard, and whoever is nearest the stern goes down (alone, that's the end of the raid). Then she's back out where she began.
- **The ship's bell** hangs on the mast. Use rings it, and then it needs 16 seconds before it will swing again. It drives Maren back three stages, and it sinks the drowned in the water near her.
- **Plimsoll** walks ahead of you through the deep water, on a path that keeps clear of the rocks. While she's inside his ring (it widens the faster she goes), Maren holds where she is. Fall too far behind and he'll walk on without you after a few seconds.
- **The flare locker** at the stern fires a flare. It lights every rock within 350 m for seven seconds, and then the locker takes 35 seconds to reload.
- **The drowned** come up out of the sea alongside, climb her side and come in over the low places in the rail. More of them come the further out you get, and quicker again while the Gannet comes up.
- **Music.** The Skerries have their own: a tense, driving shanty at night over low strings, the hull creaking, the waves and a far-off bell.
- **The panel** at the top left shows her planks and the flood, the bell and the flare (ready, or how long), how far off Maren is and whether Plimsoll is holding her, and who has the helm and how fast she's going. The line above it gives the distance to the mark. Under the top of the screen, a bearing strip shows her bow, the Light, the mark, Plimsoll and his ring, and Maren.
- **The Gannet's mark** is a ring of broken spars standing up out of the sea, with reef round it; you hear her bell long before you get there. Bring the cutter inside the ring and hold her there while the Gannet rises alongside, black and streaming, her bell ringing. Then climb her side: Use at her ladder by the port rail. When all of you on your feet are up, or 25 seconds after the first of you, you go on to the next part.
- Together, the host sails her and runs everything. Her pose, the holes, the flood, Maren, Plimsoll, the bell, the flares and who has the helm go out in the raid's part of the host's word (keys beginning `g`). A joined player at the helm sends their keys to the host whenever they change, and once a second besides, and their own view of her follows the host's smoothly. A joined player asks the host to take or let go of the helm, ring the bell, fire a flare, patch a hole or climb the ladder.

### The Gannet's Wake, Part II: Below Decks

Up her side from the cutter and down the hatch off her weather deck, you come out on the risen Gannet's gun deck (`src/gwhold.js`). She's an 1880s brig, a year on the bottom and come up again. It's dark down here and everything runs with water. The only light is from the few lanterns still swinging from her beams as she rolls. Twelve guns stand on their carriages at shut ports, with hammocks slung between the beams over them, and the masts come down through the deck. Two railed companion stairs lead down to the hold, where ballast stones sit in slopping bilge water and casks are lashed in tiers along her sides. Forward of the hold, a low door opens on the cable tier, flooded far over your head, with the anchor cable coiled on its floor. Forward on the gun deck is the forecastle, with the galley hearth, the crew's bunks and their sea chests. Aft is the captain's cabin. Its door is shut on three locks.

- **Knock.** The Gannet's figurehead has walked off her bow. He's a carved plank of a man, taller than any of you, with rolling eyes, a long nose and a grin. He hunts whichever of you is nearest, but **he only moves while none of you can see him**. He counts as seen when he's in your view with nothing solid between you, and either near you or near a lantern. In the dark and far off, you can look straight at him and he'll keep coming. While he moves, you'll hear three knocks on wood, quicker and louder the nearer he is. An arrow shows which way they came from, and the screen says **KNOCK IS MOVING**. If he reaches you, you're down at once, whatever your health. He goes off into the dark for a while afterwards. Nothing hurts him; shots just thud into the wood. Left alone and far from everyone for long, he turns up somewhere nearer, out of sight.
- **The three keys.** The cabin door needs all three. Any of you can carry any of them. If you go down or leave, whatever you carry falls where you were, for someone else to take up. The keys show at the top left, each with where it is or who has it.
  - **The Bosun's key.** The Bosun is a big drowned sailor in a rotten oilskin, with a boarding axe and the key hanging at his belt. He stands guard in the hold and never leaves it. He sweeps the axe round close in and brings it down overhead, and the floor flashes where the chop will land. Put him down and his key falls where he does.
  - **The cable tier key.** It lies on the floor at the far end of the flooded cable tier, among the coils. Under the water you hold your breath, and a bar shows how long you have left (about nine seconds). After that you take a knock every second until you're up out of it. The first time anyone goes under, the drowned start coming up out of the water.
  - **Knock's key.** It hangs on a cord round Knock's neck. Hold Use beside him to slip it off, but only while he's held still. One of you keeps him in sight while another goes up to him. Alone, keep your eyes on him all the way. Once his key is gone, he's quicker.
- **The cabin door.** Use it with keys in hand to set them in their locks, one by one; different players can each set their own. When the third key turns, the door swings open. Use it again and you all go on to the next part.
- **The drowned.** They keep coming the whole time, up out of the bilge and through the decks wherever you aren't, with more for each extra player. You mend by yourself if they leave you alone for a few seconds.
- **Music.** Below decks has its own: creaking timbers, dripping water, slow suspense and knocking far off.
- **Together.** The host runs all of it: Knock (where he is, whether he's moving, and who's watching him), the keys, the Bosun and the drowned. The host judges what each player can see from where they stand and which way they're looking. A joined player asks the host to take up a key, take Knock's key, set keys in the door, or go through.

### The Gannet's Wake, Part III: The Quarterdeck

Through the captain's cabin door and up the companion, you come out on the Gannet's open weather deck under the night sky (`src/gwcapt.js`). She's an 1880s brig, risen off the bottom a year to the night after she broke up on the skerries. Her two masts stand over you, with the yards crossed, the sails hanging in rags, and the shrouds and ratlines black against the stars. Weed hangs from her rails and yards, and sea-water stands in pools on her planking.

- **The deck:**
  - **Aft**, up a short ladder each side, is the quarterdeck, with the wheel, the binnacle, a skylight and the stern lantern.
  - **Forward** is the forecastle, with the ship's bell in its belfry.
  - **The waist** lies between them. It holds the main and fore hatches, the capstan, her guns run out along both rails, casks and coiled rope, and the two flare lockers.
  - **All round her** is the sea. A way off to the west, on its rock, **Skerry Light** turns its beam, and once each sweep it crosses her deck. The beam is on the same clock on every screen and in every part of the raid.
  - You mend by yourself again here, as at the Lighthouse.
- **Captain Absalom Crane.** Her master drowned with her and hasn't let her go. He comes up through his own deck aft, by the wheel. He's more than twice the height of a man, in a rotted greatcoat with a cape and a wide sagging hat, with sea-water running off him all the while. He carries a brass speaking trumpet in his left hand and a boat hook in his right.
  - **He can only be hurt while there's light on him.** That means the Light's beam crossing the deck, for about a second and a half each sweep (the deck brightens and his coat flushes gold), or a flare.
  - **In the dark a shot does nothing at all.** It throws sparks off him, and the top left says **He's in the dark: no shot touches him**, with a count of how long until the beam comes round.
- **The flare lockers:** one on each side of the waist, port and starboard. Use one to fire a flare. It goes up over the deck and hangs there, lighting the whole deck (and him) for **7 seconds**. The locker then takes **35 seconds** to reload. Fire them one after the other, or both together for a short, bright burst.
- **The ship's bell:** on the forecastle. Use it to ring it; it can be rung again after **15 seconds**. At the sound of it:
  - Crane **staggers** where he stands, his hand to his head, for a couple of seconds.
  - Every one of the drowned on deck goes back over the side.
- **His blows:**
  - **The boat hook:** close in, he winds the hook back over his shoulder and sweeps it across in front of him. It takes **half your health**.
  - **The bellow:** he puts the trumpet to his mouth, and a pale cone shows on the deck in front of him. The screen warns you to **get out of its line**. When it blows, anyone in the cone takes a knock and is thrown back down the deck and off their feet.
  - **All hands!** He raises the trumpet to the sky and calls his crew. His drowned come up over the rails, more for each extra player, and a few more keep climbing aboard between calls.
- **Phase 2, the black sail.** At half his strength, he stops and hauls a black sail up her port side, between the deck and the Light.
  - From then on, **the beam never reaches the deck**, and only the flares light him.
  - The lockers reload quicker (**22 seconds**), so you'll want to take it in turns to fire them and keep him lit.
  - The Gannet **starts to founder**. She lists to starboard, more and more, so the horizon leans. The sea comes in over the waist and pushes you towards the low side, and her timbers groan.
  - Anyone who bled out gets back up as she starts to founder.
- **His pirate token.** When he falls, he leaves **the Captain's Doubloon** where he fell. Walk over it and it's yours, **one for each of you**. It's a pirate token like the Lighthouse's, spent on the Keeper's Last Light card. Anyone down gets back up when he falls, so everyone can take theirs. Once everyone standing has taken theirs (or after 45 seconds), the raid is won.
- **The victory screen:** as at the end of Maren's End, *Captain Crane goes down with his ship*. All of you stand together, with the MVP on the gold step. Two more things can come up under each player: **flares fired** and **times the bell rung**.
- **Together.** The host runs all of it: Crane, his crew, the lockers, the bell, the black sail and her foundering. Each screen works out for itself whether he's lit, from the same clock for the beam and the host's word for the flares. A hit that crossed the wire just as the light went off him still counts. A joined player asks the host to fire a flare or ring the bell, and everyone's hits on him add up.

## Fog Run (a voyage through the haar, alone or up to four)

A sixth game, on its own card on the title screen (**Set out**). On the fogbound nights of 1889 something out in the haar has been putting out the lights of the outer rocks, and you take the keeper's cutter out to light them again.

- **The chart.** The run follows a chart: seven islands across three reaches of sea (two in the Near Skerries, three in the Grey Reach, two in the Haar), with two or three to choose between at every stop, joined by branching ways, so the islands you pick now decide what you can reach later. As each sail begins the chart opens over the view: tap or click an island ahead (keys 1 to 3, or the D-pad and A on a controller; Esc or B puts it away). Each shows its kind and its rule, which island it is, and where it leads on to. Together, everyone votes: five seconds after the first vote it's settled (sooner once all of you have voted), the most votes wins and the host breaks a tie. Alone, your pick is the course. The cutter never waits: she sails on while you choose, and if nobody votes in twenty seconds, or she comes in sight of the islands, the chart picks one. The chart table on deck, port side aft of the hatch, has the route drawn on it; Use it to open the chart again.
- **At sea.** The cutter's deck, as in The Gannet's Wake: one of you at the helm, the rest on deck. Rocks show in the fog, the beam and the flares; hitting one stoves a hole to patch, and a full flood bar sinks her and ends the run. The bell and the flare locker work as there. The Light's beam is three times as wide here as in the other modes, so each sweep shows the rocks for three times as long. The fog cuts how far you see (55 m out in the Near Skerries, 38 m in the Grey Reach, 24 m in the Haar; each island lit pushes it back 5 m for the rest of the reach); past it is a solid wall of haar, with no sky and no far shapes, never how fast she sails.
- **The islands.** Each leg ends on an island's beach, and each island has a light to light (its beacon): light it, and its beam goes up through the fog, the fog draws back a little, and seven seconds later you're back aboard. Seven kinds, each with its mark on the chart:
  - *Fight* (crossed swords), one of three rules: **Hold the Light** (hold Use at the beacon four seconds to light it, then keep the drowned off it for a minute; if they crowd it, it goes out and you light it again), **Clear the Shore** (20, 30 or 40 drowned by the reach, half as many again for each of you after the first, up out of the shore in waves; send them all back, then light the beacon) and **the Crab Tide** (a swarm of crabs out of the water's edge while the tide comes in: every 15 s the beach shrinks by 4 m, and standing in the sea knocks you every second and a half; send back the swarm, then light the beacon, and the tide goes out).
  - *Elite* (a skull, never in the Near Skerries): one of the Lighthouse's bosses (the Diver, the Mooncusser or the Keelback), weaker than at the Lighthouse (60%, 80% and full strength by the reach), with the drowned. Put it down for a Captain's card in everyone's salvage.
  - *Puzzle* (a lens), built for working together but each can be done alone, and no fight unless you're slow or wrong: **Bend the Beam** (turn the mirror posts with Use, 45° a turn, to carry a lamp's beam post to post to the beacon through 4, 5 or 6 of them by the reach; 2 or 3 decoy posts stand off along the wrong ways, and every post is geared to another that turns a step with it, which glows blue while you stand at the first; the drowned come up every minute you take), **Knock's Garden** (carved figureheads that only move when nobody's looking at them; cross to the beacon with someone always watching, as a figurehead that reaches you takes half your health), **the Bell Buoys** (the fog horn plays a tune of 3, 4 or 5 notes; ring the island's bells in that order, and the horn post by the landing plays it again; a wrong note wakes the drowned) and **the Tide Stones** (the beacon stands cut off by water; hold all four stone plates down at once to raise the causeway: a plate stays down for a while after you step off it, longer the fewer of you there are, 16 s alone).
  - *Wreck* (an anchor): a wreck on the beach and her sea chest: hold Use at it and it gives each of you a gun (the wreck chest's odds) or, one time in four, reforges the gun in your hand. One wreck in three is guarded: something sleeps under the sand by the chest, and the chest won't open (nor the beacon light) until it's down.
  - *Chapel* (a candle, on one branch of the Grey Reach): Chapel Islet, a rest. Everyone's mended, everyone lost in the fog is brought back, and every extra life is given back.
  - *Unknown* (a question mark): you only learn which as you land: a stranger in a rowing boat who mends you and leaves you a tonic for 750 points (once each), a drowned man who asks you to ring his bell (it rises across the island: ring it within 30 s for a Captain's card in everyone's salvage), a sunk hoard in a deep pool (hold Use to dive, before your breath runs out, for what a wreck's chest would give, once each), a castaway by a fire (speak to him, then keep the drowned off him for 40 s: saved, he mends everyone, stows a spare plank aboard and leaves a Captain's card in everyone's salvage), the Dicing Man (throw once each: 7 or more wins what a wreck's chest would give, a double six pays twice, under 7 costs 300 points and a hit), the Keeper's Oil (four casks of lamp oil across the island: each one broached pushes the fog back 8 m for the rest of the reach, all four earn a Captain's card), the Wreckers' Gold (500 points a reach each, but every share taken wakes a rush of runners and the beacon won't light for 20 s), the Fog's Bargain (a standing stone takes all your points, 500 at the least, for a relic you haven't got, once each), a message in a bottle (2 chart fragments each to keep, and the first reader finds a fair wind for the next sail), or a fight.
  - **The Lost Man's Bell.** Together, once any of you is lost in the fog, an old ship's bell rises out of the ground somewhere on the island. Hold Use at it for five seconds and everyone lost comes back on their feet. The drowned are drawn to it while it's up.
- **Lives.** Alone, going down ends the run. Together, each of you has one extra life: the first time you bleed out you're back on your feet ten seconds later. After that you're lost in the fog, watching the others, until the crew lights the next beacon. The run ends when everyone is lost at once, or the cutter goes down.
- **Every run is different**: the legs, the rocks and the islands come from a seed drawn fresh for each run, the same for the whole crew.
- **Together**: the card's own lobby (host a run, join one, Share invite: `?mode=fogrun&lobby=...`), host-run like the raids, and you can rejoin a run in progress.
- **What you keep.** When a run ends, whether it's won, lost or left, you get a crate for every two islands lit: the same weapon and outfit crates as the raids, a weapon crate first. A win gives three more. Every island you light also gives you a chart fragment, kept for good in your browser and your save. At 10, 25, 50 and 100 in all, new cards join the salvage. The Fog Run card shows how many you have and the next mark. Fog Run has its own column on the scoreboard: the furthest island reached, the time through a won run, and the islands lit (in all, on your bests). A run left before its end gets the yellow asterisk (`src/frkeep.js`: `frFrags()`, `frFragTier()`).
- **The salvage.** After every island you light, each of you is dealt three cards of your own and keeps one (click it, or press 1, 2 or 3; a controller: left and right, then A). 20 seconds, then the left-hand card is kept for you. Three rarities, as the crates: Deckhand, Bosun and Captain; the rare ones turn up more the further out you are, and an elite island's hand always has a Captain's card. A hand never holds the same card twice or more than one crew card.
  - *Guns*: a gun from the wreck (any wall gun, Deckhand; any wreck-chest gun, Bosun) in place of one you carry; *Salvaged and Forged* (Captain), a chest gun already reforged once; the *Lampwright's Spark* (Captain), the gun in your hand reforged one tier; a *Rum Tot*, your ultimate and grenade ready now.
  - *Relics*, kept to the end of the run, shown as roundels beside your stamina (and back with you if you rejoin): Keeper's Oilcan (you see twice as far through the fog ashore), Gull Feather (jump again in mid-air, on top of the Mercenary's own), Drowned Man's Ring (each kill mends a quarter of a knock), Salt in the Wound (half as much again on the burning drowned), Plimsoll's Tooth (crabs never nip you), Brass Spyglass (rocks at sea stay in sight 2 s longer, for everyone), Powder Monkey (your grenade back in half the time), Bell Clapper (blade kills ring a bell that throws back everything near), Wrecker's Lamp (every fifth kill leaves a burning flare), Slow Match (your ultimate fills half as fast again), and a cork for each tonic (Fleet Foot, Stout Heart, Quick Hands, Grog Ration, kept for the whole run).
  - *Crew cards*, for everyone aboard (square roundels): Fresh Planks (the next rock only scrapes her), A Second Lamp (the flare locker reloads in half the time), Fair Wind (the next sail is half as long, never slower), The Haar Lifts (the next island's fog as the reach before's), Light the Way (the Light's beam sweeps the next island every 5 s for 40 s, burning the drowned), and together Call Them Home (the next time anyone's lost in the fog, they're straight back) and Maren's Ribbon (your extra life back).
  - *More cards* (the second salvage). ## Freebooters' Sea (an open sea of islands, alone or up to four)

A seventh game, on its own card on the title screen (**Set sail**). 1889: beyond the Near Skerries lies the Wide Water, a sea the charts gave up on. When the Gannet went down, a hundred years of wrecks came up with her and drifted out there, and their cargo washed up on every rock. The keeper's cutter is fitted with four swivel guns and sent out from the outpost of Saltcote to bring the salvage home before the Mooncussers' black sloops get to it, or the drowned take it back. There are no stages to clear and no end: you sail where you like, and leave when you like.

- **The Wide Water.** One sea, about three kilometres square, the same on every screen and every time: Saltcote at its south end and twelve islands across it (Gullstone, Widow's Teeth, St Brannoc's Isle, Tallow Cove, Beacon Knoll, Marrowfen, Crowsnest Rock, the Coffin Stones, Hangman's Holm, Gilt Strand, Mother Carey's Rest and Blackwater Holm), each in one of Wreckers' Night's looks, with reefs and loose rock between them, thicker the further north. Sailing is the Gannet's Wake deck: the helm, the holes and the flood, the bell and the flare locker, and the drowned over the rail out on the open water (never near Saltcote). She sails as fast as the Brig, always.
- **Going ashore.** The strip at the top of the screen marks the nearest island, with its name and how far off its shore. Come within reach of any island's beach and you can drop anchor (Use at the wheel, or at the capstan forward), and a moment later everyone's ashore on it. From the beach you can see the cutter lying at anchor offshore. Back aboard (Use at the landing boat, with nothing in your arms: everyone goes four seconds later), she lies where you left her, her head, holes, flood and hold as they were.
- **The islands.** Each is laid out the same at every visit: a landing with the boat drawn up and a lamp, ruins, rocks and wrecks, and what's lying about (a sea chest, better the further north the island, a trinket or two, now and then a cargo crate). What you leave is still there next time. The drowned come up out of the shore a few at a time, more the more of you there are.
- **Carrying and the hold.** Use picks a thing up and you carry it in your arms (no gun while you do; you walk as ever). Use again puts it down; going down drops it where you fall. Carry it to the landing boat and Use to stow it in the hold. The hold goes where the ship goes; if she sinks, it goes down with her and floats a while where she went down, and the crew washes up at Saltcote with a fresh cutter lying off the quay.
- **Selling.** At Saltcote, the factor's table by the landing (Use) buys the whole hold, and whatever you're carrying, for gold sovereigns: a battered sea chest 120, an ironbound chest 300, a gilded chest 650, a drowned captain's bell 450, a cargo crate 160, a strongbox 500, trinkets 25 to 60. The sovereigns go in the crew's purse, shared by everyone aboard; the purse and the hold are shown top right.
- **Ship's guns and the black sloops.** Four swivel guns on the cutter's rails, two a side: Use to man one, aim with the mouse or stick, fire; a few seconds to reload, and the ball arcs, holing what it hits. The Mooncussers' black sloops sail the Wide Water a few at a time, more the more of you there are: they chase what they see, fire broadsides that hole your deck as a rock does, and send the drowned climbing over the rail. Sink one (five planks of hits) and its loot floats: bring the cutter alongside and Use at the rail to haul it into the hold.
- **Voyages.** The voyage board on the quay at Saltcote offers work, one voyage at a time, each paying its company's standing: a **hoard chart** for Gilt & Gull Company (a chart names an island and draws an X by its landmarks: find the spot and hold Use with the spade to dig up a chest; two or three charts a voyage), a **bounty** for the Bellmen (a drowned captain holds a camp on an island: clear his drowned, put him down and bring home his bell), **salvage** for the Tidewrights (a wreck at sea, marked on the chart: anchor over it and dive, on one breath, for her crates). Take a notice with 1 to 3 or a click, give a voyage up at the board, and M (View on a controller, or the chart button on a phone) shows its charts. Sinking a black sloop pays no company, but her strongbox sells well. The chart table on deck shows the whole sea, your ship and your voyage's marks.
- **Lives.** Go down and you crawl, and someone can get you up, as in the raids. Bleed out (or, alone, go down at all) and you're back on your feet ten seconds later, aboard or at the landing. There's no run to lose.
- **What you keep.** Every sovereign the factor pays the crew is yours to keep: your card shows the sovereigns you've banked in all, your best session, your company standings and what your last session left you with, and the pause menu shows this session's purse, the next crate and all three standings. **Crates**, paid as they're earned: a weapon or outfit crate (a weapon crate first, then in turn) for every 1,000 sovereigns in a session's purse, and a **pirate token** (spent on the Keeper's Last Light card) for every company standing gained. Each company's **standing** goes from 1 to 10 by the voyages you've done for it (level 2 at the first, then at 3, 6, 10, 15, 21, 28, 36 and 45). Kept in your browser and your save, merged by taking the larger, and counted by the session's own id, so leaving and rejoining never pays twice.
- **The scoreboard.** Freebooters' Sea has its own column: each session, the sovereigns banked in it, the voyages done in it and the sovereigns you've banked in all. A session ends when you leave it, so it never gets the asterisk.
- **Together.** The card's own lobby (**Host a crew**, join one from the list, **Share invite**: `?mode=fbsea&lobby=...`); the host sets sail when everyone's aboard. Up to four on one ship, host-run like the raids: the host runs the ship, the sloops, the loot and the voyages, and everyone sees the same ship, hold and purse. You can rejoin a session in progress: your place, what was in your arms and your wait to be back on your feet come back with you, and if you're gone for good, whatever you were carrying is left at the landing.

How it's built: `src/fbsea.js` (the session, its stages, the loot, the hold, the factor, lives and the hook API), `src/fbsail.js` (the open sea, on src/gwsail.js through `GWS_LEG`), `src/fbisle.js` (the islands and Saltcote), `src/fbdress.js` (the sea's looks: the islands' silhouettes, Saltcote's light, the cutter at anchor seen from ashore), `src/fbkeep.js` (what you keep: sovereigns, standings, crates, its board, rejoin), on the raids' stage system (src/raid.js, `ZM_MAPS.fbsea`). `src/fbguns.js` (the swivel guns and the black sloops) and `src/fbvoy.js` (the voyage board, hoard charts and digging, bounties, salvage, the chart table) are built on its hooks (`fbHook`, `fbOn`).

## Skerry Links (nine holes of golf on the cliffs, alone or up to four)

An eighth game, on its own card on the title screen (**Tee off**; on the desk menu it's number 8). Before the lamp failed, the keepers laid out nine holes along the headland, and on a still evening you can still play them. The course is laid out after the front nine at Pebble Beach: the real pars (par 36), the yardages near enough, and the shape of each hole, drawn in the game's own look with names of its own.

| Hole | Name | Par | Yards | What it is |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The Gate | 4 | 377 | A dogleg right, gently uphill, a bunker in the corner |
| 2 | The Long Road | 5 | 511 | A long par 5 with a creek (the barranca) across the fairway short of the green |
| 3 | Down to the Cove | 4 | 404 | A dogleg left, running downhill towards the cove |
| 4 | First Sight of the Sea | 4 | 331 | A short par 4 with the sea and a low cliff all down the right; a small green |
| 5 | Cliff's Edge | 3 | 192 | A par 3 along the cliff top, the sea on the right |
| 6 | The Climb | 5 | 506 | Down off the tee, then a steep climb to a green on the headland; the sea on the right |
| 7 | The Point | 3 | 109 | A little downhill par 3 to a tiny green on the point, with the sea on three sides |
| 8 | The Chasm | 4 | 427 | Up onto the plateau off the tee, then the second shot over a sea-filled chasm to a small green |
| 9 | Homeward Cliff | 4 | 481 | A long par 4 along the cliff, the fairway leaning towards the sea |

- **On a phone.** The club box sits on the left and the swing meter on the right, above the Swing buttons and the Power bar, so the middle of the screen stays clear.
- **Before you tee off.** Players on this screen (1 to 4, taking turns, each with a name), which holes (all nine, or three at a time: 1 to 3, 4 to 6, 7 to 9), the wind (calm, breezy or blowing a gale) and the swing meter (normal, or gentle and slower). The choices are kept on this device. Settings opens the game's Settings card.
- **Clubs.** Driver (252 yards at full power), 3 wood (228), 5 wood (210), irons 4 to 9 (192 down to 133), pitching wedge (118), sand wedge (88) and the putter. Up and down (W and S, or the ▲ ▼ buttons, or the stick or D-pad) change club; the club box shows how far it carries at full power from where you lie. The game hands you a club for the distance to start with. From the rough a shot loses some distance, from deep rough more, from a bunker most (the sand wedge does best there), and the driver off the fairway a little.
- **Aim.** Left and right (A and D, the arrow keys, the ◀ ▶ buttons, or the stick) turn the dotted line; hold Shift to aim finely. The ring at its end is where a full shot comes down in still air. Off the tee of a dogleg you start aimed down the first leg. **M** (or the **Map** button, or X on a controller) shows the hole from above, and the map in the top right corner always shows the whole hole, your ball, the others' balls and your line.
- **Power.** The **Power** bar at the bottom right (above the Map and Card buttons; under the club and Swing buttons on a phone) sets how far you mean to hit it: drag it, or Z and X (5% a press), or LT and RT on a controller. It shows the percentage and the carry in yards (in feet for a putt). The game sets it for the distance to the pin each time you're up, change club or change the putt scale. It moves the green zone on the swing meter, the ring at the end of your line and the green crosshair on the map, so you can see where it comes down before you swing; stop the meter in the green zone to hit that distance. It's hidden once the swing starts. Stop the meter anywhere in the green zone and you hit exactly that power (the zone is wider on the putter). For a chip under 70% power the meter rescales so your power sits at 70% of the bar, and its marks show the real percentages.
- **The swing meter.** An arc at the bottom right. Press Space (or tap the screen or **Swing**, or A on a controller) to start the swing; the marker climbs. Press again to set the power (up to 100%, and the red beyond it is an overswing: a few more yards, but the strike becomes much harder to time). The marker swings back down: press once more as it crosses the white strike line. Right on it is a perfect strike; early hooks the ball left, late slices it right, and the further off, the more it bends. Missing the line altogether is a bad slice.
- **The wind.** The arrow by the hole's name shows which way it blows as you're looking, and how hard, in miles an hour. It's new on every hole. High, long shots feel it most; into the wind costs distance, downwind adds it, and a crosswind carries the ball sideways. The flag on the pin streams with it.
- **Watching it fly.** The camera follows the ball from high up behind and a little to one side, looking down on it, so you see the ball, its line and where it's coming down; the higher the ball goes, the further back and higher the camera sits.
- **The ball.** It flies with drag, the lift of its spin (which dies away), the wind and any side spin, then bounces and rolls by what it lands on: the fairway runs, the rough grabs, bunkers hold it, and the greens are quick. Wedges bite and stop; long clubs run on. Slopes take a rolling ball downhill, and a ball rolling off the top of a cliff goes over.
- **Putting.** On the green (or close by on the fringe) you have the putter. Its meter is a length, not a distance in yards: its scale (10, 20, 40 or 80 feet at full) is set to suit the putt, and Q and E (or the − + buttons, or LB and RB) change it. Small arrows on the green point downhill, longer and warmer in colour where it's steeper: uphill putts need more, downhill ones less, and putts across a slope break. Timing on a putt only pushes or pulls it a little. On the green the ball rolls out freely and eases to a stop, feels a little under half of the slope's pull, and is drawn in when it's slow and near the cup: a ball dying within about 11 inches creeps on and drops, and none comes to rest within 8 inches of it. The putter's meter runs slower, and a putt outside the green zone is only out by about half as much. Reach the cup slowly enough and it drops; too fast and it lips out.
- **Trouble.** The sea, the creek on the 2nd and anywhere off the course cost a stroke and a drop back where the ball went in, clear of the cliff edge. Trees catch the ball and drop it. Ten strokes on one hole and you pick up (scored as 10).
- **Slope.** On or near the green a **Slope** button (or H, or Back on a controller) lays a heat map of the green over it: blue where it's low, through green and yellow, to red where it's high, with a thin contour line every 2.5 cm of height and a bolder one every 10 cm. Lines close together mean steep; a ball breaks from the warm side to the cool side. A key under the distance to the pin shows the colours. It stays on (or off) from putt to putt and is kept on this device; bunkers are left uncovered.
- **The whole course.** The nine holes are laid out together on one map: the 1st to the 3rd run south down the inland side and the 4th to the 9th run north along the cliffs, each tee a little past the last green. From any hole the other eight show at low detail (a coarse coloured mesh of their fairways, rough, greens, tees and bunkers laid over the land, their trees and a flag on each green), wherever they fall outside the hole being played and on dry land. They're scenery only; a ball that goes out there is out of bounds.
- **Frame rate.** The 3D view watches its own frame time: every 1.5 s, if frames average slower than 50 a second it lowers its resolution a step (to no less than 0.6 of a pixel per screen pixel), and after a few checks running faster than 58 a second it raises it again. Phones and screens at 2x or more start at 1.25x with no antialiasing; others at up to 1.5x. The aim line and the HUD canvases (map, wind, meter) only redraw when what they show changes.
- **The light, and the Light.** The round runs through the day: midday on the 1st, the sun lower and warmer hole by hole, sunset on the 6th, and moonlight and stars on the 7th, 8th and 9th (the hole decides it, so the 7 to 9 set is always played at night). Skerry Light, white with two red bands, stands off in the distance from every hole, on its rock out at sea or, from the two inland holes, on a far hill. Its lamp is dark by day; from sunset it turns once every ten seconds, its single beam sweeping round with a flash as it comes towards you, and the keeper's cottage window is lit. On the holes by the sea a little harbour lies under the cliffs, in view from the tee where the coast allows: a stone quay, a curving breakwater, cottages, lamps (lit after dark) and two boats moored. Three ships come in from far out past the Light, then thread between the skerries lying across the straight line to the harbour, swinging out round each in turn, and on round the breakwater to the quay. A few more rocks lie round the Light's own rock.
- **Turns.** Everyone tees off in turn, the best score on the last hole first. After that, whoever is furthest from the hole plays next, until everyone has holed out.
- **The scorecard.** At the end of each hole it shows everyone's score for each hole against the par and yardage, with a circle for a birdie, two for an eagle or better, a box for a bogey and two for worse, and the total and score to par. Tab (or the **Card** button, or Y on a controller) shows it at any time. At the end of the round it says who won (or your score alone), and **Play again** starts another round with the same players.
- **The menu.** Esc, the ☰ button or Start: back to the hole, the scorecard, leave the round, and the volume slider.
- **Your best.** The card shows your best full round and how many you've played, kept on this device, and its own **Best rounds** list: the top five rounds from the scoreboard.
- **The scoreboard.** Skerry Links has its own column on the scoreboard: each full round of nine, ranked by score (lowest first), with the holes played and the birdies (or better). A round left before the ninth hole is logged with its holes and an asterisk. Only full rounds of nine count; three-hole rounds don't go on the board. With more than one player on one screen, the board takes the first player's round.
- **Together, online.** The card's own lobby (**Host a round**, join one from the list, **Share invite**: `?mode=golf&lobby=...`); the host tees off with everyone who's in, and the host's choice of holes, wind and meter. Up to four, each on their own screen and each swinging on their own go; everyone watches every shot. The host's screen keeps the round. A player who drops out for ten seconds is marked as left (their card stands). If the host leaves, the round ends.
- **Your golfer, ball and clubs.** Your golfer wears the outfit you've chosen from the Tailor's Crate (**Outfit crates** on the Wreckers' Night card): the coat, its trim and lapels, the sash, the buttons, the breeches and boots and the outfit's own hat, stooped over the ball, shimmering, glowing or alight just as on the island. Without one it's the plain golfer in your player's colour. **Ball crates** and **Club crates** on the card open the crates below. On one screen with others, the first player plays with your things and the rest in their colours; online, everyone plays with their own and everyone sees them.
- **Ball and club crates** (`src/gfcrates.js`). Two crates on one card, with the same grades, odds and reel as the other crates. The **Chandler's Crate** holds a ball: 5 Deckhand (Gull's Egg, Harbour Buoy, Oilskin Yellow, Sea-Glass Green, Tarred Rope), 4 Bosun that leave a coloured trail (Ship's Compass, Signal Flags, Glass Net Float, Mackerel), 3 Mate whose paint moves and shimmers and which throw sparks in flight (Moon Pearl, Phosphor Tide, Northern Lights), 3 Captain that glow, pulsing, with a halo (Ember Isle, The Corpse Light, Storm Glass), and 3 Admiral, alive: **The Skerry Light** (cream and gold filigree with the lighthouse's beam and its fainter twin sweeping round the ball as it flies), **The Maelstrom** (a whirlpool of blue and violet turning on it) and **Verity's Grin** (his own round yellow face, blinking up at you and grinning wherever it lies). The ball turns as it rolls and flies. The **Shipwright's Crate** holds a set of clubs made from a ship's gear (every club in the bag, putter too): 4 Deckhand (Driftwood Oar, Belaying Pin, Boat-Hook, Rope-Bound Anchor), 3 Bosun (Brass Anchor, Whalebone, Whaler's Harpoon), 2 Mate that shimmer (Narwhal Tusk, Sea-Glass Oar), 3 Captain that glow (Ember Harpoon, Corpse-Light Lantern, Kraken's Arm) and 2 Admiral: **The Keeper's Beam** (a little lighthouse for a head, its lamp lit and its beam turning) and **The Drowned Trident** (green fire and bubbles rising off its prongs). ## Skerry League (ships and a ball, one to three a side)

An eleventh game, on its own card on the title screen (**Kick off**; on the desk menu it's number 11). Down in the Harbour Pool, a walled basin of still water between two lighthouses, two sides of ships, Blue and Orange, try to knock a great striped buoy into each other's goal. You sail for Blue, with bots on your side and against you.

- **On the card:** ships a side (1 against 1, 2 against 2 or 3 against 3), the length of the match (3, 5 or 7 minutes), how good the bots are (Easy, Fair or Hard), your ship (the Brig, the Cutter, the Barque or the Carrier, in the skin you have on her), and whether the others sail any ship or the same as yours. Your record (won, drawn, lost, and your goals) is shown under the buttons and kept on this browser.
- **Sailing.** Your ship handles as she does on a voyage: W (or ↑) makes sail and it keeps climbing the longer you hold it, with no top speed; S (or ↓) shortens it. Keep holding S with the sail already short and she backs her sails and goes astern. A and D (or ← →) steer. Each ship keeps her own feel (the Cutter is quickest to answer, the Carrier the heaviest), and every ship turns more sharply in the pool than at sea. Hitting a wall knocks some way off her, as a rock does at sea.
- **The Gale.** Shift or Space blows her on much faster for as long as the gauge (bottom right) lasts. The yellow gale buoys on the water fill it: the small ones by a third, the big ones (in the four corners and at the middle of each side) right up. A buoy comes back a few seconds after it's taken.
- **The ball.** It floats, slows on the water, and bounces off the walls, the posts and the crossbar. The faster you hit it, the harder it flies, and a hard knock lifts it out of the water; a heavy ship moves it more and is moved less. It's a goal when the whole ball is over the line, under the crossbar. The bell rings and the lighthouse behind the goal flashes, and everyone goes back to their marks for a count of three.
- **The match.** The clock at the top counts down. Ahead when it runs out and you've won; level, and it's overtime, where the next goal wins (the clock counts up in gold). The card at the end shows the score and each ship's goals and touches.
- **The camera.** Ball cam (the default) sits behind your ship and keeps the ball in view; Ship cam looks the way she's heading. C (or the button top left) swaps them, and the game remembers which you like. The chart at the top right shows the pool, every ship and the ball.
- **The bots.** On each side, the ship best placed goes for the ball and lines up behind it on the line to the goal; with more than one, another keeps goal and any third waits behind the play. Your bot team-mates count you in: if you're closer, they leave it to you. Easy bots are slower and see less of where the ball is going; Hard ones go flat out and use the Gale.
- **Controller:** left stick or D-pad steers, RT makes sail, LT shortens it, A or B the Gale, Y the camera, Start the menu. **Phone:** steer with the buttons bottom left; shorten, the Gale and make sail bottom right.
- **Esc** (or ☰) pauses: back to the match, start again, or leave.

How it's built: `src/league.js` (the match, the ships' handling, the ball, the walls and goals, the bots, the screen and the controls) and `src/league3d.js` (the 3D view: the pool, the walls and lamps, the cliffs, the goals and lighthouses, the ships built from their boats' shapes and skins, the wakes, the Gale's spray, the goal bursts and the camera). Online play is `src/lgnet.js`: a lobby on the card for up to six, seated by side with bots in the empty seats; each screen sails its own ship, and the host's screen runs the ball, the bots, the gale buoys, the clock and the score and sends them to the rest, who forecast the ball between snapshots and send their own hits for the host to check. `server/server.js` relays one match's players to each other at full rate (the `lg` room key).

### Spindrift (Skerry League side-on, one or two a side)

The League's second way to play, picked on the same card: set **Game** to **Spindrift (side-on)** and press Kick off (or host it online). It's played in the Sluice, the old tide-lock under the Light, seen side-on as if the near wall were cut away: a vaulted stone chamber half full of sea, with a lock gate for a goal in each end wall. Blue's gate is on the left, Orange's on the right. One against one or two against two, three, five or seven minutes, the same bots, ships and skins as the Harbour Pool.

- **On the water.** A and D (or ← →) sail her left and right; she turns about at once. Every ship reaches the same speed on the water; a handier one (the Cutter) gets there sooner.
- **Leaping.** W, ↑ or Space leaps her out of the water. Press it again in the air within a moment and a half and she flips the way you're holding: A or D for a flip forwards or back, S (↓) to dive. A ship that strikes the ball mid-flip hits it much harder. Hold nothing and the second press is a plain second jump.
- **In the air.** A and D turn her over. She always lands the right way up, rolling over if she comes down on her deck.
- **The Gale.** Shift (or K) drives her the way her bow points, in the air as well as on the water, so point her bow up and she flies. The gauge refills from the yellow gale buoys floating on the water: small ones a third, the big ones by the walls all of it.
- **The ball.** The same striped buoy. It floats, bobs back up if it's knocked under, and bounces off the walls, the vault and the gate lintels. A goal is the whole ball past the gate's line, under its lintel; the lamp over that gate blazes.
- **The bots.** The nearer of a side goes for the ball from behind it, leaps at a high one and flips into it, and (Hard) flies at it on the Gale; with two a side the other keeps the gate.
- **Online.** In the League's lobby the host's Game pick decides what's played; a Spindrift match takes four people, and the lobby list marks it. Everything else (sides, bots in empty seats, rejoining, the host running the match) is as in the Harbour Pool.
- **Controller:** left stick steers and turns her, A leaps and flips, B, X or RT the Gale, the stick held down to dive. On a phone: ◀ ▶ to sail and turn, ▲ to leap and flip, ▼ to dive, and Gale.
- The card keeps a separate Spindrift record (and a separate one online).

How it's built: `src/lgside.js` (the rules: the ships, the ball, the stone, the buoys, the bots, and the hooks the online code uses) and `src/lgside2d.js` (the view: a 2D canvas of its own). Everything else (the phases, the clock, the score, the end card, the lobby) is the Harbour Pool's, in `src/league.js` and `src/lgnet.js`, which hand over to these when `LG.side` is set. Debug: `lgDebug({ game: 'side', size: 2 })`, `lgDebugGoal(0)`.

## Skerry Chess (chess, and chess on the reef, for one or two)

A twelfth game, on its own card on the title screen (**Play the bot**; on the desk menu it's number 12). The keepers' game, played in the lamp room on long nights: standard chess with every rule, or Skerry rules, a variant on a reef. Play one of three bots, or one other person online.

- **On the card:** the rules (Standard chess or Skerry rules), the bot (Easy, Fair or Hard), and the colour you play (White, Black, or either at random). Your record against each bot and online, for each set of rules, is shown under the buttons. A game against a bot that you leave is kept, and **Resume your game** picks it up.
- **Moving.** Tap or click a piece and its moves are marked (a dot for a move, a ring for a capture); tap where it goes. Or drag it there. A pawn reaching the far side asks what it becomes. The last move is shaded, and a king in check glows red.
- **Standard chess**, every rule as in a tournament: castling (not out of, through or into check, and only with the king and that rook unmoved), en passant, promotion to a queen, rook, bishop or knight, check, checkmate and stalemate. A game is drawn at once by stalemate, by the same position coming up a third time (same pieces, same side to move, same castling and en passant chances), by fifty moves each with no capture and no pawn move, or when neither side has enough left to checkmate (king against king, king and bishop or knight against king, or kings and bishops all on one colour of square).
- **Skerry rules.** The Light (cream and brass) plays the Wreckers (tar and red); the Light moves first. The armies start where the standard pieces do, but every piece is the station's own, with a move or a power of its own (a guide to them, **The pieces**, sits beside the board):
  - **Keeper** (king's place): one step in any direction, or swaps places with one of its own hands next to it (not if that would put the hand on its own far rank). No castling. Written `K~d2` for a swap.
  - **Lantern** (queen's place): sails any distance diagonally, or leaps as a knight does.
  - **Tower** (rook's place): a lighthouse. It walks one step in any direction onto an empty square, and takes with its **beam**: the first enemy piece along its rank or file, at any distance, without moving (a flash of light shows it). Rocks and pieces block the beam, and a tower gives check along its lines. Written `T*e5`.
  - **Cutter** (bishop's place): sails any distance diagonally, or tacks one step straight, so it isn't tied to one colour.
  - **Gannet** (knight's place): dives exactly three squares in a straight line or diagonal, over anything in the way (rocks too), or takes one step straight.
  - **Hand** (pawn's place): one step forward, or one step sideways onto an empty square; takes one step diagonally forward. No double step, so no en passant. On the far rank it becomes a lantern, tower, cutter or gannet.
  - **The reef:** rocks stand at c4, f4, c5 and f5. Nothing may stand on a rock or move or shine through one; only gannets dive over them.
  - **The Light:** the four centre squares (d4, e4, d5, e5) glow gold. A keeper that ends its move on one, by a step or a swap, wins outright. It still can't move into check, so the Light must be safe to step on. Other pieces may stand on the Light and block it.
  - **No dead draws:** a lone keeper can always make for the Light, so there's no draw for insufficient material. Stalemate, threefold repetition and the fifty-move rule still draw.
  - Notation under Skerry rules uses K, L, T, C and G for the keeper, lantern, tower, cutter and gannet (hands have no letter).
- **The bots.** Hob the deckhand (Easy) looks about one move ahead, with plenty of guesswork and the odd careless move, but never misses a mate in one. Mate Tamsin (Fair) looks three moves deep with a little guesswork. The Old Keeper (Hard) thinks for up to two seconds a move, searching as deep as he can, and plays his best every time. All three know Skerry rules, the reef and the Light. **Take back** undoes your last move and the bot's reply.
- **Online, one against one.** **Set up a board** and the other player joins from the card's list or from the invite you share (**Share invite**). The one who set it up picks the rules and their colour on the card, and presses **Start the game**. Each of you plays on your own screen. **Offer a draw** (the other sees **Accept their draw**; making a move withdraws it) or **Resign**. At the end, **Rematch** goes back to the board and starts again with the colours swapped once you've both asked. If your opponent closes the game or isn't heard from for 45 seconds, you win; if your page reloads mid-game, open the card and tap your board to go back to it.
- **Keys:** the arrow keys move a cursor round the board, Enter or Space picks up and puts down, Esc lets go of a piece or opens the menu, F turns the board round. **Controller:** D-pad or left stick moves the cursor, A picks up and puts down, B lets go, Y turns the board, Start opens the menu. **Turn board** does the same on screen.
- **Esc** (or ☰) opens the menu: back to the game, resign, or leave (against a bot the game is kept; online, leaving resigns).

How it's built: `src/chess.js` (the rules: the board, every move and the draws, the notation, and the bots' search and evaluation), `src/chessui.js` (the board on the screen with its two sets of pieces drawn in SVG, the card, the game against a bot, the keys and controller, the sounds) and `src/chnet.js` (online games, on the presence key `ch`: the lobby, and each screen's moves sent as the whole game so far and checked on the other screen; `server/server.js` relays one board's two players to each other at once). For tests, `chDebug({ v, opp, me })` starts a game, `chDebugMoves(['e2e4', ...])` plays moves, `CHS` is the state, and `chPerft(chNew('std'), 4)` counts positions (197281 from the start, as it should).

## Skerry Clash (a duel of lights, one against one)

A ninth game, on its own card on the title screen (**Duel the bot**, **Triple draft** to build a deck on the spot, or **First person** to fight as your own troops while bots play the cards; on the desk menu it's number 9). Two keepers on two rocks face each other across a tidal race, each sure the other's light is the false one that puts ships on the reef. They settle it in three minutes: crews, crabs, gulls and worse sent over the causeways, spells cast from the rocks, and lamp oil for all of it. Fell the other keeper's towers before they fell yours. Everything in it, the cards, the towers and the pictures, is drawn in the game's own world: keepers, wreckers, the drowned, the Diver, the Mooncusser, the Keelback, the haar.

- **The field.** Your rock is at the bottom of the screen and theirs at the top, whichever side you are; the race runs across the middle and two causeways cross it, one in each lane. Each side has its **Great Light** at the back in the middle and two **Lamp Towers** forward, one in each lane. The Lamp Towers shoot anything in range, on the ground or in the air.
- **Lamp oil.** Every card costs oil, shown in the drop on its corner. You start with 5 and gain one every 2.8 seconds, up to 10; oil past 10 is wasted.
- **Your hand.** You hold four cards from your deck of eight, with the next one shown beside them. Playing a card sends it to the back of the queue, so the eight come round in turn.
- **Playing a card.** Pick it, then tap where it goes. Troops and buildings go down on your own half, on dry rock: not on the water, not on a tower. When one of their Lamp Towers falls, the front of their rock in that lane opens to you as well. Spells go anywhere.
- **How troops move.** On foot they walk to the nearer causeway, cross, and go for the nearest thing they can hit within about five tiles; with nothing near, for the next tower down their lane. Some (the Powder Monkey, the Hermit Crab, the Crab King, the Crab Mother, the Rocket Crew) go only for buildings and towers. Flyers (the Gull Flock, the Puffin Squadron, the Cormorant, the Gannet, the Storm Petrel, the Sea Eagle, the Will-o'-the-Wisp) go straight over the water, and only what can hit the air can touch them: towers, spells, and the cards that go for anything (the Harpooner, the Coastguard Pair, the Lamplighter, the Head Keeper, the Fishwives, the Siren, the Stormcaller, the Swivel Gun, the Martello Tower, the Burning Lens, and the Gulls, Puffins, Petrel, Cormorant and Sea Eagle themselves). Troops jostle and push apart; buildings wear down over their life.
- **Champions.** Three champions sit beside the 64 cards, framed in gold at the front of the deck builder (they have their own filter). A deck may hold **one** champion, in one of its eight places; to put a second one in you swap it for the first. Play a champion like any troop, but while it's on the field its card stays out of your hand, and comes back into the queue only once it falls. While it stands, a round button over the right end of your hand (or **Q**) uses its **ability** for a little oil; then the button sweeps round while the ability comes back. It glows when it's ready.

- **Crowns.** Every tower you fell is a crown. Fell the Great Light and you take all three at once and the match ends there.
- **The Great Light wakes.** It sleeps until it's hit or one of its own Lamp Towers falls, then it wakes and shoots as well, harder than a lamp.

- **The other keeper.** Against the bot, choose how good it is on the card: Easy, Normal or Hard. - **Your deck** (`src/rldeck.js`, **Deck** on the card). Three deck slots, Deck 1 to 3, each eight different cards; the slot you last chose is the one you play, and each starts as the starter deck (Deckhand, Shore Crabs, Harpooner, Gull Flock, The Diver, Hermit Crab, Cannon Shot, Sea Battery). No card is offered to you twice in one draft, and champions are never offered. Each round has a theme, shown under the round number, so every drafted deck can hold its own: a big hitter, two rounds of things that hit flyers, a spell that strikes, two cheap troops, a building or a spell, and one where anything goes, in a shuffled order. Your picks fill the eight slots along the bottom with their average oil; tap a card or press 1, 2 or 3 to take it, Escape to leave. Against the bot there's no clock, and the bot drafts from its own offers (the ones you see under yours); **Again** on the end card drafts afresh. Online each pick has 15 seconds (a card is taken for you at random when it runs out), you can see how far your rival has got, and the duel starts the moment both decks are done. A drafted duel is rated, recorded and earns crates like any other. If either keeper leaves the draft it's called off and both go back to the lobby.

Whatever you raise, your side's colour (blue or red) stays on the plinth ring, the gallery rail, the door and the pennant, so the two sides always read apart. **Towers** opens the crate card at your collection, where you choose which to raise. - **Your record.** The card keeps your duels played, won, lost and drawn, crowns taken, your current run of wins and your best. Decks, record, crates and towers are kept in this browser (`skerry-rival`) and in your save, with everything else.
- **Together, online.** The card's own lobby: host a duel, join one from the list, or share an invite. Two keepers, one on each rock. Each keeper's rating shows beside their name in the lobby and the list of duels.
- **Watching.** Any duel online can be watched, waiting or under way: **Watch** beside it in the list of duels (an invite to a duel that's full or started offers Watch instead of turning you away). Buildings can't be held, and the spells are the bot's.
  - *Controls.* Click the view to look round with the mouse (Esc frees the pointer), **WASD** walk (the arrow keys walk and turn), **Space** or the left button strikes, **Q** uses your champion's ability. A controller: left stick walk, right stick look, **RT** or **A** strike, **LB**/**RB** the troop before or after, **Y** the newest, **B** let go, **X** the ability. On a touch screen: drag on the left half for a stick, drag on the right half to look, and hold **Strike**.
  - *Fighting as a troop.* A held troop does nothing on its own: it walks where you walk (a bit quicker than it would by itself, 1.6 times its pace; walkers still can't cross the race except by a causeway, and towers are in the way) and strikes only while you hold strike, at its own rhythm, whatever it can hit that's in reach in front of you (a wide arc for the ones that strike up close, a narrower one for the shooters, which still send their shot). Under the crosshair is what you're aiming at and its health; the crosshair turns gold when it's in reach. Everything about the troop is as on its card: the Ship's Cat pounces when you strike at something just out of reach, the Keelback charges once you've walked two tiles, the Surgeon mends all round it wherever you walk it, flyers fly over the water. Bottom left: your troop's card and health. Top right: a map of the race, your rock at the bottom, with you and the way you're looking.
  - *Three keepers* (v225): **Three keepers, first person** on the card, or tick **First person** in a match for three online. The same, on the round skerry: a bot plays every keeper's cards (against the bots, theirs at the level on the card), each keeper fights as their own troops, the map is round with your rock at the bottom, and a name plate in each rival's colour floats over the troop they hold. A bot takes any empty seat online, as ever.
  - *Online*, two keepers, one on each rock: the host's screen runs both bots and the match, the guest's moves go to the host about ten to twenty times a second, and a name plate floats over the troop your rival holds. Watchers see the board from above with both held troops marked on it. First person matches give crates and count in your record, but aren't rated.
- **Three keepers** (`src/rl3.js`, **Three keepers** on the card; online, **Host three keepers**, or tick **Three keepers** in the lobby as the host). Every keeper for themselves: one round skerry cut three ways by channels running out from a pool in the middle, each keeper's rock between two of them, and a causeway over each channel to the next rock. You're always at the bottom in blue; the keeper round to your left plays in red and the one to your right in gold, each with their own plate up top (name, oil and towers standing). Each keeper has a Great Light at the back of their rock and two Lamp Towers forward, one beside each causeway, and the towers have four fifths of a duel tower's health, since everyone's oil goes two ways. You play cards on your own rock as in a duel; a fallen Lamp Tower opens that bit of the owner's rock to whoever's next to it. Troops cross the nearer causeway and go for that neighbour's towers, and once a troop picks a rival it sticks with them. Every tower you fell is a crown, to whoever struck last. Lose your Great Light and you're out: your other towers and all your troops fall with it and the other two fight on. The last Great Light burning wins. At time, the keeper with the most towers standing wins; level, a minute of overtime, and still level, the one with more health left across their towers. Against the bots, two of them take the other rocks at the difficulty chosen on the card, and **Again** on the end card plays another. Online, up to three can join a Three keepers room, from the list or an invite, and a bot takes any empty seat when the host starts; a guest who leaves or drops is out, as if their Great Light had fallen, and if the host leaves the match ends unrecorded. Watchers can watch it (**Swap ends** turns through all three). Emotes work as in a duel, over each keeper's Great Light, and **Mute rival** mutes both rivals. The end card shows the finishing order. It earns tower crates (one for finishing, one more for a win) but isn't rated and doesn't count in your duel record.
- **Rating (MMR)** (`src/rlmmr.js`, and `server/scores.js` on the game's own site). Every finished match moves your rating, which starts at 1000: up for a win and down for a loss, by more when the odds were against you (Elo). A duel online counts in full (K 32, against the other keeper's rating); a match against the bot counts for half as much (K 16), against a rating of the bot's own: Easy 800, Normal 1100, Hard 1300. A draw is half a win, and nobody goes below 0. The card shows yours under your record ("MMR 1034 (best 1080)"), the end card shows the change ("+16 MMR"), and the scoreboard has a **Skerry Clash** board of everyone's. The rating belongs to your username, the one in the name boxes, and to nothing else: play under that name on any device, with any save, and every match moves the same rating. On the game's own site a device that's never played under the name shows its real rating as soon as the name's typed (the page asks the server for it, `GET /api/scores?mmr=<name>`, when it opens, when the name changes and whenever the board is read). Rename yourself and you move to that name's rating (from 1000 if it's new); the old name keeps its own. On the game's own site the server keeps the ratings, and a duel online is rated only when both keepers' screens have reported the same result, so neither can rate the other alone; the end card says "once they're in" until then. Leave a duel before its end and it counts as a loss (and a win for the one who stayed); a duel cut off by a lost connection isn't rated. Against the bot, one result a minute counts. On claude.ai, with no server, each keeper's rating is worked out on their own screen (from the other's rating as their lobby gave it) and kept in their own board document, like the other boards there. Your rating is in your save too.

How it's built: `src/rlfp.js` (First person), `src/rlcards.js` (the 64 cards and the starter deck), `src/rlart.js` (every card's portrait, painted in canvas with no image files, and the card faces), `src/rldeck.js` (the three decks, the deck builder and the save) and `src/rlcrates.js` (tower skins, the 3D towers for the match, the Lampwright's Crate, your record and the card's buttons).

## The Chronicle of the Light (a campaign, alone or up to four)

The tenth game, on its own card on the title screen (**The Chronicle of the Light**, marked New). It plays the Skerries' timeline from the lore compendium as a story: eighteen missions, one for each key moment, from the first fire on Lantern Rock to the war on the Drowned Reach. Every mission has an opening and a closing cinematic and more along the way, spoken by the people who were there.

- **The missions**, in five acts:
  - *Act I, Before the Light:* **The First Fire** (c. 1200s, Lantern Rock: gather driftwood, keep the fire-keepers' beacon through the haar and against what comes out of it; as the first mission its fire is forgiving: two bundles and one for each of you to light it, slow to burn down, hard for the fog or the drowned to put out, and long on embers) and **The Mooncussers** (the 1600s: put out the wreckers' false lights before a merchantman strikes, and take their master alive).
  - *Act II, The Light:* **Lighting the Light** (1841: carry the Lampwright's lens up the new tower and light it for the first time) **The Nine Holes** (c. 1883, from Skerry Links: lay out the course on the headland with the keepers, strike the one shared ball hole by hole as the round goes on into the night, keep it out of the drowned's hands at the Chasm, light the cliff barrels with burning balls to turn a brig off the point, and put down the Chasm Crab), **The Harbour Pool** (c. 1885, from Skerry League: win a summer evening's match against the brig Patience with the keeper's cutter and the Gale in a bottle, then shove the old buoy out through the fog to the Hound's mooring before the packet comes in, and ram Kittow's lugger, the Rook, and fight him on your own deck), **The Reef Board** (c. 1886, from Skerry Chess: keep the lamp through the middle watch, then play Holm's reef board as one of its pieces, moving only as Skerry rules lets it, light the four gold squares, shoot out the Wreckers' keeper's false light and see Holm onto the Light) and **The Night of the Gannet** (14 December 1887: keep the beam on the brig as she comes in, until the lamp fails).
  - *Act III, The Strange Year (1888):* **The Wren Comes In** (sail behind Plimsoll through the deep water), **The Kittiwake** (the first account of Verity, aboard a fishing smack off the Near Skerries), **Wreckers' Night** (a dark night on the Bonded Quay with the Receiver of Wreck's crew) and **The Gannet's Wake** (a year to the night, out to the risen brig by flarelight; in the ring of her spars everyone is handed a flare pistol, slot 3, to light her four tall spars, each a big target, and the cutter's flare locker always lights one of them).
  - *Act IV, After the Gannet:* **Keeper's Last Light** (hold the station through the winter's worst nights, from the yard to the lantern gallery), **Freebooters' Sea** (sail a Gilt & Gull chart to Crowsnest Rock and bring the hoard home), **Fog Run** (light the outer rocks again and find what's putting them out), **Maren's End** (the Dry Night of 1890, across the bed of the sea to the fort) and **The Fall of Wrackhaven** (get the townsfolk to the Moot Hall as the sea comes up Fish Street, and hold the Belfry until dawn).
  - *Act V, The Years After:* **The Drowned Reach**, the finale: cross the Reach at low water, take the Light's rock and light it. Then **Slack Water** (from Skerry Clash: the night a second light is lit across the Race. Light your Lamp Towers and hold your rock through the first slack water, cross the causeways as the tide uncovers them to fell his towers for crowns, and face the champion who crosses for him, all before the tide turns).
- **Each mission is its own.** They borrow the places, people and creatures of the other games, but each has its own ground (often a part of a known place you haven't walked before), its own goals and its own fights: carrying, holding, escorting, sailing, puzzles and a boss to finish. A mission has two to four parts and takes about ten to fifteen minutes. The objective is shown at the top of the screen and a gold marker shows where to go, held at the edge of the screen when it's behind you.
- **Cinematics** can be skipped by holding Space or Enter (A on a controller, or a finger on the screen) for a second; a ring in the corner fills as you hold, and a tap does nothing. Together, holding is a vote: the cinematic skips only once every one of you has voted, and the corner shows who has. Your mouse stays held while one plays (clicks do nothing), so Escape won't knock you out of full screen and you can play on the moment it ends.
- **Maren's End, the Old Drain.** One of you carries Maren's Lantern (anyone can take it up if it falls). Any of you can light a brazier while the lantern is near it, and any of you can turn the nine lamps in the Cistern Hall. Once anyone reaches the hall, the drowned stop coming, so the lamps can be worked out in peace.
- **Voices, music and sound.** Every line is spoken, each character with a voice of their own that stays the same from mission to mission, and subtitled for as long as it's said. The campaign has its own themes (tense nights, sailing, fighting, bosses, sorrow, fog, hope and victory, and a few moments of its own), and otherwise borrows the other games' music. Its fires, winches, storms, ships and floods are real recordings. A mission's sounds load as you start it.
- **Progress** is kept in your browser and in your save code. Missions open in order: finish one to open the next, and any mission you've finished can be played again. The four tales of the harbour games (The Nine Holes, The Harbour Pool, The Reef Board and Slack Water) open with the mission before them but never hold the next one shut, so they can be played whenever they're open. The card shows each mission's best time and how many times you've finished it. Finishing a mission for the first time gives a weapon crate and an outfit crate.
- **Callings.** The campaign has four callings of its own, picked over the view as each mission starts (the picker remembers your last). Each has two weapons, a trait that's always on, its own jump in mid-air, a grenade (G; LB on a controller) that comes back after a while, and an ultimate (X; RB or up on the D-pad) that fills as you fight and faster with kills. Nothing starts, not the opening cutscene, the clock or the drowned, until every one of you has picked (those who have see who's still choosing; after 90 seconds it starts anyway, and whoever's still at the picker is safe there until they choose). Together each of you picks your own, and everyone sees what the others throw and set down; a crew is strongest with one of each, but any of them can carry a mission alone. Nothing here touches the boats.
  - *Keeper* (Fresnel Lamp, Pepperbox; in the campaign the lamp burns for 70% less than elsewhere, about a gun's worth): **Lantern-Bearer**, you and friends within 9 m mend a knock every five seconds. **Lamp Step**, jump again in the air and a ring of light bursts under you, lighting them (lit, they're hurt half again as hard by anyone) and pushing them off. **Storm Lantern**, it stands where it lands for twelve seconds and everything within 7 m is lit, slowed and scorched. **Rekindle**, a flash 14 m round you that burns and hurls back every one of them, mends every friend in it to full and gets the fallen up.
  - *Wrecker* (Punt Gun, Cutlass): **Hard as Nails**, every knock on you lands a quarter lighter. **Lunge**, jump again in the air to hurl yourself forward through them. **Grapple-Chain**, everything within 8 m of where it lands is dragged into a heap, and then it bursts. **Wreckers' Fury**, twelve seconds when every shot is a wide cleave that throws them back, knocks are halved, and each one dropped mends a knock.
  - *Riding Officer* (Longarm, Pepperbox): **Dead Eye**, head shots hit a third harder and every gun loads a quarter quicker. **Hang Fire**, jump again in the air to hang there steady for up to two seconds (jump again to drop). **Rescue Rocket**, flies fast and nearly flat and bursts where it strikes, lighting what it doesn't kill. **Rocket Barrage**, twelve rockets fall over four seconds about the spot you're looking at.
  - *Lampwright* (Avtomat, Harpoon gun): **Gunsmith**, your guns hit 15% harder. **Grapnel Line**, jump again in the air to fire a line at what you're looking at (up to 22 m) and be hauled to it. **Lens Sentry**, a brass lamp on a tripod that burns the nearest of them within 16 m for fourteen seconds. **The Great Lens**, for sixteen seconds a great lens burns three at a time within 20 m and slows them, and friends within 6 m of it take half the knocks.
  - The picker shows how many missions you've seen through with each calling; that's kept in your save code too, and merged, never replaced.
- **Lives.** Each of you can bleed out once and be back on your feet ten seconds later. After that you watch until the mission ends. If a mission fails past its first part, **Try again from part ...** picks up where you reached (together, the host's next start does). After a win alone, **Next** goes straight on to the next mission.
- **Together**: the card's own lobby (host a mission, join one, Share invite: `?mode=campaign&lobby=...`), run by the host like the raids. The host picks the mission from those the host has opened, and the fights grow with the size of the crew.

## Wreckers' Night (multiplayer FPS)

A separate game with its own card on the title screen, below Skerry Light (a banner at the top of the title screen points to it). The card has the weapon picker (the pepperbox now, four slots waiting for more weapons), Go ashore, Settings, your name, who's on the island, and how to play. You go ashore on foot on a skerry at night with a six-shot pepperbox pistol and a lantern, and shoot it out with everyone else on the island. It never touches your voyages, crates, cosmetics or crew; your equipped skin only colours your coat and lantern.

- **Light:** you only see what light reaches. The lighthouse beam sweeps the island and shows anyone it crosses (a warning tells you when you're in it). Your lantern (L) lights what's near you, and shows you to the whole island while it burns. Flares (F, two a life) light a wide circle for everyone.
- **The bell** (B, or LB on a controller): everyone shows on your bearing strip for a few seconds, and you show on theirs.
- **Five weapons**, all carried (1-6, Q for the last one, the mouse wheel, Y or the d-pad left and right on a controller, the weapon button on a phone; V or R3 draws the cutlass); the one picked on the title card is in hand when you land:
  - **Pepperbox:** six quick shots, one damage (two to the head). Its barrels turn a place each shot; reloading swaps the whole barrel cluster.
  - **Blunderbuss:** one shot of nine balls in a wide spread: deadly close, harmless past fifty metres. A flintlock: the cock snaps onto the frizzen and the pan flashes before it roars. Reloading: powder from the horn, a handful of shot, two strokes of the ramrod, prime, shut the frizzen, cock.
  - **Longarm:** one heavy shot, three damage, a head shot kills; a little wander from the hip, dead true through its brass telescope (hold right mouse, LT, or Scope on a phone). Reloading: tear and pour a paper cartridge, ram it twice, return the rod, thumb on a fresh cap, cock.
  - **Harpoon gun:** a barbed harpoon on a line that flies fast and flat (100 m/s, dropping about 10 cm over 30 m) but still slow enough to see, catches anyone it passes within a hand's breadth of (0.2 m past the body), and kills what it hits; it stays stuck where it lands for a while. Reloads in 2.2 s. Reloading: crank the line back onto the drum, slide a new harpoon down the barrel, cock.
  - **Cutlass:** no powder; swing within arm's reach for two damage a cut, in a forehand, backhand, thrust combination with a pale trail through the air. R turns it to the light and wipes the blade.
  - **Avtomat** (6): an automatic rifle. Twenty-five rounds a magazine at about 700 a minute for as long as you hold the trigger, one damage a hit (two to the head). It climbs hard and jumps sideways with every round, sprays from the hip, and settles back only part of the way when you let go; the reload is long (3.9 s): tip it, drop the empty magazine, rock a full one in and slap it home, rack the handle. Hold right mouse, LT or Aim on a phone to look down its iron sights: the view comes 1.45× closer, the crosshair gives way to the notch and the hooded front post, every round goes where the post sits, and the climb is about a third less.
  - **Aiming down the sights:** every gun aims, not just these two (the blades don't). Hold right mouse, LT or Aim on a phone and the gun comes up to the middle of the view with a small dot where it will hit, the view comes a little closer (1.15× for the blunderbuss, punt gun and the like, up to 1.35× for the harpoon gun) and the look slows to match. A shot of balls spreads just as wide. It works the same in Wreckers' Night, Keeper's Last Light, the raids and Fog Run, for every gun the Lighthouse's chests and bench give you too. Toggle aim in Settings works for all of them.
  Everyone sees what everyone else is carrying, and every shot (balls, harpoons and cuts included) is judged fairly by whoever it was aimed at.
- **Names and health:** anyone you can see well (players and wreckers alike) has their name over their head and under it a small health bar, a segment for each of the four hits they can take, green, amber when hurt, red on the last, shrinking with distance.
- **Cover:** the skerries and old wrecks stop shots. Head shots do double damage. Four hits and you're down; you're back ashore 3.5 seconds later, somewhere away from everyone else, and patch yourself up after a few quiet seconds.
- **The drowned crew** wade ashore now and then and go for whoever is nearest.
- **Rounds** last five minutes on the clock, so everyone's round ends together; the scores reset and the island moves to the next of the three maps (the Bonded Quay, Pilchard Row, the Flensing Yard, in turn) and the next of the six seas.
- **Wreckers (bots)** fill the island up to six people (five bots when you play alone) (about half in outfits from the Tailor's Crate, and always one in an Admiral's), and step aside as players come ashore. They and the drowned crew are run by one player (whoever has the lowest id on the island) and shown the same to everyone; if that player leaves, the next one carries them on where they were.
- **Fair hits:** a shot travels as an event (where it was fired from and which way). Whoever it was aimed at checks it against where they really were over the last third of a second, rocks included, so ducking into cover in time always counts. The host judges shots at the wreckers and the drowned the same way.
- **Controls:** mouse and keyboard (click to aim, WASD, hold Shift to run (11 m/s against a 6.5 m/s walk; the view widens a touch as you go); Settings has a Field of view slider (60° to 110° across the screen, 69° by default; also in the in-game menu) and Toggle aim (off by default: right mouse or LT aims while held; on, one click aims and the next lowers the sights, as does breaking into a run), click to fire, Space jump, C crouch, R reload, V cutlass, T view, Tab scores, Esc menu), a controller (sticks, RT fire, LT aim, A jump, B crouch, X reload, Y next weapon, R3 cutlass, LB bell, RB flare, D-pad down lantern and up view; click the left stick to sprint until you stop; in the Lighthouse X also uses what's in front of you, and holding it boards up a window or gets someone up; the full table is under **Shooter controls**). The menu (Start) has a Controller page with the controller's own look sensitivity and its aiming sensitivity (how much of that is left while aiming down the sights or the telescope; both remembered) and the button map, and the whole menu works from the controller (D-pad or left stick to move, left/right to slide, A to choose, B back), or touch: put your left thumb down anywhere on the left to walk (a stick appears under it; push to the edge to run), drag on the right to aim and tap there to fire, or hold the big Fire button and drag it to aim and shoot with one thumb. **Unstuck** (in the menu, in every game you play on foot: Wreckers' Night, the Lighthouse, the raids, Fog Run and Freebooters' Sea): caught on a rock, a wall's corner or a rail, it steps you to a clear spot picked at random within a metre of where you stand (a little further, up to three metres, if nothing within one is clear). It only picks a spot you could have walked to, never through a wall, a shut door or out of a window, and it won't work while you're down or at the wheel. Once every 5 seconds; in co-op the others see you move as usual. Come down from a jump onto a cart, a pot or a rail and you slide off its edge rather than stick in it, and the maps' edges (a jetty's or pier's side, a quay's low wall, the Lighthouse's gallery parapet, a battery's parapet, a stair hole's rail) stop you even mid-jump, so there's no getting out of bounds or round a shut door. Aiming eases onto anyone you can see near the crosshair. The phone layout keeps the screen clear: menu and scores top left, a small compass and clock, your score and the last two lines of the feed top right, and the lantern, flare, bell and reload buttons in an arc round Fire, which shows your ammo.

It uses the same connection as crews (the claude.ai room, or the crew server), on its own key in presence, so the two never mix; the server relays island players to each other at full rate.

### The islands

Every round is the next of three maps, in turn: **the Bonded Quay**, **Pilchard Row** and **the Flensing Yard**, then round again (shifted by one every six rounds, so each map comes round in every sea), as well as being in one of the six seas. Everything on it is laid out from the round number (`src/fpsisle.js`), so every player, alone or online, builds the same map and nothing extra is sent over the network. The six open islands below are no longer in the rotation; they're kept for testing (`?isle=shingle` and so on).

- **Pilchard Row** (built for close fights, laid out like a little street): two fishermen's cottages face each other across a lane, each with a front door off the street, a back door to its garden, two rooms, and windows you can shoot through over the sill. A lugger lies beached on its side across the middle of the lane, the cover everyone fights over, with a handcart, barrels and pots about it and a lamp at each end of the row. Behind the houses are gardens inside low drystone walls, with fences down the sides and a shed in each, and beside the south cottage stands an open timber net loft. Low walls close each end of the row, with a gap for the road out.
- **The Bonded Quay** (small, walled and quick): a customs yard shut in by the high backs of the warehouses, packed with a grid of long cargo stacks under tarpaulins, some two high to go round and some one high to shoot over, turned against each other so the lanes between are short and the corners blind. The middle is cleared round a crane on a stone plinth with a load hanging from its jib, low bales to duck behind, a customs hut in one corner and a lamp on each wall. No drowned come up here: there's no shore to wade up.
- **The Flensing Yard** (mid-sized, with a tower to fight over): a derelict whaling station's yard on a spit of sand, shut in by the stone backs of the boiling sheds, a timber palisade down two sides and a fence along the shore side that you can shoot through. In the middle stands the old **lookout**, a timber tower you can climb: walk up its stair to a planked platform four and a half metres up, with a boarded rail round it that covers your body but not your head, open only at the top of the stair. Walk off the edge and you drop to the sand; you can walk underneath it too. Round it are two rusting oil tanks with pipes on trestles, low runs to crouch behind and shoot over, and high runs over the lanes to the tower that you walk under, the long timber flensing shed (doors at both ends and both sides), the stone boiler house, the try-works (a brick furnace with two great iron pots, its fire-holes still glowing), a whaleboat on blocks, a handcart, barrels and crates, a whale's jawbone arch inside the gate and a lamp on each side. The wreckers keep to the ground. No drowned come up here either.
- **More cover everywhere:** the other six islands each get two or three more buildings, cottages, huts and sheds with doorways and windows, some of them roofless ruins.
- **The Shingle Skerry:** grey shingle and tide pools, boats drawn up and turned over, lobster pots, fish drying on a rack, driftwood, cairns, a half-fallen sheepfold, and a bonfire still glowing. Gulls overhead.
- **Wreckers' Reef:** dark wet rock and weed, five broken hulks, timbers, spilled crates and barrels, old cannon, an anchor, a fallen mast, a salvage fire and a lantern on a spar.
- **Chapel Islet:** turf, a roofless chapel with a tall gable and candles on the altar, a graveyard inside a low broken wall, headstones and a cross, a well with a lantern, a ring of standing stones, dead trees. Bats round the lights.
- **Smugglers' Cove:** sand, a jetty out into the sea with a boat moored at the end, a boathouse open to the water, stacks of crates and barrels, lantern posts, nets drying.
- **Signal Head:** heather, crags along one shore with a stair cut up them, a ruined round signal tower with its beacon lit, drystone field walls with gaps, standing stones, wind-bent thorns, a sheep fence.
- **The Salt Marsh:** mud, pools and reed beds, boardwalks, a fowler's hut, eel traps, peat stacks, fences and posts, a punt, and pale marsh lights over the water. Thick fog.

Walls, crates, barrels, boats, headstones, stones and the rest are real cover: they block you, stop shots and hide anyone behind them, like the rocks. Low walls stop a shot at the body but not one at the head. Fences stop you but not shots, and a roof stops shots but not you. Lanterns, fires and marsh lights light the ground round them, and anyone standing near one. After laying an island out, the game checks that you can walk to every part of it from the middle, and thins the props out if less than 60% of it is walkable; respawns and the wreckers only go where you can walk. Wreckers turn aside to get round things in their way.

In 3D the ground has gentle rises and hollows (a quarter of a metre at most), patches of colour, worn tracks with footprints, a wet band and foam at the shore, grass, reeds, stones and seaweed, and each kind of island has its own sky tint and fog. The props are merged into a few meshes, so the island costs only a dozen or so draw calls. The Classic renderer draws each prop as a flat-shaded block, with the pools, boardwalks, lights and birds.

### Weapon crates

**Outfit crates** (`src/ocrates.js`), also on the Wreckers' Night card, open the **Tailor's Crate**: outfits for your wrecker, the coat, its trim, the sash, the buttons, and the hat and its style (a tricorn, a bicorne, a sou'wester, a knitted watch cap, or a hood). Everyone on the island sees you in yours (it goes out with your figure). Without one you wear your boat skin's colours, as before. One crate for every 6 kills and one for each round seen out ashore; the first is free. Kept in this browser (`skerry-ocrates`).

**Everyone in 3D.** With the 3D renderer, the other players and the wreckers are the same detailed figures as your own (`src/fpsbody.js`): coat and skirt, lapels and buttons, waistcoat, sash and belt, bandolier and pouches, breeches and sea boots, a face and a queue, their hat, their weapon (in its plain finish) in their hands and their lantern, with a stride as they walk, leaning into a run, their aim followed by head and gun, and a flash when they fire. Walls and rocks hide them properly, being in the scene; the dark hides them exactly as before, and their names and lantern glow are still painted over the top. The nearest ten are shown as figures. The Classic renderer keeps the flat figures.

Wreckers' Night has its own crates, the **Gunsmith's Crate**, opened from **Weapon crates** on its title card. They hold skins for the six weapons (pepperbox, blunderbuss, longarm, harpoon gun, cutlass and avtomat): ten each, 3 Deckhand, 2 Bosun, 2 Mate, 2 Captain and 1 Admiral, plus a plain finish everyone owns. They use the same grades, odds, reel, Open all and duplicate counts as the Skerry Crate, and gold in the reel is always your prize. Captain and Admiral finishes carry a glowing accent. The six Admiral finishes are alive: each wears a moving, glowing finish of its own on the 3D gun, in your hands and on your figure over the shoulder. **The Skerry Light** has the Light's beam sweeping along it from breech to muzzle, lighting up gold filigree as it passes; **Mouth of Charybdis** a whirlpool of violet and gold twisting down the barrel into the bell; **Plimsoll's Eye** blue sea-glass with light through moving water running over it, a glint travelling the barrel and an eye in the telescope's eyepiece that looks about and blinks; **Golden Leviathan** molten gold scales with glowing seams and a ripple of light running through them; **The Last Lamp** a lamp flame flickering up through the blade, with embers rising off it; **The Ninth Wave** foam-crested seas rolling along it with spray flying, and every ninth sea bigger and whiter, with gold in its spray. Their crate pictures move too (a sweeping beam, a turning whirlpool, rippling water and a blinking eye, a molten ripple over scales, firelight and rising embers, rolling waves and spray). Reforged at the Lighthouse's bench, the galaxy takes over, and the Admiral finish comes back when it's gone. It's one shader (`src/wcadmiral.js`) on the gun's own materials; with reduce motion it holds still. You earn one crate for every 5 kills (the count carries over between rounds and visits) and one for each round you see out on the island after at least a minute ashore; your first one is free. Weapon crates are entirely separate from the boat crates: their own save in the browser (`skerry-wcrates`), their own count, and their own save code (`WCRATES-...`) on the card, so nothing here touches your boats, voyages or crew. `src/wcrates.js` gives the gun renderer `wcEquippedSkin(weaponId)` (colours: metal, dark, wood, brass, accent, glow), and fps.js calls `wcEarn('kill' | 'round')`.

## Boats

Choose your boat with the Brig / Cutter / Barque / Carrier picker on the title, harbour, end and crew screens, or from the shipyard at the top of the **Crates** screen. Your choice is kept on this device, and your crew sees which boat you're sailing.

| Boat | Hull | Acceleration | Steering | |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Brig | 4 | 1.3× | 1.2× | the all-rounder, two masts, a carpenter who patches a plank every 40 seconds, and floating crates reload her flare and bell |
| Cutter | 2 | 1.7× | 1.4× | small and quick, one tall mast |
| Barque | 5 | 0.8× | 0.9× | long and heavy, three masts, and a bow gun |
| Carrier | 6 | 0.6× | 0.7× | a modern supercarrier, 40 m long; 8% faster flat out, floodlights, and a scout jet |

Each boat has its own deck and bow as you see them from the helm, and its own length and rig as your crew sees it.

The Barque's **bow gun** fires by itself when you're about to run onto a rock, blowing it apart before you hit. It only fires at rocks close ahead (about a second and a half of sailing, at most 45 m) and takes 3 seconds to reload after each shot; the *Bow gun* marker under your hull fills up as it reloads. In a crew, a rock it destroys is gone for everyone.

The **Carrier** is a modern nuclear supercarrier in the style of the newest carrier class: a flat flight deck with an angled landing deck to port, the island aft on the starboard side with its mast and turning radar, aircraft parked on the bow, and a row of lit hangar openings. From her deck you look out over the non-skid, the landing-area markings and the catapult tracks to a broad flat bow. She is the toughest boat (six planks) and a touch faster flat out, and her **floodlights** light the water 1.6 times as far as a lantern. Her **scout jet** (J, the *Launch jet* button, or B on a controller) is thrown off the bow by the catapult and flies a loop about 330 m out along your course and back, its searchlight lighting every rock under it; then it is ready again 25 seconds after launch (about 17 seconds after it lands). The trade: she is huge, so there is far more of her to put on a rock, she gathers way slowly and she turns like a carrier. The jet can't fly in the Backrooms.

## Crates, skins and pennants

Every voyage you play earns a **Skerry Crate** if you go down, and if you make harbour, **5** on voyage 1, then the Fibonacci sequence for each voyage in a row (8, 13, 21, 34, 55... up to 4,181 from voyage 15 on); a sinking starts the streak over at voyage 1. About a dozen floating crates (look for their lanterns, pillars of golden light, and the gold diamonds on the bearing strip) are scattered around each voyage: sail into one to haul it aboard. Make harbour and each one you're carrying is worth **two** more crates; sink and they go down with the ship. Any crate can hold any item:

- **Boat skins** for all four boats (44 Brig, 22 Cutter, 22 Barque, 16 Carrier, plus each boat's plain finish). The Carrier's run from Fleet Grey and Arctic White through Dazzle Stripes, Night Ops and Neon Deck to Stealth, Ghost Fleet, and the Admiral of the Fleet and Aurora Flagship. Thirty of them come from the far seas: fjord ice, basalt and ember, lagoon turquoise, barrow moss, crystal, temple marble, the Places In Between, and the Nether and Wine-Dark Sea monsters, from Lava-Forged and Golden Fleece (Admiral) down. Each boat keeps its own equipped skin. A skin changes your deck, rails, bowsprit and lantern as you see them, and your hull and sails as your crewmates see them; crewmates' ships are lit by their own lanterns, so you can make out their skins from about 300 m away.
- **Animated flair**: every Mate, Captain and Admiral skin moves. Mates glow, Captains have paint flowing along the hull and sails, Admirals sparkle with a sweeping gold sheen, and some have their own: Northern Lights cycles colour, the Ghost Ship and Flying Dutchman flicker with green wisps, Lightning Bolt crackles, Spice Route and Sunset Regatta throw off embers, Ice Barque glints with frost, Abyssal Black has a dark aura, Maren's Lantern burns warm, and Witch Light has orbs circling the masts. It shows on your ship in third person, on your deck in first person, on crewmates' ships, and in the crate previews.
- **Admiral skins** go further (`src/admiralfx.js`): all of them breathe a bright glow, sweep a sheen of light along the hull and throw sparkles round the rig, and each has a signature look of its own. Plimsoll Blue: an electric-blue waterline of light rippling along the hull. Skerry Gold: a lighthouse lantern at the masthead, its twin beams turning. Abyssal Black: a black hole between the masts, ringed in cyan, with an accretion disc circling the ship and sparks spiralling in. The Gannet, Restored: white sails shining and seabird feathers drifting down. Lava-Forged: molten cracks pulsing in the hull and fire dripping into the sea. Silver Gull: moonlit silver shimmer over the sails and gulls wheeling the masthead. The Sleeper's Eye: a glowing eye on the mainsail that opens, looks about and blinks, with lilac dream-motes. Royal Barque: purple and gold banners of light streaming from the masts and a crown over the stern. Golden Fleece: gold wool curling in the sails and gold dust rising. Admiral of the Fleet: gold braid lights chasing round the flight deck and a starburst over the island. Aurora Flagship: aurora curtains rippling in the sky over her. On your deck in first person each has its own version (the beam sweeping overhead, the eye in the sky ahead, cracks of fire across the planks, and so on), their crate pictures are animated, and their crate cards glow and breathe in the skin's colour. Far-off ships show only the shared glow, to keep a busy sea smooth; with reduced motion they hold still.
- **Flags**: 20 pennants, which fly from a staff at the tip of your bowsprit (and from your masthead for your crew) on any boat. The two Admiral pennants are alive: Plimsoll's Laces glows ice-blue, a white shimmer runs down the fly, its checks twinkle and it trails frost sparks; Verity's Grin glows hot yellow with a gold shimmer and sparks, and the grin in its black square lights up and flickers like a sign on the blink.
- **Flare colours**: 12 of them, from Magnesium White and Green Star to Pier Lamps (red and green), Gold Rain (sparks) and Aurora (the Admiral flare: curtains of green, blue and violet light hung beneath the star and rippling, a long trail of colour down its climb, and a spray of twinkling sparks). Your crew sees your flares in your colour.

Your first crate is free. The Crates screen shows how many you have, with an Open button, and tabs for your boat skins, flags and flares. A reel spins and lands on your prize. With two or more crates, **Open all** opens the lot at once (same odds) and shows the haul: how many of each grade, your best pull, and every new item. Every crate uses the same five grades: **Deckhand** (sky blue) 79.9%, **Bosun** (violet) 16%, **Mate** (pink) 3.2%, **Captain** (red) 0.64% and **Admiral** (gold) 0.26%. Crates saved by older versions become Skerry Crates.

**Cloud saves** (`src/cloudsave.js`, `server/saves.js`). On the game's own site, every browser gets a **recovery code** (SKR-XXXX-XXXX-XXXX-XXXX) the first time it plays, shown on the Crates screen under Keep your loot with a Copy button. Everything you own (boat skins, flags, flares, weapon skins, outfits, banners, golf balls and clubs, pirate tokens, and your crate counts) is saved to the server under that code a few seconds after anything changes, and checked for news from your other devices every three minutes and whenever the game comes back into view. To get it all on another phone or browser, paste the code into the same box (Load) or open a link ending `#code=SKR-...`: the two saves are merged (nothing either side has is ever lost) and from then on both devices share one save; if both change at once, the server notices and they're merged again. The code is the only key: whoever has it has the save. The server keeps saves in the same free Upstash Redis database as the scoreboard (the same two environment variables; see the scoreboard's setup above), storing each under the SHA-256 of its code, at most 48 KB, let go after 400 days untouched. Without Upstash it keeps them in memory, and the box says they'll be lost when the server restarts. Not on claude.ai, where your save is kept in your claude.ai account instead.

**Keeping your loot.** Your crates and cosmetics are saved in your browser. Links opened inside other apps (Discord, Snapchat, Instagram, Messages), private windows and cleared site data start with an empty save, so there's a one-tap **Back up save** button on the menus (a red dot means you have loot that isn't backed up). Where the browser has a share sheet (most phones, and some desktop browsers) it opens that so you can send the save to yourself; elsewhere it copies a **save link**. Opening that link on any phone or browser loads everything. The Crates screen also has the save as a plain code, and as a file you can download and load back. The save link, code, file and claude.ai account save all carry everything: boat skins, flags, flares, weapon skins, outfits, banners, golf balls and clubs, and pirate tokens. Loading a code never takes anything away; you keep every item from both. On claude.ai, your save is also kept in your claude.ai account automatically.

Gold (Admiral) items only ever appear in the reel when one is your prize, so if you see gold scroll past, it's yours. The chance of gold is still 0.26%.

Crates and loot are kept in your browser on that device.

## Sailing together

Up to eight people can sail as a crew. Each of you sails your own brig through the same skerries, and you can see each other's ships and lanterns, hulls and bells.

- On the title screen, one person picks **Start a crew** and taps **Share invite** on the crew screen; opening the link puts a friend straight into that crew (see Invite links, below). Others can also pick a crew from the list, or use **Find me a crew** to join any crew with a free berth.
- The crew screen has a **chat** for planning between voyages, and messages also appear in the in-game log.
- The host (whoever started the crew) sets sail when everyone is in. Anyone who joins later sails straight in on the current voyage.
- **Ships within 55 m of each other keep Maren where she is**, the same way Plimsoll does.
- **The race to harbour**: the first ship into harbour on a crew voyage wins a bonus of 3 Skerry Crates for every other ship in the crew (+3 with two ships, +21 with a full crew of eight), on top of the usual harbour crates. "First" means the fastest voyage time, timed from your own launch, so a late joiner isn't penalised; a dead heat goes to the ship with fewer hits. The crew screen tells you while you wait whether you got in first, and the scoreboard marks the winner with a ★ when the voyage ends.
- **Sailing in company**: stay within 45 m of a crewmate for 10 seconds and your carpenters patch a plank.
- **A bell rung within 160 m of a crewmate** drives her back two stages on their ship too.
- **Flares** light the water for everyone.
- **Spectating**: once you sink, are taken or make harbour, you can watch a crewmate's voyage from their deck until the voyage ends, and once per voyage you can **ring a bell for them**, driving their Maren back a stage.
- **Hails**: keys 1 to 4 (the hail buttons on a phone, or LB and the D-pad on a controller) call *Here!*, *Help!*, *Follow me* or *Maren close*. Your crewmates see it over your ship and hear your horn.
- **Scoreboard**: at the end of each voyage the crew screen shows each ship's outcome, time, hits, bells, flares, planks patched, drowned fed to Plimsoll and close shaves.
- **Reconnecting**: if a connection drops, the game reconnects by itself and carries on with the same crew.
- A voyage ends when every ship has made harbour or gone down. If anyone made it, the crew goes on to the next voyage. If nobody did, the host can start again from voyage 1.

Crews connect in one of two ways:

- **On your own website**, through the crew server in `server/`. Anyone with the link can join, with no sign-in. Players type a name on the title screen so their crew knows who is who.
- **On claude.ai**, through the artifact `room` capability, for people who are signed in and can see the artifact. Visitors from a public link get the solo game there.

If neither is available (for example, the file opened straight from your computer), the game plays solo.

## Invite links

Every mode with a lobby has a **Share invite** button: the crew screen (Skerry Light), the war lobby (Drowned Reach), the night lobby (Keeper's Last Light) and the raid lobby (Maren's End). Wreckers' Night has one on its card, since everyone there shares one island.

- On a phone or tablet it opens the share sheet; on a computer it copies the invite to the clipboard. Either way it reads "Join my game on Skerry Light!" followed by the link.
- Opening the link takes your friend straight into your lobby, on the right game's card. If the lobby has closed or the game has already started, they're told so and can host their own. The link is used once: it's cleared from the address bar, so a reload or a reopened tab starts on the main menu. If they pick another game before the lobby turns up, the invite steps aside and leaves them on the game they chose. A Wreckers' Night link opens that card, ready to tap **Go ashore**.
- On claude.ai the link is the game's page itself, as everyone there plays in the page's own room: your friend opens it and taps your lobby in the list.
- Links look like `https://skerry-light.onrender.com/?mode=raid&lobby=z1ab2cd` (modes: sail, wreckers, war, lighthouse, raid, fogrun, fbsea, golf, rival, league, chess, campaign). Old crew links ending in `#gannet-12` still work.

## Rejoining a game in progress

Drop out of a game you're playing with others (you reload the page, the browser crashes, your phone sleeps or loses signal) and you can get back into the same game as the same player. This works in every mode you can play together: crews in Skerry Light, Wreckers' Night, Drowned Reach, Keeper's Last Light, both raids (Maren's End and The Gannet's Wake), Fog Run and Freebooters' Sea.

- **The Rejoin bar.** Open the game again, in the same browser, within fifteen minutes. While the game you left is still going, a bar at the top of the main menu says so; tap **Rejoin** to go straight back in, or **Not now** to forget it. It works after a reload or in a new tab, as long as the old tab is closed (a tab that's still open keeps its own place).
- **From the lobby or a link.** The lobby list marks a game you dropped out of "under way · tap to rejoin", and your old invite link takes you back in too.
- **What comes back.**
  - Keeper's Last Light and the raids: your points (and anything the host credited you while you were gone), your weapons and ammunition, your tonics, reforged weapons, your calling and your ultimate's charge, your MVP numbers, where you stood (if it's still the same part of the raid), your health, and whether you were down or out until the next round.
  - Drowned Reach: your nation, as it is now. If you were gone long enough for a bot to take it over, it's handed back to you, and the host sends you the whole map again.
  - Wreckers' Night: within the same round, your deaths and who took them (which is where everyone's kills are counted from), your weapon, where you stood, your health and your flares. Kills already paid out in crate keys aren't paid again.
  - Freebooters' Sea: the session (the host keeps the ship, the hold and the purse), what was in your arms, and your wait to be back on your feet if you'd bled out. What you'd banked isn't counted again.
  - Skerry Light crews: your old place in the crew (a host is still the host) and, if the voyage is still being sailed, back out on it where your ship was, with its hull and the voyage's tally.
- **Everyone else** sees "<name> is back." in the feed or the war's log.
- **The host.** In Keeper's Last Light, the raids and Drowned Reach the host's browser runs the game, so if the host drops out the game ends for everyone, as before. Wreckers' Night and crews have no such host, so anyone can come back, the crew's host included.

## Putting it on a website

### Game and crews together (recommended)

`server/server.js` serves the game and runs the crew connection from the same address, so one deployment gives you a link anyone can open and play together. It uses only Node's standard library (Node 18 or newer), so there is nothing to `npm install`.

Try it on your own computer first:

```
npm run build
npm start
# open http://localhost:8080 in two or three browser windows
```

To put it online, use any host that runs a Node web service and allows WebSockets, such as Render, Railway or Fly.io. On Render, for example:

1. Put this folder in a GitHub repository.
2. In Render, create a **Web Service** from that repository.
3. Set the **Build Command** to `npm run build` and the **Start Command** to `npm start`.
4. Deploy, then share the address Render gives you.

The server reads the port from the `PORT` environment variable, which these hosts set for you. `MAX_PLAYERS` (default 300) caps how many people can be connected at once, and `MAX_PER_ADDR` (default 12) how many connections one address may hold. The rate limits and that cap read the visitor's address from `True-Client-IP` (set by Cloudflare in front of Render), not from `X-Forwarded-For`, which a visitor can fill in themselves. Nothing is stored on disk; restarting the server just empties the lobby. `/healthz` reports how many players are connected.

### Game on a static host, crews on a separate server

If you'd rather put the game on itch.io, Netlify or GitHub Pages, run the server somewhere as above and point the game at it:

1. In `src/config.js`, set `SERVER` to your server's WebSocket address, for example `'wss://skerry-light.onrender.com/ws'`.
2. Run `npm run build`.
3. Upload `dist/skerry-light.html`, renamed to `index.html`. On itch.io, zip it first and upload it as an HTML game.

You can also point any copy at a server without rebuilding by adding `?server=wss://your-server/ws` to the page address. A page served over `https` must use a `wss://` address.

### Solo only

Upload `dist/skerry-light.html` anywhere that serves static files. Everything it needs is inside that one file; the crew section on the title screen will say it is solo only.

### Working on the source

`index.html` loads the files in `src/` separately, so you can edit them and reload. Serve the folder (for example `python3 -m http.server 8000`) and open it in a browser; crews won't connect there, since that server has no `/ws`. After changing anything in `src/` or `assets/`, run `npm run build` to update `dist/`.

### Version number

The version shown at the top of the main menu is `VERSION` at the top of `src/config.js`. Every change pushed to `main` bumps it by one: pull the latest `main` first, add one to `VERSION`, then build, so two changes never ship the same number. It started at 97, the count of commits on `main` when it was added.

### Patch notes

Every change pushed to `main` also adds an entry for its new version at the top of `PATCH_NOTES` in `src/patchnotes.js` (the file's top comment shows the shape: `v`, `date`, `title` and any of `New`, `Balance`, `Changes`, `Fixes`, `Sound`). `src/patchnotesui.js` draws the page. Write them like real patch notes, from the player's side, and leave secrets out entirely: no Easter eggs, secret bosses, secret rewards or hidden options, not even a hint.

### Lore compendium

Every new character, place, boss or story beat gets a page (or a line) in `src/lore.js`, linked with `[[id]]` or `[[id|words]]` from the pages it touches, so the compendium keeps up with the game. Leave secrets out of it, as from the patch notes.
