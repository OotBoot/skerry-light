    Skerry Light

    In the winter of 1887 the lamp at Skerry Light failed, and the keeper's wife, Maren Holm, rowed out with a lantern to warn the brig Gannet. The Gannet broke up on the rocks. Maren's skiff came back empty. Since then the sea has not been one sea.

      The story, and how to play

      She still stands on the water when the lamp turns, nearer every turn, and she follows any ship she has chosen. The Gannet's drowned crew swim with her, and her figurehead, Knock, walks the water with a belaying pin. The only thing Maren will not come near is Plimsoll, the shark who walked out of the surf that winter in the Gannet cabin boy's blue shoes. He knows the deep water through the skerries, but he won't wait for you. Follow his shoes.

      • See rocks, wrecks and drifting timber show up when the beam or a flare is on them, then fade away over 3 seconds once the beam moves on
      • Follow Plimsoll's glowing shoes. Keep up with him (within about 120 m at a cruise, further the faster you go) and Maren holds where she is: his ring on the bearing strip fills while he's keeping her off. Fall behind and he soon walks on alone
      • Ring the bell (Space) with her anywhere ahead of you (anywhere at all once she's close) to drive her back three stages. It also sinks the drowned and freezes Knock. It swings again in about 15 seconds
      • The drowned swim after you; their eyes catch the light. Sail fast to shake them off, or ring the bell. Plimsoll sometimes snaps one up
      • Knock moves only while you can't see him. Listen for three knocks on wood, turn, and keep him in your light. The bell freezes him
      • Flare (F) lights everything around you for a few seconds, for your whole crew
      • Steer ← → or A / D, sail ↑ ↓ or W / S. On a phone, hold the left or right side to steer and use the ▲ ▼ buttons for speed
      • Pause Esc or P (or Start on a controller) pauses a voyage on your own; in a crew it opens the settings, and your crew sails on. Settings (title screen or pause): volume, softened jumpscares, fewer flashes, less motion
      • Boats the Brig takes four hits, her carpenter patches a plank every 40 seconds, and each floating crate she picks up reloads her flare and bell; the quick Cutter takes two, the heavy Barque five. The Barque's bow gun fires by itself to blow apart a rock just ahead that you're about to hit, then takes 3 seconds to reload. The Carrier takes six, her floodlights reach far, and she launches a scout jet (J) that lights the rocks along your course, but she is huge, slow and slow to turn. Glancing off a rock only slows you; hitting it head on costs a plank.
      • Hull when the planks run out she goes down. Let Maren reach the bow and she comes aboard
      • Tonight's enemies each voyage brings three enemies from a pool of 21 (the log names them as you set out), on top of Maren. Your very first voyage brings just one gentle one, and the second two. Each has its own trick and its own counter: read the log when one turns up, and watch for the call-out in the middle of the screen when something needs an answer now
      • Verity sometimes you'll find a small smiling yellow orb in a corner of your deck, offering to help. He isn't helping, and the bell doesn't bother him. Fire a flare before twenty seconds are up, or see what he really looks like. He only comes aboard when your flare is nearly loaded, and his countdown waits (the ring turns grey) until it is. A crewmate's flare close by sends him off too
      • Six seas the first voyage is always Skerry Light. After that the voyages go round six seas in turn: Skerry Light, the Frozen Fjord, the Ember Isles, the Coral Lagoon, the Barrow Shore and the Crystal Shoals. Each has its own sky, coast, weather and rocks (icebergs, lava-veined basalt, coral reefs, gravestones, crystal). They look different, but they sink you just the same. And about one voyage in twenty (never the first), a portal stands in the water ahead: you sail through it into the Nether, a lava sea under a roof of red rock with lava pouring down from it. The Nether has its own three enemies and nothing else, not even Maren: the Cinder Kite (dodge the orange rings its fireballs fall into; a flare drives it off), the Slag Wader (ring the bell before it reaches your bow) and the Furnace Moth (if it lands on your lantern, fire a flare and it chases that instead). Another one voyage in twenty you find yourself on the Wine-Dark Sea of the old stories, with white temples on the headlands, a golden moon and shooting stars, and three monsters of its own (again, nothing else): the Sirens (their song drags your helm to their rock; ring the bell to drown them out), Charybdis (a whirlpool that opens on your course and drags you in; sail hard round it) and the Cyclops (dodge the pale ring where his rock will land; a flare in his eye blinds him). And one more in twenty takes you to the Places In Between: a glassy sea stuck at dusk, with doors and staircases standing in the water. Its own three, and nothing else: the Doors (dark ones send you back, the lit one sends you on), the Echo (a pale copy of your ship that steers the mirror of you; turn late and hard, or ring the bell to shatter it) and the Sleeper (a face under the water; when the water over it brightens, ring the bell before its eyes open). One more in twenty you're not sailing at all but flying a plane over the clouds: ↑ climbs, ↓ dives, steer round the storm clouds at every height, and come down low between the runway lamps to land
      • The Backrooms about one voyage in twenty, the lights go out and the sea comes back with walls: a flooded maze of yellow rooms. Follow Plimsoll to the green EXIT sign. The Stowaway lives there too: when he screams and charges down the corridor, dodge to the side
      • Floating crates bob about the water, each with a little lantern. Sail into one to haul it aboard. Make harbour and each is worth two Skerry Crates; sink and they go down with you. Making harbour pays 5 crates on voyage 1, then follows the Fibonacci sequence for each voyage in a row: 8, 13, 21, 34...
      • Crew sail with up to seven friends. Ships close together keep her back and patch each other's planks. Share flares, chat in the crew screen, and watch your crew after your own voyage ends. Keys 1 to 4 (or the hail buttons on a phone) call Here!, Help!, Follow me and Maren close to your crew. The first ship into harbour wins 3 bonus crates for every other ship in the crew
      • Close shaves slip past a rock at speed (over 15 knots) with under 2.5 m to spare and it counts as a close shave on your end card

      Wreckers' Night

      Leave the ship. Go ashore on the skerry with a pepperbox pistol and a lantern, and shoot it out with whoever else is on the island tonight. Nothing here touches your voyages, crates or crew.

      On the island · up to 8 players

      Looking for other players…

      How to play

      Light is everything. The lighthouse beam sweeps the island and shows anyone it crosses. Your lantern lets you see, and lets everyone see you. Flares light a wide circle for all. Ring the bell and everyone shows on your bearing strip, and you on theirs. The drowned crew wade ashore now and then. Keep out of trouble for a few seconds and you patch yourself up. Rounds last five minutes and the island moves to a new sea each round. Verity comes ashore too: if a small yellow thing sits down in front of you smiling, fire a flare before he stops. Bullets and the bell don’t bother him, and you can’t walk away from him. Wreckers (bots) fill the island up to four.

      The avtomat fires as long as you hold the trigger, twenty-five rounds a magazine, but it climbs and kicks with every round, sprays from the hip, and takes a long time to reload. Aim down its iron sights (right mouse, LT, or Aim on a phone) to bring the view closer and put every round where the front post sits; it still climbs, so pull down as you fire, and let go now and then.

      Keyboard and mouse: click to aim · WASD walk · Shift run · Space jump · V over-the-shoulder view · click fire · right mouse: telescope (longarm) or iron sights (avtomat) · 1-6, Q or the wheel: weapons · R reload · L lantern · F flare · B bell · Tab scores · Esc menu.

      Controller: sticks walk and aim · A jump · R3 (click the right stick) view · RT fire · LT telescope or sights · RB or d-pad weapons · X reload · Y lantern · B flare · LB bell · Back scores · Start menu.

      Touch: put your left thumb down anywhere on the left to walk (push to the edge to run); drag on the right to aim, tap there to fire; or hold Fire and drag it to aim and shoot with one thumb. The arrow by Fire jumps; the camera button at the top switches between first person and over the shoulder. Aiming eases onto anyone you can see near the crosshair.

      A third game · one against many · a war for the islands

      The Drowned Reach

      Land on an archipelago of skerries and take it. Your people grow with the land you hold; send a share of them against the empty shore, then against your neighbours, tile by tile. Boats carry landings to other islands. Gold raises cities, factories and their railroads, harbours, watchtowers and beacons, and buys flares and fire-ships. Sixteen nations (or up to fifty) want the same islands, the tide opens and shuts the shoal roads between them, and at night the drowned come up on any shore the Light doesn’t reach.

      Your war

        How to play

        Land. When the map opens, tap open land on a big island to mark your landing (or let the clock run and be put somewhere). Your people grow on their own; the more land you hold the faster, up to a limit the land sets. Gold comes in with land, harbours and whole islands.

        Fight. The slider sets what share of your people an order sends. Tap empty land, drowned land or an enemy’s land that touches yours to march on it; the force spreads along the border and takes tiles until it is spent. Hills, watchtowers and a ringing bell make tiles dearer to take. Tap a shore across the water and a boat carries the force there instead (a landing needs at least 25). Enemy watchtowers fire on passing boats.

        Build. City: room for 250K more people (people are counted in thousands), a little faster growth, and gold. Factory: lays railroads to every city of yours within 16 tiles and joins them; cities on a railroad pay double gold. Harbour: gold, light on the shore, and fire-ships. Watchtower: tiles near it cost attackers far more, and it fires on boats. Beacon: light that keeps the drowned off the shore round it. Flare: bursts over a spot, lights it for a while and clears the drowned there. Fire-ship: sails from a harbour and burns everything round where it lands, friend or foe. Bell: your people hold for a while, dearly.

        The Reach. The Light’s beam sweeps the map; the nation holding the Light’s rock is the Keeper, and gets gold and safe shores. At night the drowned rise on dark shores and take the land into the dark; by day they sink back and leave it empty. The tide turns every ninety seconds: at low water the shoals dry into land bridges, at high water they drown and anyone on them loses them. Hold a whole great isle for a bonus (✦). Pacts last four minutes; break one and every nation remembers.

        Together. Host a war and the others here can join it, up to ten players, with none to fifty bot nations. The host starts it whenever they like. Everyone chooses a landing and taps Ready; you all land when everyone is ready or the clock runs out. Players can swear pacts with each other as with the bots (each offer waits half a minute for an answer). The war runs at one speed and doesn’t pause, and it ends for everyone when one nation holds most of the Reach or stands alone. If a player leaves, their nation fights on by itself; if the host leaves, the war ends.

        Keys: drag or arrows to pan · wheel or +/- to zoom · Q/E the share · C city · V factory · H harbour · T tower · G beacon · F flare · X fire-ship · B bell · Space pause · 1/2/3 speed · Enter land now · Tab the leaderboard on a phone · Esc menu. Touch: drag to pan, pinch to zoom, tap to order.

                Boats and Crates

                Pick your boat. Each voyage earns Skerry Crates: one if you go down, and more for every harbour you make in a row (5, 8, 13, 21...), plus two for each floating crate you bring in. Any crate can hold a skin for any boat, a flag or a flare colour. If you see gold in the reel, it's yours.

                    Wreckers' Night · the tailor

                    Outfit Crates

                    Outfits for your wrecker: the coat, its trim, the sash, the buttons and the hat, a tricorn, a bicorne, a sou'wester, a watch cap or a hood. Everyone on the island sees you in it. Mate outfits shimmer, Captain outfits glow and their eyes shine, and the Admirals' are alight from collar to hem, with a halo. You earn a Tailor's Crate for every 6 kills and one for each round you see out ashore. Same grades and odds as the other crates: if you see gold in the reel, it's yours.

                      The Drowned Reach · the herald

                      Banner Crates

                      Banners for your land: the colours your nation's land shows on the map, to you and to everyone you play with. The better the grade, the more it does: Mate banners move, Captain banners move and glow along your borders, and the Admiral's banner has the Light's own beam sweeping over it. You earn a Herald's Crate for every 400 tiles you take, one for each war you see through, and three for a win. Same grades and odds as the other crates: if you see gold in the reel, it's yours.
